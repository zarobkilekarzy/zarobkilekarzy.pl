Doctors with diplomas from outside the European Union, mainly from Ukraine and Belarus,[^rz-lew] work in Poland on three different legal bases. According to Statistics Poland (GUS), in 2024, 2,586 doctors with Ukrainian citizenship worked with patients, or 1.8% of all doctors in direct patient care.[^gus] Some of them went the full way: recognition of the diploma, internship and the final examination. Some work on the basis of a decision of the minister of health, which since 2020 has made it possible to skip recognition of the diploma. We describe how these routes work, how many doctors use them and what changed with the Polish language requirement, because of which the medical chambers declared several hundred licences to practise (PWZ) expired in 2026.

## Three routes into the profession

The standard route is the same for all doctors with a non-EU diploma and leads to a full licence to practise. The other two were added in 2020, during the epidemic. Both begin with a decision of the minister of health, on the basis of which the regional medical council grants a licence to practise — limited in time.[^ustawa]

The Medical Verification Examination (Lekarski Egzamin Weryfikacyjny, LEW) as an alternative to recognition of the diploma (nostryfikacja) was introduced by the act of 16 July 2020, with effect from 1 January 2021.[^dz1291] The conditional licence to practise (warunkowe prawo wykonywania zawodu) was added by the amendment of 14 August 2020, in force from 1 September 2020,[^dz1493] and the minister’s consent for a defined scope of activities (zgoda ministra na określony zakres czynności) by the act of 28 October 2020, published on 28 November and in force from the following day.[^dz2112] In 2022 the Act on assistance to citizens of Ukraine opened a separate route to a conditional licence for doctors from Ukraine.[^specustawa]

Where both simplified procedures sit among the other barriers to entering the profession is described in our text [Restricted access to the profession](/en/mechanisms/restricted-access-to-the-profession/), and where the staff shortages that these procedures were meant to fill come from — in the analysis [Why Poland is short of doctors](/en/analysis/why-poland-is-short-of-doctors/).

## The standard route: nostrification or the LEW

Since 1 January 2021 nostrification of the diploma has not been necessary — instead, a candidate can pass the Medical Verification Examination (LEW).[^nik] Nostrification nevertheless still exists, and more diplomas are recognised this way: according to data that Rynek Zdrowia collected from universities, in 2023 diplomas were recognised through nostrification for 557 people, while 93 passed the LEW.[^rz-nostryf] Universities differ greatly in the number of applications. The Medical University of Białystok recognised 1,269 diplomas out of 2,318 applications in 2020–2024, while the medical faculty of the university in Lublin did not receive a single application in 2020–2022.[^rz-nostryf]

After recognition of the diploma or a passed LEW, the doctor completes a postgraduate internship (staż podyplomowy) and takes the Medical Final Examination (LEK). For a doctor who worked in Poland under a simplified procedure, the minister may recognise that time as equivalent to the internship.[^ustawa]

The LEW is prepared by the Medical Examinations Centre (CEM). The test has 200 single-choice questions;[^rz-lew] a pass requires at least 60% of the points, the examination is held only in Polish, and each registration costs <Money value={700} />.[^cem-lew] CEM publishes the questions with the correct answers five years after the examination — sessions from 2021 onwards are available.[^cem-testy]

Only a small share of those who sit it pass. From 2021 to spring 2026, citizens of Belarus sat the LEW 1,925 times and 280 people passed; citizens of Ukraine 1,928 times, with 165 passes; Poles with a non-EU diploma 279 times, with 26 passes.[^rz-lew] In total in these three groups that is 471 passes out of 4,132 attempts, or about 11.4%. This is our own calculation, excluding individual candidates of other nationalities, whom the source does not add up.

In the session of 26 May 2026, 28 of 92 citizens of Belarus, 12 of 89 citizens of Ukraine and 2 of 28 Poles with a non-EU diploma passed. In the lead of the same article Rzeczpospolita states that this year 8.8% of Ukrainians and 31.5% of Belarusians passed; the figures in the article give 13.5% and 30.4%. The newspaper does not explain this difference, which is why we give the numbers of people.[^rz-lew]

Graduates of Polish universities pass the Medical Final Examination at the first attempt in 95–97% of cases.[^cem-lek] The comparison has limits: the LEK is taken by graduates right after their studies in Poland, and the LEW by doctors trained in another system who write the test in Polish. In addition, the LEW statistics count all attempts, including repeat ones.

## The conditional licence to practise

The minister may grant consent for a conditional licence only during a state of epidemic threat or an epidemic. The requirements include a diploma from at least five years of medical studies outside the EU, full legal capacity, a state of health that allows the doctor to work and an impeccable ethical stance; the act does not require legalisation of the diploma or an apostille, and knowledge of Polish is not among the conditions. A doctor without a specialist title works under supervision. On the basis of the minister’s decision the regional council grants the licence within 30 days. It is valid for at most five years and cannot be extended.[^ustawa]

For citizens of Ukraine, a similar consent was provided for in Article 61 of the act of 12 March 2022.[^specustawa] The act of 15 May 2024 extended the period in which it could be obtained to 32 months from 24 February 2022 — so applications were accepted until 24 October 2024.[^dz854] Since 5 March 2026 Article 61 has no longer been in force, and doctors who used it are covered by the provisions on the conditional licence in the Act on the professions of doctor and dentist.[^ukr]

After five years the conditional licence expires. The Ministry of Health replies to doctors who ask for it to be extended that there is no legal basis for this: anyone who wants to keep working must have the diploma nostrified or pass the LEW.[^rz-maj] Some doctors are already taking this route. The director of the Department of Medical Workforce Development at the Ministry of Health said in June 2026 that the ministry issues many decisions recognising work in Poland as equivalent to the postgraduate internship.[^rz-800]

## The minister’s consent for a scope of activities

The second simplified procedure is intended for specialists. It requires at least three years of work as a specialist in the given field within the last five years, a certificate from the hospital that wants to employ the doctor, and legalised diplomas — the minister may waive this condition when meeting it is impossible or considerably hindered. The minister may seek the opinion of a national or regional consultant. The licence covers the specified scope of activities, period and place of work, lasts at most five years without extension, and for the first year the doctor works under the supervision of a mentor.[^ustawa]

This procedure is still in operation, although fewer and fewer people use it. According to the explanatory memorandum to a draft act of June 2026, in 2025 the minister issued about 100 consents under it, and since 2020 more than 4,500 non-EU doctors in total have obtained rights under the simplified procedures.[^ud384]

## How many doctors work under the simplified procedures

The state of the register is given by the Supreme Medical Chamber (NIL) in half-yearly summaries. On 30 June 2026, 1,867 non-EU doctors held a conditional licence to practise, and 619 a licence for a defined scope of activities. A year and a half earlier the figures were 2,607 and 756.[^nil2026][^nil2024] Over the same period the number of licences to practise of foreigners outside the simplified procedures (excluding EU citizens) rose from 1,332 to 2,815. The summary does not say how many of them are doctors who previously worked under a simplified procedure.

## Polish language: the B1 requirement and expiries

Until 24 October 2024, for consent for a scope of activities a declaration of knowledge of Polish was sufficient, and for the conditional licence the act did not require knowledge of the language at all.[^dil][^ustawa] The act of 15 May 2024 changed this in two ways. Anyone who applied for the minister’s consent later receives a licence to practise from the chamber only after presenting a document confirming Polish at least at B1 level. Anyone who had consent from an earlier application — under the conditional procedure, for a scope of activities or under the special act on Ukraine — had to present such a document by 1 May 2026 or lose the licence.[^dz854]

According to Infor.pl, the obligation covered 2,321 doctors and 1,024 dentists.[^infor] Before the deadline the Ministry of Health estimated that as many as about 2,000 doctors would not meet it, and the medical self-government about a thousand.[^rz-300]

After 1 May the regional chambers began declaring licences to practise expired. In the first half of May the president of the Supreme Medical Council spoke of 146 people.[^rz-nrl] By 12 May there were 207 (Rynek Zdrowia, citing Rzeczpospolita, gave 208),[^rz-maj][^rz-208] at the beginning of June about 300,[^rz-300] and according to NIL data as of 11 June — 441.[^rz-weto] This is one growing series, not different measures. NIL estimates that in the end about 800 doctors will lose their licence.[^rz-800] We did not find more recent data on expiries.

### Where the most licences expired

In mid-May Rzeczpospolita gave a breakdown by voivodeship (province): the most in Masovian (mazowieckie, 107), followed by Warmian-Masurian (26), Podlaskie (19), Lublin (16), Silesian (15), Lower Silesian (8), Greater Poland (5) and Lesser Poland (4).[^rz-maj] These eight voivodeships add up to 200, although the newspaper gives a total of 207 — the source does not assign seven of the expiries.

A month later the picture was different. According to NIL data as of 11 June, the most licences were declared expired by the Lower Silesian Medical Chamber (129), followed by the chambers in Warsaw (99), Warmia and Masuria (52) and Greater Poland (42).[^rz-weto] Some chambers, including the Greater Poland chamber, initially held back from declaring expiries.[^rz-300] The breakdown therefore largely reflects the pace of proceedings in individual chambers, not only where these doctors worked. The data from May (voivodeships) and from June (chambers) are not directly comparable.

The director of the workforce department at the Ministry of Health judged that the loss could be a problem for individual hospitals with “just enough” staff, but not for the system as a whole. Rzeczpospolita also established that over four years the Patient Ombudsman received seven complaints about poor knowledge of Polish among foreign doctors.[^rz-maj]

## The veto and the legal position in autumn 2026

At the end of April 2026, just before the deadline, an amendment extending the deadline for the certificate by a year, to 1 May 2027, was added to an amendment of the Act on publicly funded healthcare services — which mainly concerned the funding of HIV treatment and hepatitis C diagnostics. The Sejm passed the act on 15 May and the Senate on 22 May 2026.[^rz-weto] The Ministry of Health explained that the Polish language requirement remains, and that the longer deadline was due to the limited availability of examinations.[^xyz] On 11 June 2026 the President of Poland vetoed the act, citing patient safety.[^rz-weto]

On 10 August 2026 the ministry sent the draft again — to the Standing Committee of the Council of Ministers, in its original version, without the provision on the deadline for doctors.[^rz-sierpien] We found no information about the veto being overturned. The deadline of 1 May 2026 therefore still applies, and the chambers may continue to declare licences expired.

### Draft: an end to consents for a scope of activities

The ministry also wants to close the second simplified procedure. Under a draft amendment to the Act on the professions of doctor and dentist (in the government’s list of legislative work: UD384), applications for consent under Article 7(2a)–(2b) will be accepted until 31 December 2026, and the minister will leave later ones unexamined. The explanatory memorandum states that the number of doctors in Poland is growing and that fewer and fewer consents are being issued. The draft also gives the chambers more tools to oversee doctors working under the simplified procedure, for example the possibility of asking the employer for a professional opinion. The Council of Ministers is to adopt it in the fourth quarter of 2026.[^ud384] It is still a draft and does not change the law in force.

## Verification of diplomas and disputes with the chambers

Most of the allegations concern how the documents were checked. The registration committee of the Lower Silesian Medical Chamber wrote that for consent to be issued “a photocopy of the diploma or a photograph of the document is enough” (our translation), and described a person with a ministerial decision who, according to the chamber’s information, had not completed medical studies.[^dil] This is the chamber’s position, not a finding of an authority or a court. The act gives the minister a tool: in the case of justified doubts about the authenticity of documents, the minister may ask the Polish consul in the country where they were issued to verify them.[^ustawa] We found no data on how often the minister uses this.

The medical self-government also gives its own figures. In June 2024 the NIL spokesperson said that the ministry had admitted to work about 400 people whom the regional chambers had refused a licence to practise because they did not know Polish.[^rz-400] In February 2025 the TVN programme “Uwaga!” described a person who had obtained a conditional licence on the basis of a diploma issued to someone else. The NIL spokesperson wrote that this person had never completed medical school and had issued about 9,000 prescriptions; the prosecutor’s office took up the case.[^gl] Both claims come from NIL, not from a court judgment. Nor was the case the reason intake was closed: applications stopped being accepted on 24 October 2024, four months before the broadcast, under the act of May 2024.[^dz854]

When the chambers refused a licence despite the minister’s decision, doctors challenged their resolutions. According to the NIL spokesperson, more than 300 resolutions of the Supreme Medical Council went to court. Rynek Zdrowia analysed 24 judgments of the Voivodeship Administrative Court in Warsaw (WSA) from 2022–2025 — all in favour of the doctors. The court pointed out that the provision on the conditional licence deliberately omits the requirement of knowledge of Polish, and that the chamber acts solely on the basis of the minister’s decision.[^rz-sady] The judgments are not final: the self-government appealed to the Supreme Administrative Court (NSA), and according to NIL, in April 2026 none of these cases had been decided.[^abc]
