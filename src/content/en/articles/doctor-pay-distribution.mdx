The fullest picture of how much doctors earn in Polish hospitals comes from a compilation by the Agency for Health Technology Assessment and Tariff System (AOTMiT) of February 2026. The agency collected pay data from 538 hospitals, about 96% of the facilities in the so-called hospital network, and presented them in May at a meeting of the Tripartite Team for Health Care (<span lang="pl">Trójstronny Zespół do spraw Ochrony Zdrowia</span>).[^rp-etat][^pap] It is a working document of the agency; its figures are known from articles in Rzeczpospolita and from the Polish Press Agency (PAP).[^rp-aotmit] We have gathered them here into a single distribution: from the statutory minimum for salaried doctors to payments of over PLN 100,000 a month.

<Aside variant="warn" title="None of these amounts is a doctor’s total income">

AOTMiT collects data on individual contracts within one facility, so a doctor working in three hospitals appears in its data as three separate entries.[^wywiad][^rz-milion] For such a person, each figure below describes only part of their earnings. That is why [we call](/en/demand/) for the total paid to each person from public money to be public — under a permanent identifier, without names.

</Aside>

## Medians by form of employment

The median is the middle amount: half of the payments are higher, half lower. AOTMiT gave it separately for doctors with and without a specialisation and for three forms of employment: an employment contract, a contract, and a contract of mandate or for specific work.[^rp-aotmit]

At the median, the form of employment makes a moderate difference to pay: a specialist under a contract receives about {KONTRAKT_PREMIUM}% more than as an employee. For doctors without a specialisation the order is reversed — the employment contract has the highest median. Contracts of mandate and for specific work come out lowest.

About 73% of specialists (exactly {formatEN(SHARES.civil, 2)}%) are paid under civil-law contracts, and about {SHARES.etat}% are salaried.[^rp-aotmit] Where this predominance comes from and what follows from it is described in [Contract or employment](/en/mechanisms/contract-vs-employment/).

So the medians of contracts are known: AOTMiT collected them from hospitals. Two other things remain unknown — the amounts of individual contracts, because nobody publishes them, and the total paid to one person by many facilities, because nobody calculates it.

### How widely the amounts are spread

The median does not show the spread. From the same compilation we also know that:

- the largest group of specialists’ contracts falls in the range {BRACKETS.kontrakt.label} a month — {formatEN(BRACKETS.kontrakt.share, 1)}% of contracts ({formatEN(BRACKETS.kontrakt.n)}).[^rp-aotmit] The median is more than PLN 10,000 above this range, so contract amounts are widely spread;
- among salaried specialists the largest group earns {BRACKETS.etat.label}: {formatEN(BRACKETS.etat.n)} {BRACKETS.etat.n === 1 ? 'person' : 'people'}, or {formatEN(BRACKETS.etat.share, 1)}%;[^rp-aotmit]
- the average pay of a salaried specialist, including on-call duty and seniority allowances, is <Money value={ETAT_SPEC.average} decimals={2} />. The lowest-paid quarter receives on average <Money value={ETAT_SPEC.bottomQuartileAvg} decimals={2} />, and the best-paid quarter — over <Money value={ETAT_SPEC.topQuartileFrom} decimals={2} />.[^rp-etat]

## The floor: the statutory minimum for employees

For salaried doctors, base pay cannot fall below the minimum set by the act of 8 June 2017. From {FLOOR_FROM} it is {pln(SPECIALIST_FLOOR.monthly, { decimals: 2 })} for a specialist, {pln(NON_SPECIALIST_FLOOR.monthly, { decimals: 2 })} for a doctor without a specialisation and {pln(INTERN_FLOOR.monthly, { decimals: 2 })} for an intern.[^siatka] These amounts exclude on-call duty and allowances. The minimums for other medical professions are shown in [Other medical professions](/en/data/other-health-professions-pay/).

The hourly rate at the minimum salary depends on the number of working days in a month. The daily working-time standard in healthcare facilities is 7 hours 35 minutes, so a month with 22 working days has {formatEN(MONTH_HOURS, 1)} hours. On-call duty (<span lang="pl">dyżur medyczny</span>) for employees is paid like overtime: with a 50% supplement on weekdays and 100% at night, on Sundays and public holidays.[^udl][^wds]

Resident doctors, that is, doctors in specialist training under a residency, earned on average <Money value={RESIDENT_AVERAGE} decimals={2} /> in February 2026.[^rp-etat] This is an average, not a median, which is why we do not put it on the chart next to the medians. The source also does not say whether the amount includes on-call duty. For comparison: the median for all salaried doctors without a specialisation is <Money value={MEDIANS.bezEtat} />.[^rp-aotmit] We list resident doctors’ pay in individual specialties in [By specialty](/en/data/residents-pay-by-specialty/).

### Contracts: a market rate, not a statutory one

Contract rates are neither set by any act nor collected by any register. According to the portal Wsparcie dla Szpitala, hospitals pay specialists under contract PLN {B2B.mid.from}–{B2B.mid.to} an hour in medium-sized towns and in fields without shortages, PLN {B2B.big.from}–{B2B.big.to} in large cities, and up to PLN {B2B.max} in hospital emergency departments (SOR) and intensive care.[^wds] That is from about {formatEN(B2B_RATIO.from, 1)} to {formatEN(B2B_RATIO.to, 1)} times the minimum hourly pay of a salaried specialist.

The comparison is not like for like, however. From the amount on the invoice the doctor pays social contributions themselves and gets neither paid leave nor sick pay from the hospital. These are also market ranges, not measured payments.

## Over PLN 100,000 a month: contracts, not doctors

The threshold of PLN 100,000 gross a month is nearly four times the median contract of a specialist. How many payments exceed it depends on what is counted: individual contracts, people in one facility, or people after adding up all their workplaces.

The AOTMiT compilation counts contracts. In it, {formatEN(OVER_100K.contracts)} specialists’ contracts were worth over PLN 100,000 gross a month, or {formatEN(OVER_100K.contractsShare, 1)}% of all of them, and {OVER_100K.etat} salaried specialists earned that much ({formatEN(OVER_100K.etatShare, 1)}%).[^rp-aotmit][^pap] The president of the agency said that about {OVER_100K.persons} people exceed this threshold in one facility, just over 1% of doctors; in another statement the figure given was 1.5%.[^wywiad][^rz-milion] A doctor with two contracts of PLN 60,000 each in two hospitals receives PLN 120,000 a month, but in such a compilation does not exceed the threshold even once. The compilation gives no upper limit; the highest known amounts from individual hospitals are collected in [Record payments](/en/data/record-doctor-payments/).

After adding up all workplaces, the number of people above the threshold can only rise, because the sum of several payments is not smaller than any one of them. The president of AOTMiT estimates that if the payments to the same person from different facilities could be combined, 5–10% of doctors might have over PLN 100,000 a month.[^wywiad] Rynek Zdrowia converted this estimate assuming {formatEN(SUMMED.activeDoctors)} professionally active doctors: the result is between {formatEN(SUMMED.persons.from)} and {formatEN(SUMMED.persons.to)} people.[^rz-milion] This is an estimate, not the result of a calculation: no method was presented, and nobody publishes the data with which it could be checked. The only certainty is that for anyone who works in more than one place, the amounts on this page are a lower bound.

### Full payroll lists: entries above one million

The second measurement is the annual payroll lists that facilities send in the monitoring “<span lang="pl">{WATCHDOG_DATA.source.monitoring}</span>” (“{MONITORING_EN}”) of the Citizens Network Watchdog Poland (Sieć Obywatelska Watchdog Polska).[^watchdog] So far, full lists for {WATCHDOG_DATA.year} have come from {formatEN(WATCHDOG_LISTS)} of the {formatEN(WATCHDOG_DATA.requests)} facilities asked. They cover {entries(WATCHDOG_POSITIONS)}, including {formatEN(WATCHDOG_OVER_1M)} above PLN 1 million gross a year, or on average more than <Money value={83000} /> a month from one facility. Such amounts appear on the lists of {formatEN(WATCHDOG_OVER_1M_FACILITIES)} of the {formatEN(WATCHDOG_LISTS)} facilities.

These figures cannot be converted into doctors either. The facilities that sent lists are not a random sample, and a payroll entry is a position on one facility’s list, not always one person. For a doctor who also works elsewhere, the amount from one list is only part of their earnings. The full distribution and the method are described in [Doctors’ pay from disclosed payroll lists](/en/data/disclosed-hospital-pay-lists/).

### Contracts in the Central Register of Contracts

The third source is the Central Register of Contracts of Public Finance Sector Entities (<span lang="pl">Centralny Rejestr Umów</span>). Since 1 July 2026 it has included contracts concluded by, among others, independent public healthcare facilities (SPZOZ); hospitals operating as companies do not belong to the sector.[^ufp] At the end of August the register contained {CRU.over1m} {CRU.over1m === 1 ? 'contract' : 'contracts'} for the provision of healthcare services worth over PLN 1 million, but some of them cover several years: {CRU.threeYearOver3m} three-year contracts were worth over PLN 1 million a year, that is, over PLN 3 million over three years.[^rz-milion] Some of these contracts were concluded by hospitals with individual medical practices, that is, with doctors running their own business. The contracts for the highest amounts, however, were signed with companies, mainly for laboratory tests and reporting on imaging examinations — this is payment to a firm, not the pay of one person.[^rz-milion]

Nor is the amount in a contract a payment. The director of one hospital explained that contracts are signed “with headroom”, with a margin for extra hours, and the doctor receives as much as they work.[^rz-milion] The register therefore shows the value of a single contract, not a payment or a person’s earnings, and, like the other sources, it does not add up the contracts of one doctor. What the register covers and what it does not is described in [Lack of transparency](/en/mechanisms/lack-of-transparency/).

## One person, several unconnected payroll entries

The measurements described above have one feature in common: each shows a payment from one facility. The president of AOTMiT admitted that the agency has no data linking the pay of the same person from different entities.[^wywiad] Nor can it yet convert a contract into a full-time equivalent.[^pap]

The model below shows how this looks from the perspective of one doctor. It is an illustrative example, not a description of a specific person, and each of these arrangements is legal in its own right.

1. **A contract in a hospital.** At a rate of PLN {B2B.mid.from}–{B2B.max} an hour, every 100 hours a month gives PLN {formatEN(PER_100H.from)}–{formatEN(PER_100H.to)}. Wsparcie dla Szpitala describes an anonymous example of an anaesthetist under contract in a voivodeship (regional) hospital: {INVOICE_EXAMPLE.hours} hours a month, including {INVOICE_EXAMPLE.dutyHours} on call, and an invoice for <Money value={INVOICE_EXAMPLE.amount} /> gross.[^wds]
2. **On-call duty or standby in a second hospital.** The same doctor can be on call elsewhere — as an employee or under another contract. Employees are also paid for standby “on the phone”: each hour of it is paid at half the hourly base rate, without being present in the hospital.[^udl]
3. **An outpatient clinic or a primary care (POZ) practice.** On top of that there may be visits in a specialist outpatient clinic with a contract with the National Health Fund (NFZ), and for a family doctor with their own practice — the capitation rate: from July 2026, {pln(RATE.rate, { decimals: 2 })} a year for each patient on the list, multiplied by an age-dependent coefficient.[^poz] This is the practice’s revenue, from which it pays for tests, among other things, not the doctor’s pay.

Each of these payments goes to a different facility or to the NFZ. None of them needs to exceed PLN 100,000 for them to exceed that threshold together. And if none of them exceeds it, in the AOTMiT compilation this person is not among the payments above PLN 100,000. How work in several places adds up is described in our text on [working for multiple employers](/en/mechanisms/multiple-jobs-parallel-contracts/), and the per-patient rate in the analysis [Money per patient in primary care](/en/analysis/primary-care-capitation/).

Since 5 August 2026 the minister of health may request from facilities pay data linked to the PESEL number and the licence to practise (PWZ) number — in other words, data that can be summed for one person. The act does not, however, provide for their publication.[^ustawa-972] We describe the details in our analysis [The pay data act](/en/analysis/pay-data-act-vs-public-register/).
