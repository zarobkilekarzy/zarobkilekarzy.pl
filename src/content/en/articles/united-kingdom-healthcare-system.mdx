The British National Health Service (NHS) is financed from taxes and free at the point of treatment. It is also one of the systems that publish the most about themselves: every month it reports the length of the waiting lists at each hospital, and GP practices must disclose the average earnings of their doctors. We describe how it works — mainly in England, because Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own health services — and what is public in it. The picture that emerges from the public waiting-list data is not a flattering one: the statutory treatment deadline has not been met for years.

## One payer: the state budget

The NHS has no sickness funds and no health contribution. The money comes from the budget, and its allocation is decided by the Department of Health and Social Care. The department’s day-to-day budget is <Money value={202000000000} currency="GBP" scale="billion" /> in 2025/26 and <Money value={211000000000} currency="GBP" scale="billion" /> in 2026/27; the spending plan to 2028/29 assumes real-terms growth of 2.8% a year.[^sr]

Treatment is commissioned by local integrated care boards, which were responsible for <Money value={171000000000} currency="GBP" scale="billion" decimals={0} /> of spending in 2025/26. The British statistical office, which calculates expenditure by the same method as Eurostat, reports that in 2025 out-of-pocket payments by patients accounted for 14.1% of health spending, and public money for 81.3%.[^ons]

The United Kingdom spends markedly more on health than Poland and has fewer doctors per inhabitant. Even so, deaths that treatment could have prevented are more than one third fewer there.

## What the patient pays

A visit to a GP (family doctor), a visit to a specialist and a stay in hospital are free of charge. In England there is a charge for prescription medicines: <Money value={9.9} currency="GBP" /> per item, regardless of the price of the medicine. The charge has been frozen for the second year in a row, and about 89% of items are dispensed free of charge because the patient is exempt.[^recepty]

A referral, usually from a GP, is needed to see a specialist and for planned hospital treatment; the patient then has the right to choose the hospital.[^wybor]

## The GP: an independent contractor

GP practices are not part of the NHS but its contractors: they are run by doctors who hold a contract with the NHS. A doctor can be a party to such a contract — a partner in the practice — or its employee.[^reg27]

Every year the NHS statistics body calculates GPs’ income on the basis of tax returns. In 2024/25 a GP partner in England had an average income of <Money value={164200} currency="GBP" />, and a salaried GP <Money value={74800} currency="GBP" />.[^gp-earnings] This is income before tax, after deducting the expenses of the practice, from public and private sources combined.

## Hospital doctors: a national pay scale

Doctors in hospitals are employees of public bodies (NHS trusts) and are paid according to national scales. From 1 April 2026 the basic pay of a doctor in the first year after graduation is <Money value={41226} currency="GBP" /> a year, and that of a consultant — the equivalent of a Polish specialist — from <Money value={113565} currency="GBP" /> to <Money value={150569} currency="GBP" />.[^skale] The scale does not include on-call duty or supplements: according to NHS statistics, a consultant earned on average <Money value={148648} currency="GBP" /> a year in total, of which <Money value={120769} currency="GBP" /> was basic pay.[^zarobki-nhs]

The best consultants can receive a national award worth from <Money value={21000} currency="GBP" /> to <Money value={42000} currency="GBP" /> a year; 539 new awards were granted in 2025.[^nagrody] The level of pay rises is recommended every year by an independent pay review body; for 2026/27 the government accepted 3.5%.[^ddrb]

On 29 June 2026 resident doctors who are members of the union voted to accept the government’s offer: resident doctors’ pay rises by an average of 4.9% in 2026/27, and the government committed itself to creating additional training places.[^spor]

According to OECD estimates, a salaried specialist in the United Kingdom earned 3.34 times as much as the average worker (2020), and a self-employed GP 3.68 times (2023).[^oecd-remun]

## Doctors’ strikes

The dispute over the pay of doctors in training (*resident doctors*, until 2024 *junior doctors*) lasted more than three years in England. The doctors’ union, the BMA, demanded that the real value of pay from 2008/09 be restored; according to its calculations, based on a broader price index, pay had lost 21% by 2025/26.[^ddrb-raport] [^fullfact] This is the position of one party to the dispute — with a different measure of inflation the fall comes out several times smaller.[^fullfact]

The first wave of strikes ended with the agreement of September 2024, which doctors accepted in a ballot with 66% of the vote.[^porozumienie-2024] According to the government, strikes in healthcare cost almost <Money value={1700000000} currency="GBP" scale="billion" /> between April 2023 and September 2024 and meant more than 1.5 million cancelled appointments.[^porozumienie-2024]

In 2025 the dispute returned. The government awarded resident doctors a pay rise averaging 5.4%,[^podwyzka-2025] the union considered it insufficient and between July 2025 and April 2026 organised four strikes — 21 days in total. The government estimated their cost at about <Money value={50000000} currency="GBP" scale="million" decimals={0} /> a day.[^porozumienie-2026] In 2025/26 NHS England set aside <Money value={750000000} currency="GBP" scale="million" decimals={0} /> to cover the additional costs of industrial action.[^nhs-raport] In April 2026 the government told the House of Lords that since 2023 there had been 65 days of strikes by resident doctors, and that their total cost was about <Money value={3000000000} currency="GBP" scale="billion" decimals={0} />.[^hl16543]

The dispute ended on 29 June 2026. Doctors accepted the government’s offer by a narrow majority: 17,410 voted in favour (52.9%) and 15,522 against (47.1%), on a turnout of 57.3%.[^glosowanie-2026] The offer included a pay rise averaging 4.9% in 2026/27.[^wdrozenie-2026]

The law also changed during the dispute. The 2023 act that made it possible to require a minimum level of service during a strike was repealed on 18 December 2025.[^msl]

## What is public

**Waiting lists — every month, for every hospital.** NHS England publishes the number of people waiting for planned treatment, the waiting time and the share within the 18-week limit. In September 2026 there were 7.4 million referrals waiting, concerning about 6.2 million people; 65.8% were within the limit against a standard of 92%, and 116,353 referrals had been waiting more than a year. Half of the patients had been waiting longer than 12.2 weeks.[^rtt] The government promises a return to 92% before the end of the parliament.[^sr]

**NHS payments to every practice.** Every year a file is published with the amounts the NHS paid to each GP practice in England — with the name and address of the practice and a breakdown by component. For 2024/25 it covers about 6,400 practices and <Money value={11380000000} currency="GBP" scale="billion" decimals={2} />, that is, on average <Money value={178.88} currency="GBP" /> per registered patient.[^platnosci-gp] This is the practice’s money, not that of a particular doctor.

**Average earnings of the doctors in every practice.** The regulations on GP contracts require a practice to publish on its website every year the average earnings of its doctors after deducting expenses, and the number of doctors to whom that average relates.[^reg27] This is an average for the practice, not the amount of a particular person.

**Earnings of the highest-paid GPs — in the regulations.** Since 2021 the regulations have provided that a GP whose NHS earnings exceed a threshold — <Money value={163000} currency="GBP" /> for the year ending in March 2024 — declares them by name for publication. The provision sets thresholds for only three years, up to March 2024.[^reg27a] According to the doctors’ union, the obligation was postponed twice — in autumn 2021 and in spring 2022 — and many practices were never formally bound by it.[^bma-deklaracje] We found no published list.

**Pay of hospital directors.** An act requires the annual report of an NHS foundation trust to contain information on the remuneration of its board members.[^sch7] We found no earnings of individual doctors outside the board in the reports — what is public are the scales and the averages.

**GPs’ income by form of work.** An annual study based on tax data gives the averages separately for partners and salaried GPs, for each of the four countries of the United Kingdom.[^gp-earnings]

**Industry payments — mostly by name, but voluntarily.** The pharmaceutical industry’s database showed <Money value={929600000} currency="GBP" scale="million" /> transferred to healthcare for 2025. According to the industry’s estimate, 93.6% of the healthcare professionals who received payments other than for research and development were disclosed by name. In December 2025 the government decided that guidance was sufficient for now and did not introduce a statutory obligation.[^disclosure]

**The medical register and hearings.** The medical register is public and shows the restrictions imposed on a doctor, and the tribunal that rules on the right to practise publishes its decisions ([more in the text on professional liability](/en/analysis/doctor-discipline-licence-revocation/)).

## Satisfaction

In the annual British Social Attitudes survey, 26% of respondents were satisfied with the NHS in 2025 and 51% were dissatisfied. A year earlier 21% were satisfied — the lowest figure in the history of the survey.[^bsa] In international surveys the United Kingdom nevertheless does better than Poland ([about these surveys](/en/analysis/satisfaction-with-healthcare-in-poland/)).

## Professional and criminal liability

**Professional.** Doctors are registered and supervised by the General Medical Council (GMC), which is not a professional self-governing body but an independent regulator. The right to practise is decided by a tribunal that is separate from it. In 2025 the tribunal erased 48 doctors from the register, and 67 a year earlier.[^gmc] The Council can appeal against a tribunal decision that it considers too lenient. Two independent reviews recommended taking that right away from it; in 2026, however, the government proposed keeping it and, after objections from the doctors’ union, agreed to a further consultation.[^odwolanie]

**Criminal.** English law has no separate offence of medical error. A doctor can be liable for gross negligence manslaughter. The prosecutor must show that the doctor breached a duty of care, that the breach gave rise to a serious and obvious risk of death, that it caused the death and that the conduct was “truly exceptionally bad” — bad enough to deserve criminal punishment and not just compensation.[^williams]

The scale was examined by a review commissioned by the government in 2018. Since 1994, 47 healthcare workers had been charged with this offence, including 37 doctors; 23 were convicted, and four convictions were quashed on appeal. Since 2013 the prosecution service had dealt with 151 cases of suspected gross negligence manslaughter: seven ended in a charge, four of them in a conviction.[^williams] We found no more recent data.

Since 2015 it has also been an offence for a person who cares for a patient professionally to wilfully neglect or ill-treat them — punishable by up to five years’ imprisonment — and the provider can also be liable if grossly poor organisation was to blame.[^cjca] Providers are in addition under a legal duty of candour: after an event that the regulations require to be reported as a notifiable safety incident, they must inform the patient without delay, explain what is known and apologise.[^candour]

**Compensation.** England has no system of payments without establishing fault. Claims against hospitals are handled by a public body, NHS Resolution. In 2025/26 it paid out <Money value={3200000000} currency="GBP" scale="billion" /> for clinical negligence, of which 40% related to maternity care, and on 31 March 2026 it had set aside <Money value={60260000000} currency="GBP" scale="billion" decimals={2} /> for future liabilities across all its schemes, of which <Money value={60000000000} currency="GBP" scale="billion" /> was for clinical negligence. 84% of claims were resolved without court proceedings.[^resolution] What the alternative looks like is described in the text on [compensation without establishing fault](/en/mechanisms/no-fault-compensation-and-doctors-liability/).
