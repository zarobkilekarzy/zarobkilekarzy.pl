Germany spends 12.21% of GDP on health — against 7.84% in Poland and 10.16% on average in the EU — and has a system in which the state sets the framework while the money is decided on by the parties themselves: the funds, the hospitals and the doctors’ self-governing body. We describe how this works according to German acts and official data, and check what is public. The pay scales of hospital doctors, the fee schedule and the pay of every board member of a fund are public. How much an individual doctor earns — in a hospital or in their own practice — is not public, and there is no federal-level summary of how many doctors have had their licence to practise revoked.

## 93 funds and a separate private system

In statutory health insurance (GKV), which covers 89.0% of inhabitants,[^profil] there were 93 funds at the end of 2025; on average over the year they insured 74.5 million people — 58.7 million members and 15.8 million co-insured family members.[^kasy] [^km1] Anyone who earns above the threshold — €77,400 a year in 2026 — can leave the statutory system and take out private insurance.[^prog] In 2025, 8.79 million people had full private insurance.[^pkv]

Contributions go to a common health fund, which the federal budget tops up — by €15.9 billion in 2025.[^kv45] What the funds finance, and on what terms, is decided not by the ministry but by the Federal Joint Committee, made up of representatives of the funds, fund-contracted doctors and hospitals, and three impartial members; its resolutions are binding on all parties.[^gba]

## What it costs

<CountryBars title="Current health expenditure in 2024 (Eurostat)" codes={['DE', 'PL', 'EU']} keys={['healthGdp', 'perCapitaEur', 'perCapitaPps', 'publicShare', 'oopShare']} />

The statutory funds spent €352.4 billion in 2025. Most went on hospitals (€111.4 billion), medicines (€58.5 billion) and outpatient doctors (€53.9 billion). The funds closed 2025 — after increases in the additional contribution — with a surplus of €3.5 billion. Their reserves remain below the statutory minimum.[^kv45] [^kasy]

In July 2026 the act on stabilising the contribution entered into force. From 1 January 2027 it raises patients’ co-payments.[^stabilizacja]

## Fund-contracted doctors: the self-governing body divides the budget

A doctor who wants to treat the funds’ patients must hold an authorisation and belong to a regional association of fund-contracted doctors. The funds do not pay doctors directly. They hand the association a total amount, and the association divides it among doctors according to a national fee schedule (EBM), in which every service has a value in points. In 2026 a point is worth 12.7404 cents.[^ow]

Most of this amount is a budget capped in advance: when doctors deliver more than planned, some services are paid below the fee-schedule price.[^honorar] Since 1 October 2025 the basic services of family doctors have been exempt from this cap — they are paid at the full price with no limit.[^hausarzt]

Private patients are billed under a separate official fee schedule (GOÄ). In it a doctor can apply a multiplier from 1 to 3.5, usually up to 2.3.[^goae] The same doctor therefore receives different amounts for the same service, depending on how the patient is insured.

## Hospitals

In 2025 Germany had 1,807 hospitals with 468,800 beds. Public hospitals have 47% of the beds, church-run and other not-for-profit hospitals 32%, and private ones 21%.[^szpitale]

According to a survey carried out for the hospital association, 66% of general hospitals had a negative result in 2024.[^barometer] The 2024 hospital reform provides that a hospital will receive part of its money simply for readiness to treat. The amending act, in force since 15 April 2026, postponed the introduction of this payment by a year.[^khag]

## What the patient pays

A visit to a doctor is free of charge. Until the end of 2026 the patient pays 10% of the price of a medicine — not less than €5 and not more than €10. For a day in hospital the patient pays €10, for at most 28 days a year. From 2027 the co-payment for a medicine will be from €7.50 to €15, and for a day in hospital €15. The total of co-payments may not exceed 2% of annual income, and 1% for people with chronic illness.[^doplaty] [^stabilizacja] The charge of €10 a quarter for visits to a doctor, introduced in 2003, was abolished unanimously by the Bundestag as of the end of 2012.[^praxisgebuehr]

A separate item is the services that a fund-contracted doctor offers for a fee because the fund does not cover them. According to a survey commissioned by the funds’ medical service, insured people spend at least €2.4 billion a year on them, most of it at ophthalmologists and gynaecologists.[^igel]

Patients choose their doctor freely and need no referral to a specialist; the funds are only required to offer a voluntary programme in which the patient undertakes to start with the family doctor.[^hzv]

## Doctors’ work and pay

At the end of 2025 there were 446,120 practising doctors: 233,519 in hospitals and 173,912 in outpatient care, of whom 105,264 in their own practice and 68,648 as employees of practices and centres. The number of doctors with their own practice is falling, and the number of employed doctors is rising.[^baek]

The office cautions that the surplus is not the doctor’s income: it relates to the practice, which may have several owners, and does not take into account, among other things, their pension contributions or the repayment of the purchase of the practice. The large gap between the mean and the median shows that the result is pushed up by a small group of very large practices.[^destatis] According to the OECD, a specialist with their own practice in Germany earned 4.91 times as much as the average worker (2023), and a salaried specialist 3.12 times (2024).[^oecd-remun]

## What is public

**Pay scales and fee schedules.** The collective agreements with their pay scales, the funds’ fee schedule with the point value and the fee schedule for private patients are public.[^tv] [^ow] [^goae]

**Board pay — by name, by law.** Every fund must, on 1 March each year, publish in the Federal Gazette and on its website the pay of every board member, together with supplements and pension provision.[^zarzady] The associations of fund-contracted doctors have the same duty. The chair of the federal association received a total of €464,782.23 in 2025.[^kbv-zarzad]

**Fees by specialty.** The federal association of fund-contracted doctors publishes a quarterly fee report: a doctor’s average turnover from treating the funds’ patients, by specialty and region. In the third quarter of 2024 it was €62,593 per doctor or psychotherapist, 4.0% more than a year earlier; a quarter earlier it was €64,269.[^honorar] This is revenue, not income, and only from the funds’ money.

**A statement on request.** A patient can ask the doctor for a statement of the services billed to the fund together with their approximate cost, and can ask the fund — on application — for information on the services and their costs. For a written quarterly statement the doctor may charge €1 plus postage.[^quittung] The doctor does not have to issue it on their own initiative, and we found no data on how often patients ask for it.

**Data on hospitals.** The federal hospital atlas, run since 2026 by the Federal Joint Committee, shows for 1,568 hospitals, among other things, the number of cases treated and nurse staffing.[^atlas]

**Waiting times — from surveys.** The associations have a statutory duty to find the patient an appointment with a specialist within four weeks.[^tss] We found no official measurement of waiting times; there are surveys by the funds and by doctors.

The earnings of an individual doctor are not public. Data on the fees of individual doctors are held by the association that divides the money; we found no publication of them. Payments by pharmaceutical companies are disclosed under a voluntary industry code, by name only with the doctor’s consent. For 2024 the companies reported €768.7 million, of which €93.3 million went to individuals rather than institutions.[^fsa]

## The check for “impossible hours”

Since the association knows all of a doctor’s billing, it can check whether it could have been performed in the time a day has. The law requires such a plausibility check: every service has a time assigned to it, and the association adds it up for each doctor.[^plaus] Under the rules of the association in Saxony, a review is triggered by billing more than 12 hours a day on at least three days in a quarter, or more than 780 hours in a quarter. Exceeding the threshold does not automatically mean a cut in fees — it is a reason for the doctor to explain the billing.[^plaus-kv]

This is a mechanism that Poland lacks: nobody adds up the hours a doctor has reported across all their places of work ([more on this](/en/mechanisms/multiple-jobs-parallel-contracts/)). In Germany it is possible because a fund-contracted doctor bills through a single institution. However, we found no data on how many such reviews take place or how much money is returned.

The funds and the associations must also run units for combating misconduct and report on this every two years.[^fehlverhalten] In 2024 the police recorded 20,553 cases of billing fraud in healthcare — almost ten times as many as a year earlier. As many as 18,595 of them, however, came from a single investigation, so the figure does not indicate a wave of abuse.[^bka]

## How many doctors there are and how long people wait

Germany has 475 doctors working with patients per 100,000 inhabitants — nearly a quarter more than Poland.[^eurostat-lekarze] Even so, according to the association of fund-contracted doctors, about 5,000 places for family doctors remain unfilled, and the average age of doctors and psychotherapists in outpatient care is 53.9 years.[^kbv-liczby]

In a 2025 survey by the federation of funds, 23% of insured people waited more than a month for a visit to a specialist; half got an appointment within 10 days.[^czekanie] The appointment service centres, which are meant to guarantee a visit within four weeks, arrange a small share of visits: over a year (from the fourth quarter of 2024 to the third quarter of 2025) it was 1.47 million cases, or 0.3% of the total.[^czekanie]

In 2025, 1.0% of Germans went without a visit because of cost, distance or waiting time — 2.9% in Poland, 2.4% on average in the EU.[^eurostat-silc]

## Satisfaction and outcomes

With spending clearly above the EU average, Germany’s outcomes are average: deaths from treatable causes are only slightly fewer than the EU average (86.8 per 100,000 inhabitants), and life expectancy is slightly below the EU average (81.5 years).[^wyniki] We write about the opinion surveys [separately](/en/analysis/satisfaction-with-healthcare-in-poland/).

## Professional and criminal liability

**Professional.** Germany separates two matters. Professional misconduct is adjudicated by the medical chambers and the professional courts of the federal states. The licence to practise itself is decided on by an authority of the federal state: it revokes the licence when a doctor proves unworthy or unreliable, and can suspend it for the duration of proceedings.[^bao]

There are no figures for the whole country. In 2026 the federal government replied to parliament that such a summary would require data from the federal states.[^bundestag] The only data we found were given by Lower Saxony: in 2016–2025 the licence to practise was revoked there 17 times, 19 people gave it up themselves, and it was suspended 15 times.[^nds] The professional courts rule rarely: in Baden-Württemberg the court of second instance issued three judgments in 2025. It can impose a fine of up to €50,000, but it does not take away the licence to practise — that remains a decision of the authority.[^aekbw] For comparison: [how this looks in Poland](/en/analysis/doctor-discipline-licence-revocation/).

**Criminal.** The criminal code has no separate offence of medical error. A doctor is liable for negligent homicide, punishable by up to five years’ imprisonment or a fine, and for negligent bodily harm — up to three years or a fine. A court can impose a ban on practising the profession for a period of one to five years, and exceptionally for life.[^stgb] Proceedings for a less serious offence may end in discontinuation on condition that an imposed obligation is fulfilled, for example a payment to a charitable cause.[^stpo]

How many doctors appear before a criminal court is not known: the prosecution statistics distinguish provisions, not the offender’s profession or whether the case concerned treatment.[^bayern]

**Assessment of errors out of court.** A patient can turn, free of charge, to a conciliation board at a medical chamber or to the funds’ medical service. In 2025 the chambers’ boards issued 3,946 decisions and confirmed an error in 1,145 cases. The funds’ medical service prepared 11,735 expert opinions and found an error in 3,503, including 2,700 cases of an error that caused harm.[^baek-bledy] [^md] Both institutions caution that this is not a measure of how often errors occur — it is the number of cases that patients reported. We found no fund in Germany that would pay compensation without establishing fault; how such a fund works in other countries is described in the text on [compensation without establishing fault](/en/mechanisms/no-fault-compensation-and-doctors-liability/).

## Method

Secondary sources and materials from parties to the dispute — hospital associations, doctors’ associations, the funds — are marked in the footnotes.
