In France most of the price of a visit to a doctor is covered by the public fund, and the rest usually by a supplementary policy — the share of out-of-pocket spending is among the lowest in the EU. We describe how this system works according to data from French public bodies, what it costs and what is public in it. For every doctor it is known whether they keep to the official tariff, and the statistical office publishes doctors’ incomes by specialty. How much public money an individual doctor received — we found that in no public source.

## One fund and a budget voted by parliament

Health insurance (*Assurance maladie*) is part of the social security system. Since 2016 everyone who works in France or lives there on a stable and lawful basis has been entitled to benefits.[^puma] The money comes from contributions and taxes, including the general CSG contribution, which is 9.2% on wages.[^csg] How much the public fund may spend in a given year is set by parliament in the annual social security financing act — this is the national health insurance spending target (ONDAM).[^ondam]

In 2025 the spending target was not exceeded for the first time since 2020. Even so, the health insurance branch closed the year with a deficit of €15.9 billion.[^ondam]

Alongside the public fund there is supplementary insurance — mutual societies, insurers and employee institutions. According to the health statistics office DREES, in 2023 only 3.4% of residents did not have it. People on low incomes get a policy free of charge or for a small premium — at the end of 2024, 7.79 million people used this.[^complementaire] Of the €254.8 billion spent on medical care and medical goods, in 2024 the public fund financed 78.7%, supplementary insurance 12.8% and households directly 7.8%.[^drees-fin]

## What it costs

France spends more than 11% of GDP on health — more than the EU average and markedly more than Poland. At the same time, the share of out-of-pocket spending is more than a third lower than in Poland.[^eurostat-sha]

<CountryBars title="Current health expenditure in 2024 (Eurostat)" codes={['FR', 'PL', 'EU']} keys={['healthGdp', 'perCapitaEur', 'perCapitaPps', 'publicShare', 'oopShare']} />

According to DREES, in terms of the share of spending borne by patients France is among the EU countries with the lowest figure, behind Luxembourg and Croatia.[^drees-rac] A low share does not mean that nobody forgoes treatment because of the price — we return to this in the section on access.

## A fee per visit and tariff sectors

Doctors outside hospitals are paid mainly for each visit and procedure. The rates are set by a multi-year agreement between the public fund and the doctors’ unions. The basis for reimbursing a visit to a family doctor is €30, of which the fund reimburses 70%.[^ameli-tm]

How much a doctor may charge the patient depends on the doctor’s sector. A sector 1 doctor applies the official tariff. A sector 2 doctor may charge more (*dépassements d'honoraires*). An intermediate arrangement is the OPTAM option, in which a sector 2 doctor undertakes to limit the extra fees. In 2024, 53% of those eligible had joined it.[^hcaam-2026]

Among family doctors sector 2 is marginal; among specialists the halfway mark was passed in 2020. In 2025 the average sector 2 specialist added 51% to the tariff (55% in 2012),[^hcaam-2026] and the number of doctors in this sector has grown by 35% since 2012.[^hcaam] According to DREES, in 2024 extra fees made up 19.4% of the fees of all specialists, and 23.5% among psychiatrists. In practices outside sector 1 alone it was 31.4%, and among psychiatrists 40.2%.[^drees-spec]

## Hospitals

Hospital care cost €120.7 billion in 2024, or 47% of spending on medical care. Public hospitals — in which the statistics also count military hospitals and some private not-for-profit facilities — accounted for €93.7 billion, and the private sector, meaning for-profit clinics and the remaining not-for-profit facilities, for €27.1 billion. The public fund covered 94.3% of the cost of care in public hospitals and 87.9% in private ones.[^drees-szpitale]

Public hospitals have 71% of the beds in medical, surgical and obstetric wards. Day-case stays, by contrast, fall more often to private for-profit clinics: in 2024, 5.0 million of the 10.6 million such stays.[^drees-lozka]

For care in these wards a hospital is paid according to the work done (*tarification à l'activité*). Each stay is assigned to one of the groups of patients with a similar cost, and each group has a tariff, to which items such as expensive medicines are added.[^atih] Since 2025 the model has had three parts: payment for activity, grants for public health objectives and grants for specific tasks, such as research and teaching.[^reforma-t2a]

Public hospitals run a deficit: according to the first DREES estimate, their combined deficit in 2025 is 2.1% of revenue.[^drees-deficyt]

## What the patient pays

The patient covers the part of the cost that the public fund does not reimburse (*ticket modérateur*) — 30% for a visit to a doctor. It is usually paid for them by supplementary insurance. People with chronic diseases on the statutory list (ALD) have the treatment of those diseases covered at 100%.[^ameli-tm]

On top of that come small charges: €2 for each visit and test, and €1 per pack of medicine and €4 per medical transport. Each of these two groups of charges has a separate annual cap — €70 each from 1 October 2026, previously €50 each.[^ameli-franchise] For each day in hospital the patient pays a flat rate, €23 since March 2026.[^forfait]

Extra fees above the tariff charged by sector 2 doctors are not reimbursed by the public fund. They are covered by the policy — to the extent it provides for this — or by the patient.[^drees-fin]

## Doctors’ work and pay

On 1 January 2026 there were 242,200 doctors working in France, including 101,000 family doctors. 42% practise exclusively in their own practice (51% in 2012), and 13% combine a practice with a salaried post.[^drees-lekarze]

Every few years DREES publishes the incomes of doctors in private practice, combining the fund’s data with tax returns. This is income from the activity after deducting the costs of the practice, before tax — not a salary and not the sum of fees.[^drees-dochody]

Women earned €90,000 on average, men €148,000.[^drees-dochody] According to the OECD, a specialist in private practice in France earned 5.56 times as much as the average worker, a family doctor 3.27 times (data for 2021), and a salaried specialist 2.29 times (2023).[^oecd-remun]

Doctors in public hospitals are paid according to a national pay scale with 13 steps: from €4,633.98 gross a month on the first to €9,368.05 on the last, without on-call duty and supplements. A doctor who gives up private practice in the hospital receives a supplement of €1,010 a month.[^siatka] The scale is public, so a hospital doctor’s basic pay can be estimated if their step is known. Some doctors may also run a private practice in a public hospital: in 2022 this was used by 4,671 doctors, who earned €644 million in fees from it, including €73 million in extra fees above the tariff — about 2% of all such extra fees in the country.[^hcaam]

France has also capped the rates that public hospitals pay to doctors hired as locums through agencies. The cap is €2,681 for 24 hours of a doctor’s work. In July 2026 the Council of State upheld the principle of the cap itself, but annulled the implementing provisions on four points — including the flat-rate inclusion in the cap of hardship supplements and reimbursement of expenses — and ordered them to be corrected within six months.[^interim]

## What is public

**Every doctor’s tariff sector.** In the fund’s official directory (*Annuaire santé*) one can check whether a doctor has an agreement with the fund, which sector they are in and whether they have joined the OPTAM option. The new version of the directory does not show prices for the time being, and the doctor is obliged to display them in the surgery.[^annuaire]

**Reimbursements in the patient’s account.** The patient sees every reimbursement in their account on the fund’s website. Since 1 July 2026 they have also received an annual statement of services in five categories, including hospital, pharmacy, and visits and procedures outside hospital.[^releve]

**Incomes by specialty.** DREES publishes the average incomes of doctors in private practice, and the fund publishes open data on doctors by specialty, region and department. Where a group has fewer than five people, fees are not given.[^drees-dochody]

**The sum of the ten highest salaries in a hospital.** Public hospitals with a budget above €200 million announce every year the combined amount of the ten highest salaries — without names and without a breakdown into doctors and management. The university hospitals in Strasbourg reported €1,824,938 for 2022, or €182,494 per person on average.[^top10]

**Benefits from industry.** The public database Transparence Santé, run by the ministry of health, shows the agreements, remuneration and benefits that companies selling health products have declared as given to doctors and other healthcare entities.[^transparence]

The pay of an individual doctor in a public hospital is public only in part: according to the French commission on access to administrative documents, the fixed components of pay — grade and index — are disclosed on request, while bonuses and the total amount are usually not public.[^cada] We found the total amount that the public fund paid to an individual doctor in private practice in no public source.

The fund knows this total and uses it in its checks. In 2025 it detected and stopped fraud and abuse worth €723 million — 15% more than a year earlier. Providers accounted for more than 73% of this amount, insured persons for 16%. Providers are not only doctors: the largest amounts concerned health centres (€138 million), sellers of hearing aids and medical transport. The fund does not give a separate amount for doctors.[^fraude]

## How many doctors there are and how long people wait

France has only slightly more doctors working with patients than Poland: in 2024, 394 per 100,000 inhabitants compared with 386.[^eurostat-lekarze] How admissions to medical studies were restricted there (*numerus clausus*) is described in the text on [cutting medical school places](/en/analysis/cutting-medical-school-places/).

Access to a specialist is organised through the designated doctor (*médecin traitant*), whom the patient chooses. A visit to a specialist without a referral from the designated doctor is possible, but the fund then reimburses 30% of the tariff instead of 70%; a gynaecologist, an ophthalmologist and a psychiatrist can be seen directly. According to the French audit office, in mid-2025 88.5% of adults (48.7 million people) had a designated doctor, and 6.3 million insured persons did not.[^traitant]

In 2025, 3.7% of people in France went without a visit because of cost, distance or waiting time — in Poland 2.9%, and 2.4% on average in the EU. The reasons differ from those in Poland: in France it is more often the price (1.9% compared with 0.5% in Poland), in Poland the waiting time (2.2% compared with 1.4% in France).[^eurostat-silc] The last official survey of waiting times dates from 2018: half of patients then got an appointment with a family doctor within 2 days, and with an ophthalmologist within 52 days.[^czekanie]

## Professional and criminal liability

**Professional.** Doctors’ misconduct is ruled on by disciplinary chambers attached to the medical chamber (*Ordre des médecins*): regional chambers, chaired by an administrative judge, and a national chamber, whose rulings can be appealed on points of law to the Council of State. The penalties are a warning, a reprimand, a ban on practising for up to three years and striking off the register. Anyone can file a complaint, and a patient who has filed one is a party and can appeal.[^cnom-tryb]

The chamber last published a full breakdown of penalties for 2018: the regional chambers imposed 459 penalties, including 23 strikings-off, and dismissed 58% of complaints.[^cnom-2018] More recent reports give only the number of cases: in 2025 the national chamber heard 421 appeals, and a case took one year and ten months there on average.[^cnom-2025] In 2019 the French audit office found that complaints are not examined with sufficient rigour, and that the chambers’ rulings are merely posted at the medical chamber’s offices, so the public does not know them. It recommended mandatory publication of strikings-off and bans.[^cdc-ordre]

**Criminal.** A doctor is liable under the general provisions of the criminal code. Involuntary manslaughter carries three years’ imprisonment and a fine of €45,000, and where the perpetrator deliberately breached a duty of care — five years and €75,000.[^kary-fr] An act of 2000 distinguishes two situations here: whoever caused the harm directly is liable for ordinary carelessness; whoever only created the conditions for it to occur — for example by organising the work badly — is liable only for aggravated fault.[^fauchon]

We found no official statistics on convictions of doctors. Prosecutors’ offices increasingly inform the medical chamber about criminal proceedings against doctors — in 72 cases in 2024.[^cnom-2024]

**Compensation.** Since 2002, serious medical accidents in which nobody was at fault have been compensated in France by the public fund ONIAM. In 2025 it paid €195.7 million to injured persons; 1,065 people received compensation under the amicable procedure that year, and a commission issued its opinion on average a little over 10 months after the application was complete.[^oniam] The French audit office assessed it critically in 2017: the commissions rejected three quarters of applications because of the severity threshold.[^cdc] We write more about this in the text on [compensation without establishing fault](/en/mechanisms/no-fault-compensation-and-doctors-liability/).

**Billing fraud and abuse.** In 2025 the public fund referred about 8,000 cases for criminal proceedings — they concern all providers and insured persons, not only doctors — and in more than 70 cases excluded a provider from its agreement with the fund.[^fraude]

## Method

We base the description of the system on publications of the health statistics office DREES (the health accounts for 2024, the survey of doctors’ incomes, data on the number of doctors), Senate documents, the public fund’s website (ameli.fr), a report of the High Council for the Future of Health Insurance (HCAAM) and Eurostat data. Secondary sources — the press and communications of the hospital federation — are marked in the footnotes. The French website with the texts of legal acts does not allow automated access, so we cite several provisions after official accounts of them — a government reply, a decision of the Council of State, materials of the hospital federation — and note this in the footnote.
