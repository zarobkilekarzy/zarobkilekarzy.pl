The National Health Fund (NFZ) pays for a hospital stay according to the group into which the description of treatment falls. Whether the description matches what was done is checked by an inspection — but only in the months the Fund chooses. We show this mechanism using the example of the neurosurgery department of the Nicolaus Copernicus Provincial Hospital in Koszalin, which the NFZ inspected twice and twice assessed negatively. The case was uncovered, researched and described by nicolabo, author of the site Hospitalizacje w Polsce (“Hospital stays in Poland”) — in an article and in a film.[^nicolabo] We present the results of that investigation and analyses; we checked the figures against the NFZ source data.

## The description of the stay sets the price

A hospital does not send the NFZ a bill for individual activities. After the patient is discharged, the system assigns the stay to one of several hundred Diagnosis-Related Groups (JGP, *Jednorodne Grupy Pacjentów*) on the basis of the diagnoses and procedures entered in the report. Each group has a value in points. How the points turn into payment is described in our text [How the NFZ pays hospitals](/en/mechanisms/nfz-payment-system/).

The NFZ does not pay for minutes or for the actual cost of a procedure, but for the group into which the description fell. The difference between the rungs is meant to cover the difference in costs: the operating theatre, anaesthesia, the implant, a bed on the ward. If a simpler procedure lands one rung higher, the hospital receives money for costs it did not incur. Under a contract in which the doctor receives a percentage of the NFZ valuation, part of this difference goes to the doctor — we write about this in [Contract or employment](/en/mechanisms/contract-vs-employment/).[^katalog]

Rungs 1 and 2 are the same procedure. Rungs 3 and 4 are separated by a single code: of the 77 procedures on which assignment to H52 or H53 depends, 65 are shared, and the higher group is decided by the recorded implant.[^lista] Whether the description matches the treatment can only be seen in the medical records. Both inspections in Koszalin concerned exactly these steps: whether the denervation had to take place on a ward (Article 58 of the Act on healthcare services)[^art58] and whether the H52 stays were correctly classified.[^kontrole]

</Explainer>

A group has one value regardless of whether the patient leaves after a day or after a week. Only after a set number of days is an extra amount paid for each further day (in H52, after 31 days). This is a deliberate incentive: a hospital that treats patients for a shorter time keeps the difference in costs. In some groups, however, a short stay is valued lower: in H53 a stay shorter than one day is worth 9,225 points instead of 10,026, in H55 — 3,639 instead of 3,955. H52 has no such rate: a zero-day stay — the patient leaves on the day of admission — is valued the same as a stay of several days, at 15,040 points.[^katalog]

The catalogue also lists lower rates for “planned hospitalisation” (in H52 — 13,837 points), but they apply only under a separate type of contract with the NFZ.[^umowy] The neurosurgery department in Koszalin has a contract for ordinary hospitalisation and billed every one of its H52 stays at 15,040 points: in the compilations of the e-Health Centre (*Centrum e-Zdrowia*), the sum of points in this group divides by 15,040 with no remainder — 1,150 stays in 2021 and 1,597 in 2022.[^ezdrowie] nicolabo drew our attention to the difference between the rates.

The incentive to describe a stay as more expensive is not an accusation — we write about it in more detail in the section [JGP points](/en/mechanisms/nfz-payment-system/#jgp-points-the-description-of-the-stay-decides-the-payment). The question is how the system catches billing that does not match the description of treatment.

## What can be seen in open data

On the dane.gov.pl portal the NFZ publishes the number of hospital stays in each hospital in each JGP group, month by month, broken down by length of stay, admission mode, sex and age of patients.[^d3009] Cells with fewer than five stays are hidden, so we estimate the figures for a single hospital — the method is described below the chart. National totals and the numbers of stays by length are given exactly by the NFZ JGP statistics portal.[^stat]

In 2021–2022 the hospital reported about two thirds of all zero-day H52 stays in Poland.[^d3009][^stat] The NFZ sees this difference in its own data — in a fuller form than the public compilation.

In April 2024 the first NFZ inspection at the hospital ended. It concerned a different group — H55 — and billing from 2022. In April the hospital billed about 105 H52 stays and a few H53. In May — about 16 H52 stays and about 77 H53, and in September as many as about 159 H53. Group H52 did not return to its former level.

The length of stay moved together with the group. In 2025 the hospital billed about 1.2 thousand H53 stays — over three times as many as the second hospital in the country — and about 97% of them ended on the day of admission. In other hospitals such an H53 stay accounts for about 5%. Koszalin reported more than half of all zero-day H53 stays in Poland.[^d3009]

<Aside title="To be fair: what these figures do not settle">

- **The data show billing, not the procedure.** A short stay may result from the technique (minimally invasive procedures), from patient selection or from the way of coding. Whether the description matches the treatment can only be settled by the medical records.
- **The change of group has more than one explanation.** The hospital may have started coding differently, or it may have changed the type of procedures it performs. The data cannot tell these apart.
- **The hospital’s figures are an estimate.** Under extreme assumptions about the hidden cells the picture does not change: in 2021–2023 at least 78% of H52 stays in Koszalin were zero-day stays, in other hospitals — at most 4.2%.

</Aside>

## A recommendation “on an ongoing basis”, checked after 15 months

The first inspection ended on 30 April 2024 with a recommendation: “Provide services in hospital conditions only where the aim of treatment cannot be achieved by outpatient treatment”, with the deadline “immediately, on an ongoing basis”.[^kontrole] It concerned group H55, in which the hospital bills, among other things, radiofrequency denervation — a pain-relief procedure on the nerves at the spinal joints, performed through the skin. It can be done in an outpatient clinic or on a ward — for more than three times as many points (rungs 1 and 2 in the “Explainer” box).

NFZ data show what happened next. In 2024 denervation was performed in the hospital on about 950 patients, and in the outpatient clinic on none.[^d4896] In 2025 the hospital billed about 1.6 thousand stays in group H55, about two thirds more than a year earlier, and was first in the country in this group.[^d3009] That year denervation was performed in the hospital on about 1.4 thousand patients, and in the clinic — again on none. From July 2025 to June 2026, during and after the second inspection, about a thousand patients had the procedure in the hospital, and fewer than five in the clinic.[^d4896]

The recommendation had the deadline “immediately, on an ongoing basis”, but the NFZ checked its implementation only in the second inspection, which began in August 2025, 15 months later. It found that the recommendation had not been implemented and issued it again, once more with the deadline “on an ongoing basis”.[^kontrole] Throughout this time the growing number of stays was visible in data that the Fund itself collects every month.

## Inspection consequences and the value of billing

How much the billing was worth is shown by the e-Health Centre compilations: the value of points billed by each provider in each group and in each year.[^ezdrowie] The groups’ points did not change, but their price rose. In the Koszalin neurosurgery department an H52 point was worth on average about <Money value={1.12} decimals={2} /> in 2021 and about <Money value={1.9} decimals={2} /> in 2025. A single H52 stay was therefore worth about <Money value={16900} scale="thousand" decimals={1} /> in billing, and four years later about <Money value={28600} scale="thousand" decimals={1} />.

A reported amount is not a transfer. A hospital in the network receives a lump sum, not a payment for each stay — we describe this in the section [The 98% threshold](/en/mechanisms/nfz-payment-system/#the-98-threshold-today-s-points-are-tomorrow-s-lump-sum). The lump sum for the next period does, however, depend on the number of units “performed and reported” in the previous period.[^rozp-psz] Reported stays are thus the basis of future funding, even when nobody has checked them.

The financial consequences of an inspection follow from irregularities found within the checked scope. For a hospital, the balance therefore depends not only on how much of its billing is irregular, but also on how much of it reaches an inspection.

## What follows from this

Selective inspection is a necessity: there is many times more billing than inspectors can read. What matters, then, is how the NFZ chooses hospitals, groups and months. The published results do not show why these months, and not others, were chosen in Koszalin. In the case described, the signal was in public data: for two years one hospital accounted for about two thirds of zero-day H52 stays in the country, and after the first inspection the same pattern appeared in another group. Such deviations can be searched for automatically, in all hospitals and groups at once.

Open data will not replace an inspection of the records. But they can suggest where to look — and make it possible to check whether the Fund takes the hint.
