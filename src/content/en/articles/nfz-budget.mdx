The “budget” of the National Health Fund (NFZ) is in practice the Fund’s financial plan, and the plan goes through several versions in the course of a year: the draft, the plan approved before the start of the year, successive amendments and the final plan. After the year comes the report on how the plan was carried out. Each version gives a different amount, which is why figures from different sources easily diverge. Here we count every year the same way and show the other figures alongside, in separate columns.

## One series, one definition

The main figure is total costs in the final NFZ financial plan, i.e. the amount the Fund could spend in a given year after all amendments to the plan. The plans are published in the NFZ Public Information Bulletin (BIP).[^plany] The year {CURRENT.year} is still under way, so we show the current plan, as amended on {CURRENT_PLAN.date} (order of the President of the NFZ no. {CURRENT_PLAN.ordinance}).[^z107] This amount may still change.

## What it means in real terms

A zloty from 2015 is worth less today. According to Statistics Poland (GUS), consumer prices rose by a total of about {formatEN(GROWTH.prices)}% from 2015 to 2025.[^gus-cpi] After adjusting for inflation, costs in the NFZ plan grew {formatEN(GROWTH.budgetReal, 2)} times over that period, i.e. roughly doubled. This is our own calculation: nominal growth ({formatEN(GROWTH.budget, 2)} times) divided by the rise in prices.

Since 2020 both costs in the final plan and the outturn have doubled: in both columns the growth to {LAST_FINAL.year} is {formatEN(LAST_FINAL.costs / y2020.costs, 2)} times.[^sprawozdania] Prices rose by about {formatEN((priceLevel(LAST_FINAL.year) / priceLevel(2020) - 1) * 100)}% over that time, so in real terms costs in the plan grew by about {formatEN((LAST_FINAL.costs / y2020.costs / (priceLevel(LAST_FINAL.year) / priceLevel(2020)) - 1) * 100)}%.[^gus-cpi]

## The 2026 plan: from PLN 217 billion to PLN 227 billion

The figure of PLN 217 billion that often comes up in connection with the NFZ budget for 2026 comes from the draft plan presented to the Sejm Health Committee in July 2025. Revenue and costs in it were <Money value={bn(DRAFT_2026.total)} scale="billion" /> each, including <Money value={bn(DRAFT_2026.subsidy, 0)} scale="billion" decimals={0} /> of subsidy from the state budget.[^kz88]

In 2026 the plan was amended several times. By the end of July revenue had been increased, including the subsidy, which rose to <Money value={bn(CURRENT.subsidy)} scale="billion" />.[^z73] After the amendment of {CURRENT_PLAN.date}, costs stand at <Money value={bn(CURRENT.costs)} scale="billion" /> and revenue at <Money value={bn(CURRENT.revenue)} scale="billion" />.[^z107] That is {f1(change(LAST_FINAL.costs, CURRENT.costs))}% more than costs in the final plan for {LAST_FINAL.year}. We compare the plan for the current year with the final plan for the previous year, not with its outturn, because these are two different quantities.

Articles about the NFZ also cite other amounts from the same plan, for example the costs of healthcare services. In the current plan for 2026 they come to <Money value={bn(CURRENT.benefits)} scale="billion" />.[^z107] This is part of total costs, not a competing version of the budget. We also write about how the plan changes during the year in our analysis [Following the public zloty](/en/analysis/following-the-public-zloty/).

The media also write about a gap in the Fund’s finances. In June 2026 Rynek Zdrowia, a healthcare news site, put the “hole” in the NFZ budget at about <Money value={16e9} scale="billion" decimals={0} />.[^rz] This is an estimate, not an item in the plan. In the current plan for 2026, costs exceed revenue by <Money value={bn(CURRENT.costs - CURRENT.revenue)} scale="billion" />.[^z107]

The draft plan for 2027, presented to the Sejm Health Committee in July 2026, is balanced, i.e. it has no deficit: revenue and costs in it are <Money value={bn(DRAFT_2027.total)} scale="billion" /> each, including <Money value={bn(DRAFT_2027.subsidy, 0)} scale="billion" decimals={0} /> of subsidy from the state budget.[^kz152] The draft budget act for 2027, however, provides for a subsidy for the NFZ of <Money value={bn(GAP_2027.subsidy)} scale="billion" />, so — as the Fiscal Council (Rada Fiskalna) points out — the Fund’s plan is inconsistent with the draft budget in this respect.[^rf] According to the Fiscal Council, which cites an estimate by its office, the NFZ’s funding shortfall in 2027 will be about <Money value={bn(GAP_2027.value, 0)} scale="billion" decimals={0} />, and that is after taking the higher subsidy into account. This is an estimate, not an item in the plan. The Council also notes that in 2021–2025 the costs actually incurred by the NFZ exceeded the initial plan by about PLN {OVERRUN_2021_2025.min}–{OVERRUN_2021_2025.max} billion a year.[^rf]

## The subsidy from the state budget

Until 2019 the NFZ plan contained no general subsidy (dotacja podmiotowa) from the state budget. It included only targeted subsidies, for example for emergency medical teams. In the plans for 2020–2023 the general subsidy did not exceed <Money value={2.6e9} scale="billion" />. In 2024 it rose to <Money value={bn(y2024.subsidy)} scale="billion" />, in 2025 to <Money value={bn(LAST_FINAL.subsidy)} scale="billion" />, and in the current plan for 2026 it is <Money value={bn(CURRENT.subsidy)} scale="billion" />.[^plany][^z107] That is about {formatEN(CURRENT.subsidy / CURRENT.revenue * 100)}% of the Fund’s revenue.

## How much of this is pay

The NFZ financial plan has no item for “pay of medical staff”. Money for salaries in hospitals and clinics is included in the costs of services, together with medicines, equipment and the facilities’ other costs. The plan shows separately only the pay of the Fund’s own staff, less than <Money value={1e9} scale="billion" decimals={0} />.[^z107] How much the statutory pay rises in healthcare cost is therefore known only from estimates.

<Aside title="Where “69% of the growth” comes from">

The often-quoted figure that about 69% of the growth in spending goes on pay refers to something other than the NFZ plan. The proportion does not apply to 2025–2026.

In autumn 2025 the two measures gave the same result, but earlier they differed, so the pace of growth depends on which one is chosen. Since September 2021 the waiting time for a single service has grown from {f1(WHC_2021.single)} to {f1(WHC_LATEST.single)} months, and for a specialist consultation from {f1(WHC_2021.specialist)} to {f1(WHC_LATEST.specialist)} months.[^whc2021][^whc2025]

The amount alone does not say how much the burden has grown, however. Until the end of 2021, 7.75% of the base was deductible from income tax; since 2022 there has been no such deduction. At the same time the rules of income tax changed.[^rpo] We write more about this in our analysis [Following the public zloty](/en/analysis/following-the-public-zloty/).

## What the plan does not show

The NFZ plan says how much the Fund will spend on particular types of services, but not how much of it will reach specific people. Nobody publishes the total paid to one person from public money. What we know about doctors’ pay is shown in our compilation [Doctor pay distribution](/en/data/doctor-pay-distribution/), and [we call](/en/demand/) for these totals to be made public: under a permanent identifier, without names.

How large the debts of public hospitals are is shown in our compilation [Hospital debt](/en/data/hospital-debt/).
