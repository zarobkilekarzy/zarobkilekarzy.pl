This text describes the path from the matura — the Polish secondary-school leaving examination — to specialist training: which matura subjects count in admissions, how many points you need, what you learn over six years and how anatomy is taught in the dissection room, and then, after graduation, the Medical Final Examination (LEK), the internship and specialist training. We compare the difficulty of medical studies with other demanding programmes — law, psychology, architecture, mathematics, physics and computer science — using the same measures: the cut-off score, the number of applicants per place and the share of students who get through the first year.

These numbers show that medicine is hard to get into mainly because there are many more applicants than places. The matura result required is similar to that for law or psychology, and fewer students drop out during their studies than in many other programmes. What sets medicine apart is its length: six years and at least 5,700 hours of classes.

## The matura and admissions

Admission to medicine depends above all on extended-level biology and chemistry, and at many universities also on mathematics or physics. Each university has its own formula. The Medical University of Warsaw adds up the results in biology, chemistry and physics or mathematics to a maximum of 300 points, the Jagiellonian University takes the average of the same subjects on a scale of up to 100, and the Medical University of Białystok adds up two subjects to a maximum of 200 points.[^wzory] That is why below we compare the percentage of the maximum score rather than the points themselves.

For 2026/27, 39 universities received an admission limit for full-time studies in Polish.[^lim2026] Fourteen of them published their cut-off, meaning the score of the last person admitted. The others show results only to applicants, in their accounts in the admissions system.

What does 80% in the matura mean? According to the percentile scales of the Central Examination Board (CKE), in 2026 at least that much in extended-level biology was scored by 6% of those taking the subject, and in extended-level chemistry by 10%.[^cke] That is a result from the upper part of the distribution, but measured among everyone taking the exam, not among applicants to medicine. The CKE does not publish such data.

The cut-off is not set in advance. It is the score of the last person who fit within the limit of places, so it rises when applicants multiply and places do not. The Jagiellonian University shows this well. In 2024–2026 the number of places in medicine barely changed ({UJ_LEKARSKI.map((r) => r.places).join(', ')}), while the number of applicants grew from {formatEN(UJ_LEKARSKI[0].candidates)} to {formatEN(UJ_LEKARSKI[2].candidates)}. Over that time the cut-off rose from {formatEN(UJ_LEKARSKI[0].threshold, 2)} to {formatEN(UJ_LEKARSKI[2].threshold, 2)} points.[^uj] The difficulty of the matura in a given year also affects the cut-off, but with a fixed number of places it is the number of applicants that decides how high you have to reach. Who sets the number of places, and on what basis, we describe in the analysis [Cutting medical school places](/en/analysis/cutting-medical-school-places/) and in the text on [restricted access to the profession](/en/mechanisms/restricted-access-to-the-profession/).

### What medical studies cost

Full-time studies in Polish at public universities are free. You pay for part-time studies — in the limit for 2026/27 that is {formatEN(PAID_PLACES.extramural)} of {formatEN(PAID_PLACES.total)} places — for studies at non-public universities, full-time ones too, and for studies in a foreign language.[^lim2026][^psw-oplaty]

Six years of part-time studies at the Medical University of Warsaw cost **<Money value={417000} />** — the university gives this amount itself. Non-public universities charge from PLN {TUITION.privateMin} to PLN {TUITION.privateMax} for the first year, and studies in English at the Medical University of Warsaw cost €18,800 a year.[^czesne] The fee may not exceed the cost of running the programme, and during the studies the university may raise it at most by inflation, by no more than 30% in total.[^psw-oplaty]

Those who pay for studies in Polish have been able, since December 2021, to take out a medical studies loan. The bank transfers it to the university, at most <Money value={CREDIT.perSemester} /> per semester, or up to <Money value={CREDIT.max} /> for the whole programme — less than the tuition at most universities.[^kredyt-mp] Interest during the studies and for the following 12 years is covered by the state budget. After that the doctor repays the loan over 12 years and pays half of the interest. The loan is written off in full if, for 10 years within 12 years of graduation, the doctor works in Poland at a facility treating patients with public money and in that time becomes a specialist in one of the priority fields.[^psw-kredyt] By the end of September 2026 the bank had signed {formatEN(CREDIT.contracts)} such contracts worth a total of <Money value={CREDIT.valueBln * 1e9} scale="billion" decimals={2} />, and interest subsidies cost the budget <Money value={CREDIT.interest2025Mln * 1e6} scale="million" decimals={1} /> in 2025 alone.[^bgk]

How much the state spends on a student in free studies is not known. The Ministry of Health replies that in the subsidy for universities “it is not possible to separate the funds” allocated to medicine. As an approximation it points to the fee for part-time studies, from <Money value={44000} /> to almost <Money value={60000} /> a year[^mz-18621] — although the Medical University of Warsaw and the Pomeranian Medical University charge more.

At the Jagiellonian University the picture is similar. In 2026 the last person admitted to law had 86 points, to psychology at the Faculty of Philosophy 85.5, to analytical computer science 92, and to medicine 81.33. For ordinary computer science and mathematics 60 and 64 points were enough, because there were fewer applicants per place — 3.96 and 2.52.[^uj]

The SGH Warsaw School of Economics comes out similarly. It admits to full-time studies in Polish from one list for the whole school, and students choose their programme at the end of the first year. In 2026 its cut-off was {SGH_2026.points} of {SGH_2026.max} points, or {sghRow.display} of the maximum. That is within the range of the cut-offs for medicine ({thresholdRange.min}–{thresholdRange.max}%, chart above).[^sgh] The SGH score, however, includes four matura subjects and the school’s own test, so the comparison is approximate. SGH does not publish the number of applicants per place.

Programmes count points from different matura subjects, so the same points do not mean exactly the same thing. The CKE scales show, however, that 80% at extended level is a similarly rare result in the subjects that count for these programmes: in 2026 it was achieved by 6% of those taking biology, 8% mathematics, 10% chemistry and 14% Polish. The exception is English, in which 46% of candidates scored at least 80%.[^cke] A foreign language counts towards the result in some programmes — at the Jagiellonian University it is 30% of the points for law, and at the University of Warsaw 10% of the points for medicine.[^wzory-inne]

So the matura result required for medicine is similar to that for law or psychology at the same universities and lower than for the most popular computer science programme. What sets medicine apart is the number of applicants.

In medicine a similar share of students moves on to the second year on time as in architecture, clearly more than in law and psychology and almost twice as many as in mathematics. ### The longest programme

What really sets medicine apart is its length. The standard of medical education requires 12 semesters, at least 5,700 hours of classes and 360 ECTS credits.[^std] Long-cycle architecture studies, which also have a standard, require 3,800 hours for the same number of ECTS credits.[^arch] Law and psychology are five-year programmes worth 300 ECTS credits, without an education standard.[^inne] Psychology will get one from 19 February 2027.[^psych]

## Six years: subjects and exams

The standard divides the studies into groups of classes. The first years are basic sciences: anatomy, histology, biochemistry, physiology, and then pathology, microbiology and pharmacology. From the fourth year clinical subjects, taught in hospitals, dominate.[^wumplan] The sixth year consists of 900 hours of practical clinical teaching on hospital wards, plus 600 hours of summer placements.[^std]

The Medical University of Warsaw curriculum for students who started in 2026 has 30 exams: two in the first year (anatomy and histology), then from five to seven a year.[^wumplan] Each exam has three dates — the first and two resits. Those who fail may in some cases be admitted conditionally to the next year or repeat the year; at the Medical University of Warsaw the same year may be repeated only once.[^wumreg]

### Clinical classes with patients

Practical classes in clinical subjects may take place “only in clinics and hospital wards” whose profile and number of patients treated allow students to acquire the required skills. On top of that come exercises in simulation centres, and in the sixth year simulation may take from 5% to 15% of the hours. A group at a patient’s bedside has at most six people.[^std2025]

A university does not need its own hospital. The act on the professions of physician and dentist allows practical teaching in healthcare providers that have an agreement with the university, if they treat patients within the scope of the curriculum, employ doctors with the appropriate qualifications and have the necessary diagnostic facilities. The student works there under the direct supervision of a tutor.[^uzl-praktyka] Under the act on medical activity, hospitals run by medical universities are obliged to train students and make wards available to the university, and other hospitals may do so on the basis of an agreement. Only such hospitals and wards may use the names “clinic”, “clinical” and “university”.[^udl] The Supreme Audit Office (NIK), which in 2025 audited ten clinical hospitals, found that they provided proper conditions for training students.[^nik]

## Anatomy and the dissection room

Anatomy is the first big exam. At the Medical University of Warsaw it takes 180 hours of the first year, at the Jagiellonian University 190, at Wroclaw Medical University 160, and in Białystok 190.[^sylabusy] Most of these hours are practicals in the dissection room, on specimens from human bodies, bones and models. Structures have to be known by their Latin names, and at the Medical University of Warsaw also in Polish and English.

The exam usually has two parts. In the practical part the student moves from station to station and names the structures pointed to by a pin on a specimen. At the Medical University of Warsaw there are 30 stations with two pins each and one minute for each; at the Jagiellonian University — 20 specimens, 40 seconds each, and you pass after identifying at least half. The second part is a test with 100–120 questions.[^egzpr] During the year there are also tests; at the Jagiellonian University there are five, and you need at least half of their points to sit the exam.[^egzpr]

The specimens most often come from people who during their lifetime donated their body to the university. The act on cemeteries requires a written declaration for this; universities also accept bodies that no family member buried, on the basis of a decision of the district head (starosta).[^cment] Body donation programmes have their own rules — the Jagiellonian University, Wroclaw Medical University and the University of Warsaw require a declaration certified by a notary.[^donacje] After the classes end, universities bury the donors and hold farewell ceremonies; at the University of Rzeszów the third such ceremony took place in 2025.[^ur]

## Virtual tables, headsets and 3D printing

Since the 2025/26 academic year the rules have explicitly allowed anatomy to be taught outside the dissection room. From 5% to 15% of the hours of practicals in morphological sciences may take place in simulated conditions: on a virtual anatomy table, on 3D specimens and models, charts and manikins. The dissection room stays “to the extent of hours allowing the required learning outcomes to be achieved”.[^std2025]

Virtual tables — large touch screens with a three-dimensional model of the body that can be cut layer by layer — are already in use at more than a dozen universities. The Medical University of Lodz bought two for <Money value={1114000} scale="million" decimals={3} /> from EU funds in 2017, the Medical University of Białystok one in 2023, and the Medical University of Warsaw one in 2024 for <Money value={632500} />.[^stoly] Since 2022 Jagiellonian University Collegium Medicum has used 10 HoloLens 2 augmented reality headsets in anatomy classes, in which a model of the body is displayed in the space of the room.[^holo] In 2026 the Pomeranian Medical University opened a new anatomy building with virtual reality headsets and a virtual table — next to 75 dissection stations.[^pum] None of the universities whose information we obtained has replaced the dissection room with technology.

**In studies the new methods do no worse than traditional ones, and VR headsets and 3D-printed models even gave better test results in most meta-analyses.** The details:

- **Virtual reality (VR) headsets** gave moderately better test results than traditional methods in two meta-analyses of randomised trials, but a third found no significant difference.[^vr]
- **Augmented reality (AR)**, a model overlaid on the view of the room, did no better than traditional methods in a meta-analysis of 24 studies.[^vr]
- **3D-printed models** give the most consistent positive result, although the studies are small and very varied.[^druk]
- **Virtual tables** have no meta-analyses of randomised trials. In a review of 22 studies, student results improved in 86% of them, but only 2.4% to 30.2% of those surveyed preferred to learn on the table alone.[^telecan]
- **Dissecting yourself** did not give better test results than learning on prepared specimens, models or with computer programs — that was the finding of a review of 27 studies involving 7,731 people. The studies, however, tested knowledge shortly after the classes.[^wilson]

The new methods also have side effects. In a survey of 84 students at Jagiellonian University Collegium Medicum, 71% reported symptoms resembling motion sickness when using VR headsets.[^walecki]

The dissection room requirement itself does not come from an act of parliament or from the medical self-government, but from the science minister’s regulation on the education standard. A university’s infrastructure is to include “in particular a dissection room and a microscopy laboratory”, and classes may also take place at healthcare providers that have an agreement with the university.[^std] The rules do not, however, define what a dissection room is — for example, whether a lab with prepared specimens from human bodies, where students do not dissect themselves, is enough.

The dispute over dissection rooms concerns mainly the new medical programmes. In 2023 the Residents’ Agreement of the OZZL doctors’ union stated that 17 units were running medicine without their own dissection room. These are the organisation’s data, without a list of universities or a description of the method, so they cannot be checked.[^pr]

## More students and the quality of training

Since 2023 the medical self-government has warned that the rapid growth in students and new programmes will lower the standard of training. The Presidium of the Supreme Medical Council wrote in 2025 that ensuring proper quality “requires qualified and experienced teaching staff and access to clinical facilities”.[^nrl-jakosc] These are the same conditions the education standard lists: a dissection room, classes with patients in clinics and small groups. So the dispute is about whether universities that admit more and more students meet these conditions.

A medical programme may be launched by an academic university with a high research category in medical sciences, or by a university that already runs studies preparing for a medical profession, such as nursing, and has a research category in medical or health sciences or employs at least 12 academic teachers in these fields. Permission is granted by the minister of science after consulting the Polish Accreditation Committee (PKA) and the minister of health, but their opinions are not binding.[^pswin] In 2022–2023 ten universities received permission despite a negative opinion from the PKA.[^mnisw-pka]

When in 2024 the PKA, at the minister’s request, assessed eight of these programmes, five received a negative assessment. The ministry did not withdraw their permission, but it did not grant them an admission limit for the following year. A year later three of those five universities were assessed positively, and two — the universities of applied sciences in Nowy Sącz and Nowy Targ — negatively again.[^mnisw-pka] Earlier the Supreme Audit Office (NIK) found that staff shortages concerned “mainly newly created programmes at universities that had no experience in training medical staff”. The Ministry of Health had also not examined how exceeding admission limits affects the quality of training.[^nik-kadry]

Whether weaker doctors are coming through is not known, because programmes opened in 2023 will produce their first graduates in 2029 at the earliest. The results of universities that already have graduates, however, do not line up with the divide into “old” and “new”. In the spring 2026 LEK session, the highest average among first-time candidates was achieved by graduates of the University of Opole and Lazarski University, that is, two new universities. The lowest was at the Silesian Academy, also new, and the medical universities in Warsaw, Lublin and Gdańsk also came below the average.[^cem-2026]

The LEK is a test of knowledge, not of skills at the patient’s bedside. Those are not compared publicly between universities by anyone. The PKA responds to the self-government’s criticism that claims of a general decline in quality are “too general, subjective and unfair” towards universities with positive assessments. It also points out that its procedures for medical programmes were approved in 2025 by the World Federation for Medical Education (WFME), and that the PKA itself decides neither on creating programmes nor on admission limits.[^pka]

### What tools exist

Some tools already exist. The PKA assesses a new medical programme after the first, third and sixth year, and a university with a negative assessment may not receive an admission limit or may lose its permission.[^mnisw-pka] The science ministry has also announced a change in the law: only academic universities with a high research category (A+, A or B+) in medical sciences or health sciences could apply to run medicine. The change has not been passed so far: the consolidated text of the act from September 2026 still provides for both routes to a medical programme.[^mnisw-pka][^pswin] The Supreme Medical Council proposes more: a binding PKA opinion, phasing out programmes without a positive assessment, a re-assessment of all programmes opened since 2017 and supervision by one ministry instead of two.[^nrl-pakiet]

<Aside variant="editorial" title="Quality can be measured if the data are public">

The dispute about quality is conducted in generalities today, because comparable data on each university are missing. The CEM publishes LEK results by university, but nobody sets them against the number of students, the teaching staff, places in clinics or dropout at the same university. We believe the state should publish every year, for each medical programme: the number of students per academic teacher, the number of students on clinical wards, the share who get through the first year, first-attempt LEK results and PKA assessments broken down by criterion. Then the admission limit could be tied to how many students a university can actually train well, instead of arguing over whether “more” always means “worse”.

</Aside>

### Postgraduate internship

The diploma does not yet give the full right to practise. At first the graduate receives it only for the duration of the postgraduate internship.[^uzl-pwz] A doctor’s internship lasts 13 months. The fixed part covers internal medicine (11 weeks), paediatrics and surgery (8 each), family medicine (6), emergency medicine (3) and intensive care (2), and for 10 weeks the intern trains in a field of their own choice.[^staz]

Interns are assigned by the regional medical council. Those with a higher average exam grade from their studies are assigned first to the hospital of their choice, and the internship is organised and financed by the regional marshal (<span lang="pl">marszałek województwa</span>).[^uzl-staz] From 1 July 2026 an intern’s basic salary is <Money value={8458.38} decimals={2} /> gross a month.[^placa] The intern treats patients under the supervision of an experienced doctor; among other things they may write prescriptions and certify death, but only as part of the internship. They receive the full right to practise only after the internship and after passing the LEK.[^uzl-staz][^uzl-pwz] How many of those admitted to medical school reach a licence to practise in this way we calculate step by step in the analysis [Who drops out of medical school, and who leaves the country](/en/analysis/medical-school-dropouts-and-emigration/#from-a-university-place-to-a-licence-to-practise). It is about 55% of the places in the admission limit, but most of the difference is places for foreigners and in foreign-language programmes, not dropout.

The Ministry of Health has prepared a draft act that shortens the internship to six months. In the government’s list of legislative work its adoption is planned for the fourth quarter of 2026.[^ud384] The Supreme Medical Council opposes this: in its view, entering the profession without an internship is “simply dangerous both for patients and for the doctor themselves”.[^nrl-staz] For now it is a draft, not the law in force.

## Specialist training

After the internship and the LEK a doctor may practise independently. To become, for example, a surgeon, a paediatrician or a family doctor, they still have to complete specialist training and pass the State Specialisation Examination.[^uzl-spec] The Minister of Health’s regulation lists **77** medical specialties. Right after the internship you can start 69 of them, and the remaining eight, such as clinical transplantology or paediatric urology, only after obtaining another specialty.[^rozp-spec]

Training takes **from four to six years**. Four years are, among others, family medicine and ophthalmology, five — internal medicine, paediatrics and psychiatry, and six — general surgery, anaesthesiology and intensive care, and neurosurgery.[^rozp-spec][^cmkp-prog] Some specialties begin with a shared basic module: cardiology is two years of internal medicine and three years of cardiology. After the basic module you can move to a related field without starting from scratch, if you qualify in a later recruitment round. Training is extended by absences such as illness or maternity leave.[^uzl-spec]

So from admission to medical school to the title of specialist it takes from 11 to 13 years, not counting breaks and waiting for a place. What this means for the number of specialists in the coming years we describe in the analysis [Why Poland is short of doctors](/en/analysis/why-poland-is-short-of-doctors/).

### Residency and non-residency places

There are two kinds of specialist training places. Residencies are granted and financed from the state budget by the minister of health. A resident doctor has an employment contract with the facility where they train.[^uzl-spec] From 1 July 2026 their basic salary is <Money value={RESIDENT_PAY.other.first} decimals={2} /> gross a month, and in one of the {PRIORITY_FIELDS.length} priority fields, such as paediatrics or emergency medicine, <Money value={RESIDENT_PAY.priority.first} decimals={2} />. After two years it rises to <Money value={RESIDENT_PAY.other.later} decimals={2} /> and <Money value={RESIDENT_PAY.priority.later} decimals={2} />.[^rez-placa] A resident may also voluntarily commit to working in Poland for two years out of five after the specialty, at a facility that treats patients with public money. In return they receive PLN {COMMITMENT.other} or PLN {COMMITMENT.priority} more a month, and if they do not keep the commitment they repay up to {COMMITMENT.penaltyShare * 100}% of these supplements.[^uzl-spec]

Non-residency places are the remaining free places at facilities authorised to provide training. The doctor then trains, for example, under an employment contract or a civil-law contract with such a facility, on paid training leave from their employer or on a scholarship from the facility. They receive no salary from the minister of health’s pool.[^uzl-spec]

There is no entrance exam for specialist training. Applications are submitted through the online Training Monitoring System (SMK) twice a year: in February and in September. Up to 15 options can be listed, i.e. combinations of field, voivodeship and mode.[^uzl-spec] When there are more applicants than places, the order is set by a ranking. For a residency, what counts above all is the LEK result, up to 200 points, and a doctorate and publications can add at most 10 points more. In a tie, the average grade from the studies decides.[^uzl-spec][^rozp-spec] That is why some doctors take the LEK again even though they have already passed it.

For the autumn 2026 recruitment, the list of the Centre of Postgraduate Medical Education (CMKP) provides **6,530** residency places and **7,752** non-residency places. The most residencies are in internal medicine (774) and family medicine (749).[^cmkp-j26] The results of this recruitment are not yet known. In spring 2026, 2,868 doctors and dentists confirmed acceptance of a place, and 52% of residency places and 89% of non-residency places remained empty.[^cmkp-w26] So overall there are more places than applicants, but not in every field. The number of places and applicants in individual fields is shown in our overview [by specialty](/en/data/residents-pay-by-specialty/).

### The specialty examination

Training ends with the State Specialisation Examination (PES). It is organised twice a year by the Medical Examinations Centre. It consists of a test with 120 questions, passed with 60% of the points, and an oral exam. Those who score at least 75% in the test are exempt from the oral part.[^uzl-spec] A smaller share of candidates pass the PES test than the LEK: in 2025 **80.3%** of attempts, and among first-time candidates **89.5%**.[^pes] At the first attempt, 96.0% passed the LEK in autumn 2025.[^cem]
