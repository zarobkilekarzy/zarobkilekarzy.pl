Admission to medicine (*kierunek lekarski*, the six-year medical degree programme) in Poland runs separately for programmes taught in Polish and programmes taught in a foreign language — in practice in English, commonly called the English Division. In the 2026/27 academic year the latter pool accounts for 1,838 of the 10,730 places in the admission limit (*limit przyjęć*), or roughly one place in six.[^lim2026] Its students are mainly foreigners who pay full tuition fees and who, after graduating, usually do not work in Poland.

We look at how this pool has changed since 2012, how many of its graduates then take up work in Poland, what the degree costs and how much universities pay per hour of teaching in English. It is a legal, fee-paying educational service, and its prices and rates are published to the last zloty — unlike the amounts of the contracts on which most specialist doctors work (see [Contract or employment](/en/mechanisms/contract-vs-employment/)).

## A separate pool, a separate admission process

The admission limit for medicine is set every year by the Minister of Health in a regulation. Its annex lists each university and divides its places into four columns: full-time programmes in Polish (*studia stacjonarne*), separate places for foreigners on those programmes, part-time programmes in Polish (*studia niestacjonarne*) and programmes “in a language other than Polish”. The last column is the English Division.

Programmes in a foreign language have a separate admission process, and they are fee-paying for foreigners and Poles alike. That is why Konkret24, the fact-checking service of the TVN24 news channel, found two frequently repeated claims to be false: that foreigners take places from Polish candidates and that Polish taxpayers pay for their studies.[^konkret24]

Nor is the English Division only for foreigners. According to a report by the National Information Processing Institute (OPI PIB), foreigners make up 91% of students of medicine taught in a foreign language — the rest are Poles.[^radon]

## How many places are in a foreign language

We added up the “in a language other than Polish” column across all universities in successive regulations, starting from the 2012/13 academic year.[^limity]

<LimitTable title="The foreign-language pool is 43% larger than in 2012/13, but has stayed at a similar level since 2017/18 — and its share of the limit has fallen from 24.8% to 17.1%" />

Until 2017/18 the pool grew every year: from 1,288 to 1,865 places, or by 45%. Since then it has stayed within a narrow range of 1,824–1,967 places. In 2025/26 there were 1,888, 47% more than in 2012/13, and in 2026/27 — 1,838. Over the same period the Polish-language part grew much faster, from 3,914 to 8,892 places, more than doubling. That is why the share of programmes in a foreign language is falling: it was highest in 2014/15 (26.3%) and today stands at 17.1%.

Over 15 intakes the regulations contained a total of 26,104 places in a foreign language. These are places in the limits, not the number of students admitted or doctors trained: the limit sets the upper bound on admissions, and it is not known to what extent universities fill it.

We count the limit in its final version: the regulation together with the amendments issued in the same admission round. In five years amendments added places in Polish-language programmes, most in 2023/24 (from 9,804 to 10,289) and in 2024/25 (from 9,882 to 10,065); none of them changed the foreign-language column.[^limity] The final versions for these two years were also given by the Ministry of Science and Higher Education in its reply to a parliamentary question (*interpelacja*).[^mnisw] The same table is used in our analysis of [cuts to admission limits for medicine](/en/analysis/cutting-medical-school-places/).

## Seventeen universities, the most places in Poznań

In 2026/27, 17 universities are admitting students to medicine taught in a foreign language. The largest pool is at Poznan University of Medical Sciences, ahead of Lublin and the Medical University of Warsaw.

A year earlier the pool was 50 places larger. It was reduced most by the Silesian Academy (from 130 to 60 places) and Andrzej Frycz Modrzewski Krakow University (from 80 to 40). It was increased above all by Lublin (from 170 to 200), the Medical University of Gdańsk (from 50 to 72), and Poznań and the Medical University of Warsaw (by 20 places each).[^lim2025]

## How many graduates then work in Poland

Graduates’ careers are tracked by a state system, Economic Careers of Graduates (*Ekonomiczne Losy Absolwentów*, ELA). It links diploma data from the POL-on higher education information system with the records of the Social Insurance Institution (ZUS), so it sees everyone who, after graduating, works in Poland as an employee or runs a business. A graduate who works only abroad does not appear in these records.[^ela-metoda]

According to the 8th round of ELA, in 2021 foreigners accounted for more than 17% of graduates of medical programmes, i.e. future doctors and dentists. Of foreign graduates of long-cycle master’s programmes (*jednolite studia magisterskie*) in medical and health sciences, fewer than 1% were working in Poland one year after graduation.[^ela]

<Aside title="What this indicator does not tell us">

- **Who stays for good.** The indicator shows work in Poland one year after graduation. It does not say who comes back later or where the others went.
- **How English Division graduates fare.** Graduates are grouped by citizenship, not by the language of their studies. The indicator therefore also covers foreigners who studied in Polish, and leaves out Poles from programmes in a foreign language.
- **How medicine alone fares.** It covers the whole field of medical and health sciences and a single cohort — 2021 graduates.

</Aside>

Who are these students? The largest group of foreigners studying medicine comes from Norway (17.5%), followed by India, Germany, Ireland, Taiwan and Sweden — these six countries account for 51.7% of the total.[^radon] Norway funds its citizens’ studies abroad.[^konkret24] Graduates of such programmes may take the Medical Final Examination (*Lekarski Egzamin Końcowy*, LEK) in the language in which they studied — as provided by the Act on the professions of physician and dentist, as worded by a 2020 amendment.[^ustawa2020]

## How to read the total limit

The total limit — 10,730 places in 2026/27 — includes the foreign-language pool. Programmes in Polish have 8,892 places.[^lim2026] When the question is how many doctors will join the Polish system, the second number is a better starting point, because only a fraction of foreign graduates work in Poland.

This is also how the authors of forecasts of a doctor surplus count. An analysis published by the Supreme Medical Chamber (NIL) in June 2024, according to which “we are heading towards a significant surplus of doctors” (our translation), was based on about 7,800 places in Polish-language programmes.[^nil] The Ministry of Health, writing about the forecast surplus of doctors by 2034, gives the number of graduates of Polish nationality: 4,755 in 2023/24.[^mz] Anyone who sets the total limit against such forecasts should therefore subtract the foreign-language pool from it — otherwise they are comparing two different quantities.

The split by language, however, is not a split by citizenship. About 9% of students in a foreign language are Poles, and foreigners also study in Polish-language programmes — in 2026/27 the limit provides 107 separate places for them.[^lim2026] How the limits translate into the number of doctors over the coming decades is shown by the [doctor supply simulator](/analizy/symulator-podazy-lekarzy/) (in Polish).

## What the degree costs

Programmes in English are fee-paying from the first year. Universities publish tuition fees on their websites, usually separately for each intake and in advance for the whole programme.

## How much money this means for universities

How much universities earn from programmes in English cannot be seen in public financial reports. One example: in Wroclaw Medical University’s report on the execution of its material and financial plan for 2020, tuition fees fall under a single item — fees for educational services. They amounted to <Money value={69743400} scale="million" /> out of <Money value={301165600} scale="million" /> of operating revenue, or about 23%.[^umw]

This item, however, covers all fee-paying programmes, including part-time programmes in Polish and postgraduate studies, so it is not known how much of it is tuition for programmes in English. It is the most recent report from this university with a breakdown of revenue that we have found.

### A nationwide estimate

About 6,300 foreigners study medicine — 6.0% of the 105,090 foreigners studying in Poland (the report counts each programme studied separately), according to 2024 data.[^radon] If each of them paid the tuition for programmes in English, from <Money value={ESTIMATE.feeLow} /> (Gdańsk) to <Money value={ESTIMATE.feeHigh} /> (Poznań) a year, universities would collect from <Money value={estimateTotal(ESTIMATE.feeLow)} scale="billion" decimals={2} /> to <Money value={estimateTotal(ESTIMATE.feeHigh)} scale="billion" decimals={2} /> a year.

This assumption inflates the result. Some foreigners study in Polish, and there the university may, but does not have to, charge them fees; some groups, including citizens of the European Union and people with permanent residence, are exempt.[^konkret24] On the other hand, the result is lowered by Poles, who make up about 9% of students in a foreign language and also pay, and by higher fees in later years at some universities. The result is therefore an order of magnitude — about <Money value={400000000} scale="billion" /> a year — and not an amount from financial reports, because nobody publishes such an amount.

## How much universities pay per hour of teaching in English

Programmes in English also affect the pay of teaching staff. For a teaching hour in a programme in English universities pay more than for comparable classes in Polish, and they set the rates in rectors’ orders (*zarządzenia rektora*) and pay regulations.

At the Medical University of Warsaw (WUM) both rates — for classes in English-language programmes and in part-time programmes in Polish — are set by the same rules for awarding additional pay from the university’s own revenue, which include tuition fees: the first in annex 1, the second in annex 2.[^wum] This is the same category of pay — an allowance for teaching in fee-paying programmes — which is why we compare the two. We do not compare them with the rate for an overtime hour in full-time programmes in Polish, because that is a different kind of pay, which depends on the post.

## What is public and what is not

Programmes in English are a rare case in which the money around the medical profession is visible almost in full. The number of places at each university is announced in a regulation, the price by the university, and the hourly teaching rate in an order or pay regulations. One figure is missing: how much universities earn from these programmes in total.
