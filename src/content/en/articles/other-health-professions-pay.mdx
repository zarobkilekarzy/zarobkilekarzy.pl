Doctors are only part of the system. The largest occupational group in healthcare is nurses and midwives, and alongside them work paramedics, laboratory diagnosticians, physiotherapists, pharmacists and medical caregivers. Their salaried pay is set by a single statutory scale — and that is an important point of reference, because this part of pay is known.

<Aside variant="info" title="This scale is public">

The minimum base pay of employees working under employment contracts in healthcare providers is set by the act of 8 June 2017.[^ustawa] Every year on 1 July the minimum for each group is its coefficient multiplied by the base amount, i.e. the average pay in the economy in the previous year according to Statistics Poland (GUS) — from {BASE.from} <Money value={BASE.amount} />. This is what distinguishes this part of the system from B2B contracts, under which most specialist doctors work and whose amounts nobody publishes (see [Contract or employment](/en/mechanisms/contract-vs-employment/)).

</Aside>

## The pay ladder from 1 July 2026

Where the individual professions stand in the scale. The amounts are the statutory minimum base pay for employees — excluding on-call duty, overtime and allowances for length of service, night work or a function.[^siatka] Actual payments are usually higher.

<PayScale source={{ label: 'Rynek Zdrowia — the full scale of 10 groups', href: LINKS.siatka }} />

The coefficients come from the annex to the act;[^ustawa] the amounts for all groups are also confirmed by LEX.[^lex] Nurses and midwives fall into group 2, 5 or 6 depending on education and specialisation, explains the self-government of nurses and midwives.[^nipip-grupy] The minimums for these groups were reported by Rzeczpospolita.[^rzeczpospolita]

## Nurses and midwives — the largest group

This is the largest profession in the system[^nipip-rejestr] and at the same time the oldest demographically.[^emerytalny] The amounts in the scale need to be read in this context.

The average age of a nurse today is about 55.[^bankier] The professional self-government forecasts that in 2023–2030 the number of nurses and midwives will fall by more than 26,000, because fewer people enter the profession than leave it.[^nipip-2030]

A fair counterpoint: in OECD statistics[^oecd] Polish nurses earn about 160% of the average wage in the country, which is relatively high.[^gazeta-prawna] This, however, is relative to the low Polish average — the same one we discuss for doctors in the analysis [Doctors’ pay: Poland compared with its neighbours](/en/analysis/doctor-salaries-poland-vs-neighbours/).

## Outside employment: nurses’ contracts

Like doctors, some nurses work under contracts. And as with doctors, these rates are covered neither by the act nor by any public register. The amounts below are offers from job advertisements and estimates by industry portals, not payments.[^stawki-pielegniarek][^anestezjologiczna]

Nobody publishes contract amounts — and this applies to all staff, not only doctors. Transparency of pay from public funds should cover everyone paid from those funds. That is the core of [our proposal](/en/demand/).

<Aside title="The market is not the scale">

Market medians mix the public and private sectors and people working outside employment, and the samples are small. That is why they are sometimes lower than the statutory minimum base pay in a healthcare provider. This is not a contradiction, just two different measurements. GUS does not publish average pay broken down by these professions, so the statutory scale remains the firm point of reference. We do not publish figures “from memory”: a field without a source is marked with a dash.

</Aside>
