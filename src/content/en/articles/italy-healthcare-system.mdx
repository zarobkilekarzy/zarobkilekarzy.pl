The Italian health service is universal, financed from taxes and free of charge in hospital, and yet almost one resident in ten goes without a needed visit or test in the course of a year. We describe how it works according to Italian acts, data from the statistical office and reports to parliament, and check what is public in it. Italy has regulated what in Poland remains outside the statistics: doctors’ private practice in public hospitals. It is known how many doctors run one and how much patients pay for it. How much an individual doctor earns from it, the reports do not show.

## One health service, twenty-one systems

The National Health Service (*Servizio Sanitario Nazionale*) covers all residents. The state determines which services everyone is entitled to and distributes the money, while care is organised by 19 regions and two autonomous provinces through local health authorities. Treatment is provided by public hospitals and accredited private ones, in proportions that differ in each region.[^profil]

The level of financing is decided every year by the budget act. The act for 2026 raised it by €2.38 billion.[^budzet] In 2025, €44.7 billion went on staff pay.[^rgs]

## What it costs

<CountryBars title="Current health expenditure in 2024 (Eurostat)" codes={['IT', 'PL', 'EU']} keys={['healthGdp', 'perCapitaEur', 'perCapitaPps', 'publicShare', 'oopShare']} />

Italy spends a smaller share of national income on health than the EU average, and the share of out-of-pocket spending is higher than in Poland and than the EU average.[^eurostat-sha] According to the profile by the OECD and the European Commission, official co-payments for visits and medicines are only a small part of this amount — most of it is services bought privately, including dental care.[^profil]

## What the patient pays and what they go without

A stay in hospital and a visit to a family doctor are free of charge. For visits to specialists and for tests the patient pays a co-payment (*ticket*), the amount of which is set by the regions; some patients are exempt from it. It is the family doctor who refers the patient to a specialist.[^profil]

Every year the statistical office asks who went without a needed visit or test. In 2025 it was 9.6% of residents, more than 5.6 million people — the same as a year earlier. Waiting lists were given as the reason by 6.5% of respondents, cost by 5.2%.[^istat-bes]

This figure is higher than in the Eurostat survey, according to which 2.5% of Italians go without a visit because of cost, distance or waiting time (2.9% in Poland).[^eurostat-silc] The questions and methods of the two surveys differ, so the results cannot be set side by side directly.

## Waiting lists under state supervision

In 2024 the government created by decree a national waiting list platform, run by the agency AGENAS. It collects data from the regional booking systems and e-prescriptions, and the agency can inspect the regions.[^dl73] A public dashboard shows the number of bookings and the average waiting time by type of service and degree of urgency.[^agenas] According to the GIMBE foundation, at the beginning of February 2026 the public version showed only aggregate data for the whole country — without a breakdown by region, health authority or hospital.[^gimbe-pnla] Since May 2026 the agency has also published the results of the regions, and its first bulletin compares the regions with one another.[^agenas-boll]

## Doctors’ work and pay

Hospital doctors are employees of public health authorities and have the status of managerial staff. Their pay is set by a national collective agreement: basic pay is €50,005.77 a year, on top of which come a professional allowance of €9,466 and a position allowance — at least from €6,798 to €9,100, depending on length of service, which must be at least five years.[^ccnl] Family doctors are not employees of the health service but its independent contractors.[^profil]

According to the OECD, a salaried specialist in Italy earns 2.79 times as much as the average worker (2024) — almost the same as in Poland (2.86 times), where, however, this measure covers employment contracts only.[^oecd-remun]

Hospitals filled staffing gaps with doctors hired from cooperatives and companies and paid per on-call shift. According to the anti-corruption authority ANAC, in 2019–2023 they spent €1.7 billion on external staff.[^anac-2024] Since 2023 the rules have allowed such procurement only in case of necessity, on a one-off basis and for no more than 12 months, and a breach makes the director financially liable.[^dl34] In 2024–2025 the value of the tenders announced was €1.06 billion, of which about €81 million was for staff explicitly described as medical doctors — most tenders have a general description that does not show whom they concern.[^anac-2026]

## Private practice in a public hospital

A doctor employed in a public hospital may see private patients there after hours (*attività libero-professionale intramuraria*). The patient pays the hospital according to a price list, and the hospital pays the doctor their share. The act sets conditions: private services may not in total exceed those performed under the employment contract, the hospital is to monitor this, and 5% of the doctor’s fee is deducted for shortening waiting lists. A director who fails to ensure this can have their bonus reduced or be dismissed.[^alpi-ustawa]

Every year the minister of health submits a report on this activity to parliament.[^alpi-ustawa] [^alpi]

The report does not split the payments between doctors and support staff, nor does it state how much an individual doctor received.[^alpi]

In Poland a doctor sees private patients outside the hospital, and nobody sets this against their work paid for by the National Health Fund (NFZ) ([how it works](/en/analysis/private-visits-queues-and-prices/)). The Italian model brings private practice into the hospital — which makes it possible to count it and to limit it.

## What is public

**Doctors’ private practice — in aggregate and every year.** The number of doctors, revenue, payments and the balance for each region are in the report to parliament.[^alpi]

**The pay of heads — by name, with information about private practice.** The transparency act requires health authorities to publish the CVs and pay of directors and of heads of department and of other units, as well as data on their other professional activity — in which the provision expressly includes private practice in the hospital.[^dlgs33] In 2019 the Constitutional Court found the obligation to publish the pay of managerial staff compatible with the constitution, and struck down only the requirement that all managers disclose their assets.[^corte] Doctors who do not head a unit are not covered by the obligation. We did not check whether facilities comply with it in full, or whether they state the amount from private practice when doing so.

**Pay tables.** The national collective agreement with the pay amounts is public.[^ccnl]

**Waiting lists — for the country and the regions.** The national platform shows waiting times by type of service and urgency.[^agenas] At the beginning of February 2026 it did not break these data down by region,[^gimbe-pnla] and since May 2026 the agency has also published the results of the regions.[^agenas-boll]

**Procurement of external staff.** The anti-corruption authority publishes analyses of procurement based on the national database of public contracts.[^anac-2026]

Nobody publishes a doctor’s total income — from the employment contract and from private practice. An act of 2022 provides for a public register of the benefits that pharmaceutical companies and manufacturers of medical devices give to doctors;[^sunshine] we found no confirmation that the register has been launched.

## How many doctors there are

Eurostat gives 575 doctors working with patients per 100,000 inhabitants for Italy — the most among the countries described. However, the Italian series has a break, and the description of the method does not settle exactly whom it covers, so we do not compare this figure directly with others.[^eurostat-lekarze]

Care outside hospitals was to be strengthened by local health centres financed from the EU recovery plan. According to data from the regions collected by the GIMBE foundation, at the end of 2025, of the 1,715 centres planned, 66 were fully operational — with doctors and nurses.[^pnrr]

## Satisfaction and outcomes

<CountryBars title="Italy and Poland in opinion surveys and in statistics" codes={['IT', 'PL']} keys={['satOecd', 'satIpsos', 'treatable', 'lifeExp']} />

Italy’s health outcomes are among the best in the EU — the EU average is 86.8 deaths from treatable causes per 100,000 inhabitants and 81.5 years of life — although in the Gallup poll fewer people there than in Poland are satisfied with the availability of good care.[^wyniki] We write about the opinion surveys [separately](/en/analysis/satisfaction-with-healthcare-in-poland/).

## Professional and criminal liability

**Professional.** Professional misconduct is ruled on by the medical chambers, and appeals are heard by a central commission at the ministry of health.[^ordini] We found no annual statistics on proceedings and penalties.

**Criminal.** Italy has for years been limiting doctors’ criminal liability. An act of 2017 added to the criminal code a provision under which a doctor is not punishable for a patient’s death or bodily injury caused by lack of skill if they followed the guidelines appropriate to the case.[^gelli-karna] Since 2024 there has also been a temporary provision, extended every year and valid until the end of 2026: where there are serious staff shortages, a doctor is criminally liable only for gross negligence.[^scudo]

We found no current statistics on criminal proceedings against doctors. The only official data come from a parliamentary committee’s report for 2009–2010: in the prosecutors’ offices examined, of the closed cases of unintentional bodily injury against medical staff, 98.8% (237 of 240) were discontinued, and two ended in a conviction. This is a sample from more than a decade ago, not annual statistics.[^komisja]

**Compensation.** The same act of 2017 divides civil liability: the facility is liable to the patient in contract, and the doctor — only if the patient proves the doctor’s fault. Facilities must be insured and make public with whom; every doctor must have a policy covering gross negligence.[^gelli-cywilna] A ministerial decree of 2023 sets the minimum sums insured: from €1 million to €5 million for facilities and from €1 million to €2 million for doctors.[^dm232] How systems of payment without establishing fault work is described in the text on [compensation without establishing fault](/en/mechanisms/no-fault-compensation-and-doctors-liability/).

## Method

We base the description of the system on the texts of Italian acts from the official Normattiva service, the report of the minister of health to parliament on doctors’ private practice for 2023, the report of the Ragioneria Generale dello Stato on health spending, data from the statistical office ISTAT, dossiers of the Chamber of Deputies on the budget acts, the national collective agreement, reports of the anti-corruption authority ANAC, Eurostat data and the country profile prepared by the OECD and the European Observatory on Health Systems and Policies for the European Commission. Secondary sources — studies by the GIMBE foundation and the press — are marked in the footnotes. The website of the Italian ministry of health does not allow automated access, so we did not check some of the data, including the statistical yearbook of the health service.

Italian sources give health spending in several presentations (public spending according to the national accounts, financing of the health service, total spending according to ISTAT) — for comparisons with Poland we use Eurostat only.
