Belgium is one of the countries whose inhabitants rate access to treatment most highly: a specialist can be seen without a referral, and one person in a thousand gives up a visit because of a waiting list. The system rests on compulsory insurance, a public fee schedule and a fee for every service. We describe how it works according to Belgian official data, what it costs and what is public in it. The patient receives a statement for every visit, and for every doctor it is known whether they keep to the official tariffs. How much public money goes to an individual doctor — that we found in no public source.

## Seven funds and one insurer

Compulsory health insurance covers 99% of inhabitants. Every insured person belongs to one of seven funds: five national associations of not-for-profit sickness funds (*mutualités*, *ziekenfondsen*), the railway fund or the public fund.[^profil]

The funds set neither prices nor the scope of benefits. The money — mainly contributions proportional to income — is collected by the federal insurance institute INAMI (RIZIV in Dutch), which allocates each fund a budget in advance.[^profil] The insurance budget for 2025 provided for €45.22 billion of revenue. 84.7% was to come from the common social security fund — including 17.8% of all revenue that is tax money transferred there as alternative financing — and the contributions collected directly by health insurance, mainly from pensions, were to yield 4.0%.[^wplywy] The federal state is responsible for the insurance, the hospital budget and the regulation of the professions. The regions and language communities — among other things for the organisation of primary care, care for older people and prevention.[^profil]

For the reimbursement of services INAMI has €40.986 billion in 2026, 3.2% more than a year earlier. In 2025 it spent €201.2 million less than the budget target — €39.712 billion.[^budzet] The law limits the real growth of the budget: to 2.5% in 2025 and to 2% in 2026.[^norma]

## What it costs

Belgium spends clearly more on health than Poland — relative to GDP and per inhabitant. However, the part that patients pay out of pocket is also larger there than in Poland.[^eurostat-sha]

<CountryBars title="Current health expenditure in 2024 (Eurostat)" codes={['BE', 'PL', 'EU']} keys={['healthGdp', 'perCapitaEur', 'perCapitaPps', 'publicShare', 'oopShare']} />

According to the country profile prepared by the OECD and the European Commission, in 2023 the insurance covered about 88% of the cost of hospital treatment, but only about 63% of outpatient treatment. Out-of-pocket spending is partly driven by supplements to doctors’ fees — in outpatient clinics and in hospitals.[^profil]

## A fee for every service and the tariff agreement

Doctors are paid mainly for every service performed.[^profil] The rates are contained in the national fee schedule (*nomenclature*) — more than 8000 items — which INAMI publishes.[^profil] [^taryfa] The level of fees is set every two years by an agreement between the funds and the doctors’ organisations. A doctor may join it or not, and this determines how much they are allowed to charge the patient:[^wyszukiwarka]

- **in the agreement** (*conventionné*) — applies the official tariffs, without supplements,
- **partly in the agreement** — applies them only during specified hours,
- **outside the agreement** — sets fees freely.

85.72% of doctors joined the agreement for 2026–2027.[^przystapienie] The average hides large differences between specialties. According to the INAMI report for 2024, 96% of anaesthesiologists and 88% of family doctors keep to the official tariffs, but only 30% of ophthalmologists and 23% of dermatologists.[^hpr]

In 2021, 87% of visits to family doctors took place with doctors in the agreement, but fewer than half of outpatient visits to specialists.[^profil]

## The hospital: fees are the largest part of revenue

Two thirds of the beds in acute hospitals belong to private not-for-profit institutions.[^profil] A hospital receives a lump sum for its operations from the federal budget (*Budget des moyens financiers*), but that is not its main source of money. In 2024, in 80 general hospitals, the largest part of turnover — 36.5% — was doctors’ fees, the budget lump sum was 34.8% and pharmaceutical products 20.6%. Together the hospitals closed the year with an operating loss of €29 million.[^maha]

Since 2019 some stays have been billed differently. For simple, predictable procedures (*soins à basse variabilité*), the fees of all doctors for the whole stay are one amount set in advance, the same in every hospital.[^basse] In 2024 this came to €560 million, or 2.6% of hospital revenue.[^maha]

Hospital doctors are mostly self-employed: they charge the patient and the fund their own fee and hand part of that money over to the hospital for costs. The law provides for this, but each hospital negotiates the size of the deduction separately with its doctors.[^kce]

## What the patient pays

For a visit to a doctor in the agreement the patient pays the official fee, and the fund reimburses most of it. Since 1 January 2026 a visit to an accredited family doctor has cost €33.74. A patient whose records are kept by that doctor (*dossier médical global*) pays €4 of this themselves, and without it — €6. A visit to an accredited specialist costs the same, and the co-payment is €12. People on low incomes, entitled to increased reimbursement, pay €1–1.50 at a family doctor and €3 at a specialist.[^taryfa]

The annual total of official co-payments is capped by a limit that depends on family income (*maximum à facturer*): in 2026 from €270.60 for the lowest incomes to €2,194.20 for the highest. Once it is exceeded, the fund reimburses co-payments in full.[^maf] The limit does not, however, cover supplements to fees above the tariff.[^profil]

The patient usually pays the doctor the whole fee and gets a refund from the fund. People entitled to increased reimbursement pay only their own share at a family doctor — since 2015 the doctor has had to settle the rest directly with the fund in their case.[^tiers]

## Fee supplements

Supplements above the official tariff (*suppléments d'honoraires*) are a separate item. In hospital a doctor may charge them only to a patient who has chosen a single room — never in a double or shared room.[^doplaty-zasady] Outside hospital, since 2025 they may not be charged to people entitled to increased reimbursement, including by doctors outside the agreement.[^doplaty-bim] According to the OECD and European Commission profile, a supplement can reach four times the official tariff, and in outpatient care there is a lack of transparency about their level.[^profil]

According to the barometer of the sickness funds’ agency, in 2024 patients paid €1.60 billion for hospital stays, of which €760 million was supplements to doctors’ fees — 9.1% more than a year earlier. 57% of these costs were linked to a single room.[^aim] Belgians take out voluntary hospital insurance to cover such expenses; voluntary schemes financed 4.83% of health spending in 2024.[^eurostat-sha]

Limiting the supplements was the subject of a dispute: according to the Belgian public broadcaster, on 7 July 2025 some doctors responded to the draft limits with a strike, the first in more than twenty years.[^strajk] The framework act of 30 May 2026 contains no specific figures. It states that the supplements will be limited by 1 January 2028 at the latest — the government may move this deadline to 1 January 2029 — and that the limit, as an amount or a percentage, will be set by a royal decree at the request of the joint committee of the funds and the providers or, if the committee does not reach agreement, of the minister.[^ustawa-ramowa]

## Doctors’ work and pay

Belgian medicine rests on independent practice: family doctors work on their own account, increasingly in group practices, and specialists — in hospitals and in their own surgeries.[^profil] That is why the most telling measure of earnings is the OECD one, which compares a doctor’s income with the average wage in the country.

A salaried specialist in Belgium earns 1.95 times as much as the average employee — in Poland 2.86 times.[^oecd-remun] The Polish figure, however, covers only employment contracts, and most Polish specialists work on civil-law (B2B) contracts, which the OECD does not see ([comparison with the neighbours](/en/analysis/doctor-salaries-poland-vs-neighbours/)).

We found no official statistics on the income of Belgian doctors. INAMI publishes something else: how much the insurance paid on average for the services of one doctor of a given specialty, converted to full-time work. In 2024 this was €228,196 for a family doctor, €201,934 for a dermatologist, €607,359 for a cardiologist and €1,127,353 for a radiologist.[^hpr] INAMI cautions that these amounts cannot be equated with income: they include neither patients’ co-payments nor supplements above the tariff, and hospital doctors hand over part of the money for equipment and premises.[^hpr-metoda]

## What is public

The Belgian system shows a good deal — to the patient, to the public and to the insurer itself.

**The tariff status of every doctor.** In the INAMI search tool a doctor can be found by name or number, and one can check whether they apply the official tariffs always, during specified hours, or not at all.[^wyszukiwarka]

**A statement for every visit.** Since 1 July 2015 the law has required that the patient be given a statement (*document justificatif*), among other cases when the doctor bills the fund electronically. For each service the document gives the official fee, the amount paid by the patient and the amount billed to the fund — also when the patient pays nothing.[^justificatif] In Poland the patient will find similar data in the Internet Patient Account ([how to check it](/en/mechanisms/visits-that-never-happened/)), but does not receive them at the visit.

**Payments by specialty.** The INAMI report on the health professions gives, for each specialty, the number of doctors, the share in the tariff agreement and the reimbursement amounts per doctor. Groups smaller than five full-time equivalents are hidden so that no individual can be identified.[^hpr]

**Benefits from industry.** The betransparent.be register, operating under the act of 18 December 2016, shows by name what pharmaceutical companies and manufacturers of medical devices have transferred to doctors and organisations. 694 companies reported data for 2025.[^betransparent]

**Hospital finances — only in aggregate.** All general hospitals submit financial reports to the ministry in a single format. What is publicly available is an annual aggregate study of them, prepared by the bank Belfius.[^maha]

The total amount the insurance paid for the services of an individual doctor — under a name or under a pseudonym — we found in no public source.

## Oversight: the state sees the doctor, the public sees the statistics

Every doctor bills under their own INAMI number, so the insurer can add up their services from all places of work and compare them with others. The INAMI inspection service makes use of this. In an analysis of the urgency supplements that family doctors added to visits in 2022, half of the doctors billed at most €980 of them. 205 doctors — 1.6% of the 12,130 active ones — billed from €10,000 to €253,747; this group accounted for about 19% of all such supplements. The service sent letters to some of them, and to 23 also a questionnaire. Only an aggregate description is publicly known.[^secm]

In 2025 the service closed 334 inspections and detected undue billing of €15.87 million, of which €4.34 million in 47 cases classified as fraud. Doctors accounted for 46 inspections and €2.58 million.[^secm] This is the result of selected inspections, not a measure of the scale of abuse in the whole system — as in Poland ([what inspections by the National Health Fund (NFZ) detect](/en/mechanisms/nfz-inspection-koszalin/)).

In Poland nobody compiles how much one doctor received in total from public funds across all institutions ([more about this mechanism](/en/mechanisms/multiple-jobs-parallel-contracts/)). The Belgian insurer has such a total, because it pays the doctor directly, under one number.

## How many doctors there are and how long people wait

Belgium has fewer doctors working with patients than Poland: in 2024, 334 per 100,000 inhabitants against 386 in Poland.[^eurostat-lekarze] Almost twice as many are licensed to practise in Belgium (660 per 100,000), so in comparisons one has to check which of these figures is being quoted.[^eurostat-lekarze] According to INAMI, at the end of 2025 there were 57,556 licensed doctors, and 41,002 were actually practising.[^inami-lekarze] The number of new doctors is restricted by a quota set by royal decree separately for Flanders and for the French-speaking part. For 2032–2033 it is 1,427 people a year in Flanders and 1,089 in the French Community.[^limit]

No referral is needed to see a specialist, and the doctor is chosen freely.[^profil] In 2025, 0.9% of Belgians gave up a visit because of cost, distance or a waiting list — in Poland 2.9%, on average in the EU 2.4%. The waiting list alone was the reason for 0.1% of Belgians and 2.2% of Poles; cost — for 0.8% of Belgians and 0.5% of Poles.[^eurostat-silc]

Belgium has no official measurement of waiting times — in 2026 the observatory for chronic diseases attached to INAMI backed the creation of a national model for such a measurement.[^pomiar] In a survey by the consumer organisation Test-Achats, cited in parliament in 2025, the average wait for a visit to a specialist was 81 days, to an ophthalmologist 131 and to a dermatologist 114.[^czekanie] These are the same specialties in which the fewest doctors keep to the official tariffs — a coincidence, not proof of a link.

## Professional and criminal liability

**Professional.** Doctors’ misconduct is ruled on by the professional self-governing body (*Ordre des médecins*): provincial councils at first instance and appeal councils, whose decisions can be challenged in cassation. The penalties are a warning, two degrees of reprimand, suspension of the licence to practise for up to two years and removal from the register.[^ordre-tryb]

The proceedings are closed to the patient. The provincial councils sit in private, and the person who lodged the complaint is not a party: they are not informed of the decision, have no access to the file and cannot appeal.[^ordre-tryb] This is confirmed by a 2025 opinion of the observatory for chronic diseases attached to INAMI, which calls for complainants to be informed and for annual reports to be published.[^obserwatorium] We found no public statistics on the body’s complaints and penalties. In March 2026 the body presented its own proposal for a reform of disciplinary proceedings.[^ordre]

**Criminal.** The Belgian criminal code has no separate offence of medical error — a doctor answers for causing death or bodily injury by negligence.[^kodeks-be] A new criminal code entered into force on 1 September 2026. For unintentional acts it requires “serious fault”: causing death through a “serious lack of foresight or precaution” is punishable, among other penalties by six months to three years in prison.[^kodeks-be] According to the Belgian trade press this changes the threshold: until now the slightest fault was enough for a conviction.[^kodeks-be-prasa] We found no statistics on criminal proceedings against doctors.

**Compensation.** The Medical Accidents Fund (*Fonds des accidents médicaux*), established by the act of 31 March 2010, operates within INAMI. An injured patient can ask it for an opinion on their case. In 2025 the fund received 815 applications, almost twice as many as in 2022 (441). The fund issued 548 opinions: in 73 cases it found an accident without anyone’s liability, for which it pays compensation itself, in 66 the liability of the provider, and it considered 409 cases not eligible for compensation. More than €30 million was paid to injured patients, and 80% of the applications lodged in 2023 were handled in 12.8 months on average.[^fam] How such arrangements work in other countries is described in the text on [compensation without establishing fault](/en/mechanisms/no-fault-compensation-and-doctors-liability/).
