A drug programme (*program lekowy*) is treatment with an expensive medicine, usually a biological or cancer drug, which the National Health Fund (NFZ) finances in a hospital under strictly defined criteria. In 2025 the NFZ settled **<Money value={15530000000} scale="billion" decimals={2} />** in drug programmes, and 337,571 people were treated in them.[^mz19050] At the end of September 2026 hospitals reported to the NFZ **11,935 people** on waiting lists for 141 programmes.[^nfz-pliki]

## A right to treatment that has a limit

A drug programme is a guaranteed service: anyone who meets the criteria has a right to treatment.[^ustawa] The Ministry of Health stresses that qualification is to be purely medical and that restrictions resulting from a hospital’s financial situation are unacceptable.[^mz19050] At the same time drug programmes “are classed as limited services” (our translation): a hospital is paid up to the value of its contract with the NFZ, and for treatment above it, known as overperformance (*nadwykonania*), the NFZ pays later, when it has the money.[^mz13697]

The ministry explains that it cannot raise contract values to match current needs, because there must first be enough money for services without a limit and for the treatment of children.[^mz13697] And needs are growing fast. For the medicines alone the NFZ paid **<Money value={13950000000} scale="billion" decimals={2} />** in drug programmes in 2025, more than four times as much as in 2017, while the number of people treated almost tripled over that time.[^api-nfz]

According to hospitals’ accounts cited by the Commissioner for Human Rights, an annual contract often runs out after about half a year, and from then on the hospital buys medicines on credit and waits for the NFZ to pay for overperformance.[^rpo] This is an account, not a measurement. The scale of the arrears is shown by the ministry’s data, described at the end of this text.

In practice a patient goes through several stages and may wait at each of them.

The time needed to conclude contracts was given by the ministry at a meeting of a Sejm subcommittee.[^stenogram]

For new programmes this second stage is to stop delaying treatment. On 19 November 2026 the Sejm passed an amendment to the Reimbursement Act: **417 deputies voted in favour, none against**, and 17 abstained.[^sejm-glosowanie] When a new drug programme is created for a medicine with no reimbursed equivalent, the minister will be able to announce it in the reimbursement list one edition earlier, and the president of the NFZ is to sign contracts with hospitals in that time so that treatment can start **on the first day the reimbursement decision applies**.[^nowela] According to the explanatory memorandum to the bill, that leaves about three months to conclude the contracts. The provision is to apply from 1 January 2027, and the act now goes to the Senate.

The change concerns new programmes only. It does not change contract limits or waiting lists in programmes that are already running. The rest of this text is about the last stage, because it is the only one measured every month.

## How many patients are waiting

Every month the NFZ publishes files with the number of people on waiting lists in each hospital unit, that is, a ward or an outpatient clinic, and the average waiting time.[^nfz-pliki] The act requires it.[^ustawa] Every patient on a list has a medical category: stable or urgent. The list does not include people who are already being treated and are continuing therapy, or those the hospital did not enter at all.

<Aside variant="warn" title="One hospital reports almost a quarter of the list">
The Multi-Specialty Voivodeship Hospital in Gorzów Wielkopolski reported **2,798 people waiting** in September, including 866 in the programme for the treatment of uveitis. In the whole country **176 people** were treated in that programme in 2025,[^api-nfz] and this hospital’s contract for uveitis for 2026 is for symbolic amounts: <Money value={628.56} /> for services and <Money value={12} /> for medicines a year,[^umowy] and yet the hospital reported 61 people admitted in September. This may be a reporting error, or a waiting list with an almost empty contract. We do not know which version is true. It is a matter to be explained, not an allegation. That is why we give the figures with and without this hospital.
</Aside>

Without this hospital there were **9,137 people** on the list, including 1,966 in the urgent category. In September 4,531 people from the lists started treatment.[^nfz-pliki] Earlier estimates based on NFZ data spoke of “about 9,000” in March and more than 9,400 at the end of April 2026.[^rpo][^pap] We do not know whether they were counted in the same way, so we do not compare them directly.

## A few weeks on average

People admitted to programmes in September had waited **33.2 days** on average, or 39.8 days including the hospital in Gorzów.[^nfz-pliki] Without that hospital, half of those admitted went to units where the average wait did not exceed 17 days, and 90% to units where it did not exceed 79 days. Units with an average of more than half a year took 2.8% of those admitted.

The average has one flaw: it counts only those who have already been admitted. A list may be long but move quickly, or shorter and stand still. That is why the number of people waiting has to be read alongside the number admitted.

## The address matters

A better picture than the NFZ list comes from the programmes’ own register (SMPT), which shows when a patient was qualified and whether they received their first dose. The Polish Society for Rheumatology (PTR) calculated from it that of 5,027 people qualified for treatment in rheumatology from January to September 2026, **1,166**, or 23%, had not received their first dose.[^ptr] The PTR considers this a minimum figure, because it does not include patients whom centres did not qualify because of financial difficulties.

Among those qualified in January, 59 people had not received their first dose by 19 October, that is, for at least 261 days.[^ptr] Money is not always the reason: the ministry also points to clinical considerations and the decisions of patients themselves.[^mz19419]

For a patient this is not only a statistic. According to the European EULAR recommendations, rheumatoid arthritis is treated first with methotrexate, and if it does not work well enough within 3–6 months, patients with poor prognostic factors are given a biological or similar drug in addition.[^eular] Months of waiting for the first dose lengthen the period in which, according to these recommendations, the treatment should already have been changed.

Individual hospitals show how the limit works in practice. At University Hospital No. 2 in Bydgoszcz, qualification for biological treatment in rheumatology was suspended in February.[^medexpress] In the summer the hospital’s needs were estimated at <Money value={170000000} scale="million" decimals={0} /> against <Money value={127000000} scale="million" decimals={0} /> in the contract, and the NFZ branch replied that it could not raise the limit.[^money] By October the value of the hospital’s contracts for programmes had risen to about <Money value={149000000} scale="million" decimals={0} />,[^umowy] but in September the hospital still reported 126 people waiting and 5 admitted.[^nfz-pliki] At the Dietl Hospital in Kraków 166 people were waiting in September, 160 of them in the urgent category, and one was admitted.[^nfz-pliki] According to an account from July, 120 qualified patients were waiting there at the time, and for 8–9 months modern treatment had been restricted to cases where life was at risk.[^dietl]

## Oncology and hospitals that say something else

Of the 142 drug programmes, 41 are oncology programmes.[^mz19050] In July the daily <span lang="pl">Rzeczpospolita</span> described a letter in which the Central Clinical Hospital of the Medical University of Łódź set the start of treatment for a patient with breast cancer for the first quarter of 2027, because it had no means of financing it.[^rz-lodz] The hospital denied that it had suspended treatment, and the NFZ branch confirmed that it had received reports and was conducting explanatory proceedings.[^nfz-lodz] In September the hospital reported, across all programmes, 163 people waiting, 78 admitted and an average wait of 15 days.[^nfz-pliki]

Hospitals asked about drug programmes in August answered in different ways. The National Institute of Oncology gave an assurance that it qualifies patients as they come and does not lengthen the wait for organisational or drug-related reasons. The University Clinical Centre in Gdańsk also qualifies patients as they come, and one of the university hospitals in Szczecin admitted that qualification may take longer.[^rynek-sierpien] Without data on how many patients wait after qualification and why, it is impossible to settle which of these pictures is typical.

## Where records of a thousand days come from

Reports about drug programmes often feature records: 2,356 days in ankylosing spondylitis, 875 days in cystic fibrosis.[^mzdrowie] They were given by the tool <span lang="pl">Obserwator</span>, prepared on the initiative of the AmCham Pharmaceutical Committee, which brings together pharmaceutical companies.[^pap] The records come from averages that hospitals report for single units, so they do not describe how long a particular patient waited.

The value of 875 days comes from a unit in which nobody was waiting in September and nobody was admitted.[^nfz-pliki] In the files for September there are 663 such rows in drug programmes, with a waiting time and zero admitted. The value of 1,635 days from an NFZ analysis for the same programme[^rynek-1635] was described by the Fund in the Sejm as a reporting error. At the same meeting, however, the NFZ said that in the Lower Silesian voivodeship the first date in this programme visible on the Fund’s website fell in 2027–2028.[^stenogram] So the problem is real, but the record measures it badly. That is why in this text we give averages weighted by the number admitted and the number of people waiting.

## Are the lists growing

The NFZ does not make previous months available, so this cannot be checked on its website. This site has been saving the files since the data for September 2026, and May could be recovered because the NFZ server served it again.[^nfz-pliki] These two points show that the list without the hospital in Gorzów grew by 18%: from **7,727** to **9,137** people. Two points are a signal, not a trend.

All months can be downloaded in a single CSV file that opens in a spreadsheet: a row is a month, programme, hospital and unit in which someone was waiting or was admitted, with the category, the number waiting, the number admitted and the average wait. The column headings and names are in Polish. Names of practices that may contain a first name and surname are merged into one item per voivodeship.

## What the ministry and the NFZ do not know

The Ministry of Health wrote on 7 October 2026 that neither it nor the NFZ “have data” (our translation) on patients who meet the criteria of a programme but are waiting for reasons related to the value of the hospital’s contract.[^mz19419] What is known is how much hospitals treated above the limit: for the first half of 2026, **<Money value={1082118385.9} decimals={2} />** was waiting to be settled, of which <Money value={859960000} scale="million" decimals={2} /> for medicines.[^mz19419] In July the NFZ allocated <Money value={566100000} scale="million" decimals={1} /> to medicines in drug programmes and chemotherapy for the first quarter, and in October <Money value={3360000000} scale="billion" decimals={2} /> to overperformance for the second quarter, in other services as well. It did not say how much of that second amount went to drug programmes alone.[^nfz-srodki]

The waiting list does not say everything either. The Commissioner for Human Rights described signals of “informal lists” and suspended qualification,[^rpo] and such patients are not in the NFZ data.

<Aside variant="info" title="If you are waiting for treatment in a drug programme">
- The hospital must inform you of your medical category and date, and at your request justify why it chose that date.[^art20] In an emergency the service is due without delay.[^art19]
- In the NFZ search tool <span lang="pl">Gdzie się leczyć</span> (“Where to get treatment”) you can check which hospitals run a given programme.[^gsl]
- If a hospital refuses qualification or postpones treatment for financial reasons, you can report it to the Patient Ombudsman (helpline 800 190 590) and to the voivodeship branch of the NFZ. The Ombudsman has already intervened in such a case.[^rpp] More in the guide [Patient rights](/en/patient-rights/).
</Aside>
