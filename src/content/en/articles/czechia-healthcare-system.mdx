Czechia spends only a slightly larger share of its national income on health than Poland, yet it has more doctors, shorter waiting times and clearly fewer deaths that treatment could have prevented. The system rests on several competing funds, a contribution on wages and a price list set every year in negotiations. We describe how it works according to Czech acts and government data, and check what is public in it. A fund’s contract with a hospital or an outpatient clinic takes effect only once it has been published. An individual doctor’s pay, on the other hand, is protected more strongly than in Poland.

## Seven funds and the state as a payer

Public health insurance is run by seven funds. At the end of 2025 they had 10,851,967 insured persons, of whom 57.0% were in the largest, the General Health Insurance Company (VZP). A person can change fund once every twelve months.[^mf]

For children, pensioners, the unemployed, parents on parental leave and several other groups the contribution is paid by the state budget — that is about 6 million people, more than half of the insured. In 2025 this cost <Money value={153700000000} currency="CZK" scale="billion" />.[^mf] [^platba] The amount of this payment is set by the government.[^platba]

All contributions go to a common account and are divided among the funds according to the age and health of their insured, so that a fund whose members are more expensive to treat does not lose out.[^mf]

## What it costs

Czechia spends slightly more on health than Poland, but less than the European Union average. The share of public money is high, and patients pay less out of pocket than in Poland.[^eurostat-sha]

The system is in deficit. In 2025 the funds had <Money value={531500000000} currency="CZK" scale="billion" /> in revenue and <Money value={536100000000} currency="CZK" scale="billion" /> in expenditure — none of them closed the year with a surplus. The reserves of all the funds stood at <Money value={43200000000} currency="CZK" scale="billion" /> at the end of 2025, but the account from which treatment is paid for held enough money for 8 days.[^mf] The funds’ financial plans for 2025 never reached the government at all, so the funds operated under provisional arrangements for the whole year.[^mf]

## A price list negotiated every year

How much the funds pay for treatment is set once a year. Representatives of the funds and of the providers’ organisations negotiate the value of a point, the level of payments and the limits separately for each type of care; the negotiations are convened by the ministry of health. The minister issues the agreed outcome as a regulation, and where the parties have not reached agreement, the minister decides alone.[^ustawa-17] In the talks on the rates for 2027 agreement was reached in 11 of 15 segments; it was missing, among others, for hospitals and outpatient specialists.[^uv2027]

The basis is a public list of services in which every procedure has a value in points.[^wykaz] From there the paths diverge:

- a **family doctor** receives a monthly rate for every registered patient, weighted by age, and in addition a payment for some services. In the regulation for 2027 the basic rate is from <Money value={60} currency="CZK" /> to <Money value={78} currency="CZK" /> and depends, among other things, on the surgery’s opening hours,[^uv2027]
- an **outpatient specialist** is paid for the services performed: the number of points times the value of a point, with limits,[^uv2027]
- a **hospital** receives money under a system of diagnosis-related groups (CZ-DRG), which is being introduced gradually. The regulation for 2027 raises the share of treatment billed at a single national rate from 30.2% to 47.9%.[^uv2027]

The ministry admits in the explanatory memorandum to the regulation that hospitals’ costs are rising faster than payments, and that their financial result has been falling since 2024.[^uv2027]

The regulation applies only where a fund and a provider have not agreed otherwise.[^ustawa-17] That is exactly why it matters that such contracts are public.

## What the patient pays

A visit to a doctor and a stay in hospital are free of charge. The only fee is <Money value={90} currency="CZK" /> for using the night and holiday emergency service.[^ustawa-16] The fees of CZK 30 for a visit to a doctor and for a prescription were abolished from 2015.[^oplaty-2015] Annual co-payments for medicines are capped: <Money value={5000} currency="CZK" /> for adults, CZK 1,000 for children and people over 65, CZK 500 for people over 70; once the cap is exceeded, the fund covers the co-payment.[^ustawa-16]

Since 1 January 2026 a provider may not demand payment from a patient for anything covered by insurance, nor make treatment conditional on payment. For services not covered by insurance it may charge a fee only if they are in a price list available to patients.[^platnosci]

No referral is needed to see a specialist.[^profil]

## Doctors’ work and pay

Hospital doctors are employed on employment contracts. In state, regional and municipal facilities basic pay is set by a government table: since 1 April 2026 a doctor starting work has received <Money value={52000} currency="CZK" /> a month, and a doctor in the highest grade with more than 32 years of service <Money value={79500} currency="CZK" />.[^tabela] This is basic pay alone, without supplements or on-call duty.

Actual earnings are clearly higher. According to the state institute of health statistics (ÚZIS), the average pay of a doctor in facilities covered by the table was <Money value={126109} currency="CZK" /> a month in 2024, and in facilities that set pay themselves — <Money value={120123} currency="CZK" />. A year earlier the figures were CZK 106,603 and CZK 100,489.[^uzis] The jump results from an agreement that the ministry of health, the largest fund, the medical chamber and the trade unions signed on 8 December 2023 after a protest by doctors against the extension of permitted overtime. According to the Czech press agency, <Money value={9800000000} currency="CZK" scale="billion" /> was earmarked for pay rises for hospital staff, and doctors’ pay was to rise by <Money value={5000} currency="CZK" /> to <Money value={15000} currency="CZK" /> a month.[^porozumienie]

According to the OECD, a salaried specialist in Czechia earned 2.74 times as much as the average worker (2024).[^oecd-remun]

## What is public

**The funds’ contracts with providers.** The law requires a fund to publish online every contract with a provider and every amendment that determines the amount of payment or the scope of services — within 60 days of signing. The contract and the amendment take effect only on the day of publication. If the parties have agreed on payments other than those provided for in the regulation, the fund must indicate this when publishing.[^ustawa-17] According to the ministry of health, by June 2016, nine months after the provision came into force, the funds had published a total of 526,848 documents.[^umowy] What should be public, then, is the terms on which a fund pays a particular provider — not the annual sums paid out. The act excludes from publication information protected by other provisions.[^ustawa-17]

**The list of services and the annual rates.** The list of procedures with point values and the regulation on payments are legal acts, and the ministry also publishes an explanatory memorandum with the expected costs of each segment.[^wykaz] [^uv2027]

**The insured person’s account.** A fund keeps, for every person, a statement of spending on their treatment. Once a year it must make it available free of charge on request, and remote access — also on request.[^ustawa-43] In the largest fund’s app the statement shows, for each service, the amount paid to the provider; in April 2026 the app had 2,957,546 users.[^moje-vzp]

**Statutory deadlines.** A government regulation sets the longest permissible waiting time for planned treatment, e.g. 52 weeks for a hip or knee replacement and 30 weeks for cataract surgery, as well as the longest travel time to a doctor.[^dostepnosc] It is not known, however, whether the deadlines are met: according to the profile by the OECD and the European Commission, Czechia has no nationwide monitoring of waiting times.[^profil]

**The pay table.** The government table of doctors’ pay is a legal act.[^tabela]

An individual doctor’s pay is not public. The act on access to information requires pay to be disclosed by name only in the case of public officials and members of governing bodies — e.g. a hospital director. A request can be made for an ordinary employee’s pay, but the applicant must demonstrate that in the given case the public interest outweighs the protection of privacy.[^ustawa-106] This principle was laid down in 2017 by the Constitutional Court: information on pay must be disclosed when it serves public debate, concerns a public matter, the applicant acts as a guardian of the public interest — e.g. is a journalist or a civil society organisation — and the information itself exists and is available.[^us-2017] Pharmaceutical companies’ payments to doctors are disclosed under a voluntary industry code, and by name only with the doctor’s consent.[^aifp]

## Oversight

The funds check billing themselves. The largest, VZP, had 509 people in its review team in 2025 (355 full-time equivalents), including 98 doctors. In 64 hospitals it checked 4,128 cases billed under the CZ-DRG system and found errors in 41%; the financial effect of such checks for 2024 was <Money value={51700000} currency="CZK" scale="million" />.[^vzp]

In March 2026 the Supreme Audit Office described a different mechanism. Under an EU programme hospitals were paid <Money value={18600000000} currency="CZK" scale="billion" /> for equipment, with the grant covering the whole cost. Of the 27 devices checked, 12, worth <Money value={41100000} currency="CZK" scale="million" />, were used significantly less often than is usual.[^nku]

## How many doctors there are and how long people wait

In 2024 there were 48,781 doctors working in Czech healthcare.[^csu] Medical faculties received additional money, thanks to which the number of first-year students rose by about 20%; the first enlarged cohort graduated in 2025.[^profil]

In 2025, 0.3% of Czechs went without a medical visit because of cost, distance or waiting time — in Poland 2.9%, and 2.4% on average in the European Union.[^eurostat-silc]

## Professional and criminal liability

**Professional.** Every doctor must belong to the Czech Medical Chamber, and professional misconduct is ruled on by its bodies: review committees examine complaints, the district courts of honour rule at first instance, and the chamber’s court of honour hears appeals. Its decisions can be challenged before an administrative court. The penalty may be a reprimand, a fine, conditional expulsion, or expulsion from the chamber, which means a ban on practising.[^clk-ustawa] [^clk-skargi]

By 21 October 2025 the chamber had registered 951 complaints, of which it rejected 23% at the outset. In previous years there were from 983 to 1,125 a year.[^clk-rk] The heaviest penalties are rare: in the reporting year 2024/25 the chamber’s court of honour heard 16 cases at first instance and imposed one unconditional expulsion, one conditional expulsion and three fines; a year earlier it heard four cases and imposed one expulsion and one fine.[^clk-cr] We found no decisions of the chamber published by name — its reports contain only numbers. For comparison: [how it works in Poland](/en/analysis/doctor-discipline-licence-revocation/).

**Criminal.** The criminal code has no separate offence of medical error. A doctor is liable for causing death or bodily harm by negligence, and the penalties are higher when the perpetrator has breached an important duty arising from their profession: causing death then carries from one to six years’ imprisonment, and grievous bodily harm from six months to four years. Lesser harm is punishable only where such a duty has been breached. A court may also impose a ban on practising for a period of one to ten years.[^kk] At the same time the code contains a provision on permissible risk: no offence is committed by a person who, in the course of their profession, performs a beneficial act in line with current knowledge, if the aim cannot be achieved otherwise.[^kk]

How many doctors are prosecuted and convicted for errors is not known. In 2021 the police replied to a request for information that they record the occupation of the person prosecuted but do not distinguish whether the act was connected with practising the profession.[^policja]

**Compensation.** A patient claims compensation in civil proceedings, on the basis of fault. We found no system of payments without establishing fault, nor any work on one. How such systems work elsewhere is described in the text on [compensation without establishing fault](/en/mechanisms/no-fault-compensation-and-doctors-liability/).

The Czech provision on the publication of contracts has a simple design: transparency is a condition of the contract’s effectiveness. In addition, a fund that fails to publish faces a fine of up to <Money value={10000000} currency="CZK" scale="million" decimals={0} />.[^ustawa-44] In Poland the contracts of the National Health Fund (NFZ) with providers are public as to their value: the Fund publishes the maximum amount of the commitment and the type, number and price of the services purchased.[^nfz-135] Since July 2026 public facilities have also reported in the Central Register of Contracts the value of their own contracts for works, supplies and services; contracts with the NFZ are excluded from that register because they have their own publication.[^cru] In none of these places, however, can one see how much public money in total goes to one person working in several facilities. This is what the Polish demand is about: [public total payments for each person, under a permanent identifier and without names](/en/demand/).
