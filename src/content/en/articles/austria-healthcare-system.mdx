Austria has 564 doctors working with patients per 100,000 inhabitants — Poland has 386 — and yet it has been talking about a shortage of doctors for years. The explanation lies in the division into contracted doctors, whom the patient does not pay, and doctors without a contract with a fund, who set their own prices. We describe how this system works according to Austrian acts, the insurer’s data and reports of the audit office (Rechnungshof), and check what is public in it. Doctors’ incomes were calculated from tax data in a way that discloses no one by name. The state does not know the prices in surgeries without a contract, and information about disciplinary proceedings against doctors is restricted by law.

## An assigned fund and two purses

Compulsory insurance covers 99.9% of inhabitants — in 2025 a total of 9,087,844 people were entitled to benefits.[^dachverband] The fund is not chosen — it is assigned by occupation: employees go to the Austrian Health Insurance Fund (ÖGK), the self-employed and farmers to the SVS, and civil servants, railway workers and miners to the BVAEB.[^model] The sickness contribution is 7.65% of pay, of which the employee pays 3.87% and the employer 3.78%.[^skladka]

The money flows in two streams. Care outside hospital is paid for by the funds, which conclude contracts with doctors. Hospitals belong mainly to the federal states — which run about 75% of acute beds — and are financed from the states’ health funds, fed by contributions and by taxes.[^profil] So one body pays for a visit to an outpatient surgery and another for a stay in hospital, which makes it harder to shift treatment to where it is cheaper. In 2026 the government, the federal states and the municipalities agreed on joint planning and more shared financing of care.[^reforma]

## What it costs

Austria spends more on health than the European Union average. Yet the share of out-of-pocket spending is almost the same as in Poland.[^eurostat-sha]

<CountryBars title="Current health expenditure in 2024 (Eurostat)" codes={['AT', 'PL', 'EU']} keys={['healthGdp', 'perCapitaEur', 'perCapitaPps', 'publicShare', 'oopShare']} />

According to the profile by the OECD and the European Commission, Austrians pay out of pocket mainly for medicines, long-term care, the dentist and visits to doctors without a contract with a fund. About 39% of inhabitants have a supplementary policy.[^profil]

Sickness insurance closed 2025 — according to preliminary data — with a loss of €511 million, on revenue of €28.8 billion.[^dachverband] The audit office found in December 2025 that the financial situation of the largest fund, the ÖGK, had clearly worsened in 2018–2023, because the costs of medical care grew faster than receipts.[^rh]

## The contracted doctor and the doctor of choice

A doctor with their own surgery may or may not have a contract with a fund. A person insured with the ÖGK pays nothing at a contracted doctor’s. The rates are set by a collective contract between the fund and the medical chamber — separately in each federal state, because every chamber must give its consent. There has been no uniform contract for the whole of Austria since 2019, so with the same contributions the scope of benefits differs between regions. The audit office recommended abolishing this requirement by law.[^rh]

A doctor without a contract (*Wahlarzt*, literally “doctor of choice”) sets fees freely. The patient pays out of pocket and can then apply to the fund for a refund of 80% of the amount the fund would have paid a contracted doctor — not 80% of the bill.[^zwrot]

These bills made up 16% of the total amount spent on medical care outside hospital — counting contracted doctors’ fees and patients’ bills together — and doctors without a contract are particularly numerous in dermatology, gynaecology and psychiatry.[^rh] The gap between the bill and the refund has three sources: the refund is 80% of the fund tariff, fees can be higher than that tariff, and the bills also include services the fund does not cover at all.[^odpowiedz-wahl] The audit office did not establish in what proportion.[^rh] According to the fund’s 2024 survey, about 75% of patients submit their bills for reimbursement.[^rh] According to the health minister, in 2025 the bills already amounted to €766 million.[^orf-wahl]

According to the medical chamber, the number of contracted doctors has hardly changed for twenty years, although the population has grown from 8 to 9.2 million. The number of doctors without a contract has risen by 61% since 2004.[^kadry] At the end of 2025 — according to the medical chamber — about 8,300 doctors had a surgery with a fund contract and about 11,900 a surgery without one; the latter group also includes hospital doctors who see patients privately after hours.[^oak] Not many contracted posts as such are vacant: according to the ÖGK, in August 2026 more than 97% of about 10,300 were filled, and 94 family doctor posts and 92 specialist posts remained vacant.[^wolne]

The mechanism resembles the Polish one: a doctor sees some patients at the expense of the public payer and some privately ([how this works in Poland](/en/analysis/private-visits-queues-and-prices/)). The difference is that the Austrian fund refunds part of the private bill, so it at least knows how many such bills there are.

## Hospitals

Hospitals receive their money from the states’ health funds under a system of diagnosis-related groups.[^profil] In 2023 inpatient treatment accounted for nearly 32% of current health expenditure, against 28% on average in the EU.[^profil] The reform for 2024–2028 is meant to shift treatment from hospitals to outpatient surgeries: the funds receive €300 million a year for this, and the hospitals €600 million.[^profil]

Hospitals have a special class for patients with a supplementary policy, and the charges for it are additional income for hospital doctors.[^ihs] We found no rules for dividing this money in official sources.

## What the patient pays

A visit to a contracted doctor is free of charge for people insured with the ÖGK.[^rh] The patient pays €7.55 for each prescription medicine — up to a maximum of 2% of annual net income — and an annual charge for the insurance card: €26.85 for 2027, collected in November 2026.[^recepta] [^ecard]

No referral is needed to see a specialist, and the doctor is chosen freely.[^profil]

## Doctors’ work and pay

At the end of 2025 the medical chamber had 53,353 members; according to its statistics, 10,256 are doctors in training. One doctor in three is over 55.[^oak] Austria’s high score in the statistics — 564 doctors per 100,000 inhabitants according to Eurostat, against 386 in Poland[^eurostat-lekarze] — needs a caveat: the chamber itself notes that the Austrian statistics also include doctors in training and those working outside patient care.[^oak]

Hospital doctors are salaried under the pay tables of the federal states. In Styria a specialist has earned from €8,752.40 to €13,187.00 gross a month since July 2026, and a head of department from €13,418.80 to €17,928.90 — without on-call duty and supplements.[^kages]

Austria knows more about the incomes of doctors with their own surgery than most countries do. In 2025 the Institute for Advanced Studies (IHS) calculated them, commissioned by the federation of funds, from the finance ministry’s tax data — for each doctor from the practice and from the salaried job together.[^ihs]

This is income — revenue minus the costs of the practice and contributions — not a salary. The spread is wide: among contracted doctors one in ten had less than €64,093 and one in ten more than €474,198. The lower median for doctors without a contract is partly due to the fact that many of them run a surgery alongside work in a hospital. In the laboratory specialties and in radiology the figures do not include income from companies.[^ihs] According to the OECD, a specialist with their own practice in Austria earned 4.60 times as much as the average worker, and a family doctor 3.56 times (2021).[^oecd-remun]

## What is public

**Doctors’ incomes from tax data — without names.** The IHS report combines, for each person, the income from the surgery and from the salaried job, and publishes medians, deciles and results by specialty. A similar compilation for all occupations — median incomes of the self-employed — is published every two years by the audit office.[^ihs] The authors of the report note that incomes in Austria are generally not very transparent and that no one examines the costs of practices.

**Hospital pay tables.** The federal states and their hospital companies publish the full pay tables for doctors.[^kages]

**The value of bills from doctors without a contract.** The fund knows how many bills patients submitted for reimbursement and how much it refunded, and the audit office publishes these figures.[^rh]

**Industry payments — in aggregate.** Under the pharmaceutical industry’s code, companies disclosed for 2025 a total of €149.5 million transferred to doctors, facilities and organisations. We found no act that would require publication by name.[^pharmig]

What remains outside the state’s knowledge is the prices charged by doctors without a contract — they set their fees without limits — and the waiting time for a visit: the audit office recommended monitoring it as early as 2021 and found in 2025 that the recommendation had not been carried out.[^rh] We found no publication of the fees the fund pays to individual contracted doctors.

Since 1 September 2025 Austria has had a freedom of information act, which abolished official secrecy and introduced a right of access to information.[^ifg]

## How many doctors there are and how long people wait

The number of medical students is limited by an admission test: for the 2026/27 year public universities had 1,950 places in medicine and dentistry, and 16,880 people applied. Up to 87 places were set aside for candidates who commit themselves to working in public healthcare.[^medat]

Work is distributed very unevenly among contracted doctors. In 2022 one family doctor had 165 patients in a quarter and another 31,133. The number of filled contracted posts in family medicine per inhabitant fell by 5.1% in 2019–2023.[^rh]

Austria has no nationwide measurement of waiting times, but some federal states publish their own data. Upper Austria gives the waiting time for planned operations in each hospital: in September 2026 the wait there for a hip replacement was from 10 to 90 weeks, and for cataract surgery from 9 to 50 weeks.[^ooe] The profile by the OECD and the European Commission cites data from Vienna’s hospitals: in 2024 the wait for a hip replacement was 6 weeks in the largest hospital and more than 30 weeks in smaller ones, and a shorter wait was associated with having a supplementary policy.[^profil] Even so, few Austrians forgo a visit because of cost, distance or a queue — 0.9% against 2.9% of Poles (2025).[^eurostat-silc]

In opinion surveys Austria does much better than Poland ([about these surveys](/en/analysis/satisfaction-with-healthcare-in-poland/)).[^zadowolenie]

## Professional and criminal liability

**Professional.** Doctors’ misconduct is ruled on by a disciplinary council at the medical chamber: it is chaired by a lawyer and has two doctors as members. It can issue a written reprimand, impose a fine of up to €36,340, ban the doctor from practising for a fixed period or strike the doctor off the list.[^dyscyplina] The act prohibits informing the public about disciplinary proceedings and their outcome unless they were held in public; only the doctor concerned may speak about them. The chamber submits an annual report to the minister, but the act does not provide for its publication, and in 2022 — invoking this prohibition — the chamber refused to give parliament the number of proceedings and penalties.[^dyscyplina-jawnosc] Only partial figures are known: in cases concerning doctors’ statements about the pandemic, 46 proceedings had been opened and 7 penalties imposed by June 2023.[^covid-dysc] For comparison: [how this works in Poland](/en/analysis/doctor-discipline-licence-revocation/).

**Criminal.** A doctor answers for an error under the general rules: negligent homicide carries up to one year in prison, and up to three years in the case of gross negligence. Negligent bodily injury carries up to three months, and up to six in the case of gross negligence; up to six months also applies to grievous bodily injury without gross negligence.[^stgb] The code does, however, contain an exception for the medical professions: a person who, while practising such a profession, caused bodily injury through negligence that was not gross is not liable to punishment for the basic form of this offence. The exception does not cover gross negligence, grievous bodily injury or causing death.[^stgb]

It is not known how many criminal proceedings are conducted against doctors and how many end in conviction. The justice minister replied to parliament that court registers classify cases by the type of offence, not by the offender’s occupation.[^karne-statystyki]

**Compensation.** Each federal state has a patient advocate and a fund that pays compensation without deciding on fault. The fund is financed by patients themselves: the charge for a day in hospital includes an amount for this purpose — €0.73 as a base, index-linked — collected for no more than 28 days a year. In Salzburg in 2024 the commission considered 49 applications: in 16 cases it awarded compensation, in 24 it refused and 9 it postponed; the fund paid out €220,375.[^fundusz] We write more about such arrangements in the text on [compensation without establishing fault](/en/mechanisms/no-fault-compensation-and-doctors-liability/).

The Austrian report on doctors’ incomes shows that it is possible to add up one person’s money from several sources and publish the distribution without disclosing anyone. This is the same principle that underlies the Polish demand: [public total payments from public money for each person, under a permanent identifier and without names](/en/demand/). Austria did it once, commissioned by the insurer; the demand concerns data published on a continuing basis.

## Method

We base the description of the system on the texts of acts from the Austrian legal information system (RIS), the audit office’s press release of 12 December 2025 on medical care outside hospital, the report of the Institute for Advanced Studies on doctors’ incomes, the medical chamber’s statistics, ministers’ replies to parliamentary questions, Eurostat data, and the country profile prepared by the OECD and the European Observatory on Health Systems and Policies for the European Commission. Secondary sources — the press and materials of the medical chambers, which are a party to the disputes over doctors without a contract — are marked in the footnotes.

The comparative indicators for Austria, Poland and the European Union come from the same Eurostat datasets and the same year. We do not convert monthly amounts into annual ones.
