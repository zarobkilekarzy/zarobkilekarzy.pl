A doctor who becomes a member of a municipal, county or regional council files an asset declaration every year. In it the doctor enters income from each source separately: from the practice, from employment in a hospital, from contracts, from a company. The public part of the declaration is published in the Public Information Bulletin (BIP).[^oswiadczenia] For a councillor, then, one can see at once what nobody adds up for other doctors — income from many workplaces in a single year.

The X user Tomo (@tomo9000p) collected {ZBIOR.wierszy} such declarations. We compared each amount with the declaration in BIP. Much of the dataset turned out to be wrong, so the figures below come from the corrected rows, separately for 2025 and 2024.[^zbior] We do not give names: we are interested in the mechanism, not in people.

## Why a councillor’s income is public

The obligation comes from three local government acts: on municipal self-government (Articles 24h and 24i), on county self-government (Articles 25c and 25d) and on regional self-government (Articles 27c and 27d). A councillor files a declaration every year by 30 April, as of the end of the previous year. The information in it is public, except for the address and the location of real estate.[^oswiadczenia] Income in a declaration filed in 2026 therefore concerns 2025 — and that is how we count the year in the dataset.

The same provisions cover, among others, heads of local government units, including directors of local government hospitals. We describe their declarations in our analysis [How much hospital directors earn](/en/analysis/hospital-directors-pay/). A doctor who is neither a councillor nor a director does not file a declaration. No public source adds up such a doctor’s income from several facilities — we describe this mechanism in [Lack of transparency](/en/mechanisms/lack-of-transparency/).

## The practice is not everything

The original dataset had a single “income” column, described as income from medical activity. In {BLEDY.suma} rows, however, it was the sum of several sources: the practice, employment in a hospital, a company and contracts.[^zbior] We separated them. Income from the practice alone — from a sole proprietorship, a medical office or a doctors’ partnership — looks different from total income from work.

In {PRAKTYKA.ponizejPolowy} of the {PRAKTYKA.zObiema} confirmed rows in which we know both amounts, the practice is less than half of income from work. The rest comes from employment or contracts in hospitals. Another {PRAKTYKA.bezPraktyki} councillors do not run a practice at all: their income is employment, contracts or a dividend from their own medical company.[^zbior] The same doctor is thus often both an entrepreneur and an employee of several facilities. What follows from this for transparency we describe in [Contract or employment](/en/mechanisms/contract-vs-employment/).

### Revenue is not income

Revenue is what the practice takes in before costs are deducted: staff pay, rent, equipment. It can be many times higher than income. In the dataset the highest revenue from a practice, <Money value={MAX_PRZYCHOD.przychod} scale="million" decimals={2} />, yielded <Money value={MAX_PRZYCHOD.dzialalnosc} /> of income.[^zbior] That is why we calculate the statistics on income only.

In {RODZAJ.tylkoPrzychod} declarations only revenue is given for the practice — usually for doctors taxed at a flat rate on revenue (*ryczałt*), who do not calculate income. The original dataset then entered revenue as income. We leave income blank, and these rows are not included in the statistics. In another {RODZAJ.jednaKwota} declarations the practice has a single amount in the “revenue and income” box. We count it as income, but in the tables we state how many rows this applies to.[^zbior]

The form is filled in by hand, and everyone fills it in differently. The same amount goes once into the box on business activity, once into “other income”. Some declarations are internally contradictory or give a year in the text different from the filing date.[^zbior] Comparing declarations therefore requires reading each one separately.

## Why this is not a picture of a typical doctor

The dataset is selective. Councillors are doctors who stood for election and won, most of them with their own practice. The dataset was compiled by one person, and we do not know what share of all doctor-councillors it includes. Nor does it cover doctors who work only as employees and hold no office in local government.

The result depends on whom the dataset covers. The Co w Zdrowiu website analysed a different dataset — 160 declarations of doctor-councillors — and obtained a median of about <Money value={285000} /> and a highest income of <Money value={4900000} scale="million" />.[^cowzdrowiu] Neither of these medians says how much the average doctor in Poland earns. They only show how wide the spread of incomes is among people whose income happens to be visible.

We collect individual top settlements of doctors from other sources — pay lists, audits, hospitals’ answers — in [Record payments to doctors](/en/data/record-doctor-payments/). We do not repeat people from this dataset there.

## How we checked the dataset

The declarations were collected by Tomo (@tomo9000p); we checked each amount in the declaration.[^zbior] We opened the declaration at the link in each row, and where the link led to a list, we found the councillor’s file in BIP. Most files are scans of handwritten forms, so we read the amounts from the image of the page. We counted the year from the filing date: a declaration from April 2026 concerns 2025. We did not find three declarations — the dataset had no working link to them.

We excluded two rows from the compilation. One concerned the wife of a councillor rather than a councillor herself — the dataset’s author had already excluded it. The other concerned a person against whom criminal proceedings are pending: we exclude rows of people with pending proceedings. We do not publish names, and we give the specialty after the dataset’s author, because the declarations do not contain it. Each row in the table below links to the declaration in BIP, where the amount can be checked.

An earlier version of this page treated {STARE.wierszy} declarations as one group and gave a median of about PLN 633,000 and {people(STARE.od1mln)} with income of a million or more. These figures mixed 2024 and 2025, revenue with income, and sums of different sources with income from the practice. After the corrections, the number of people with income from work of a million or more is {L25.od1mln} for 2025 and {L24.od1mln} for 2024. The median income from work for 2025 came out the same as the former median of the whole dataset, but that is a coincidence: it is calculated from different rows and with a different definition, and the median of the practice alone for 2025 is PLN {tys(D25.mediana)} thousand.[^zbior]

## What follows

Councillors’ declarations show that a doctor’s annual income from many workplaces can be gathered in one document and published. This is done, however, only for people who hold office in local government, and in a form that cannot be compared without reading each scan. For other doctors no such total exists in any public source.

We call for the total payments from public money to each person working in facilities with an NFZ contract to be public under a permanent identifier, without names. We describe the details on the page of [our proposal](/en/demand/).
