Denmark finances treatment from taxes and charges the patient nothing for a visit to a doctor or a stay in hospital. We describe how this system works according to Danish acts and official documents, and check what is public in it. Two arrangements set Denmark apart: the patient’s right to a private hospital at the region’s expense when the public one cannot meet the deadline, and an official list of doctors’ ties to industry, published by name.

## The state, the regions and the municipalities

Three levels of government are responsible for healthcare. The state sets the rules and distributes the money, five regions run the hospitals and finance doctors outside hospitals, and 98 municipalities deal with rehabilitation, long-term care and prevention. Hospitals are almost exclusively owned by the regions. Family doctors run their own practices, and it is they who refer the patient to a specialist.[^profil]

This arrangement changes on 1 January 2027. Parliament passed the reform on 11 June 2025: the Capital Region and Region Zealand will merge into Region East Denmark, and each region will get health councils made up of regional and municipal councillors.[^reforma]

The regions’ budget is set every year by an agreement with the government. The agreement for 2027 raises the level of regional health spending by <Money value={2300000000} currency="DKK" scale="billion" /> and provides <Money value={7250000000} currency="DKK" scale="billion" decimals={2} /> for investment. It also introduces a cap on hospital spending and makes <Money value={1000000000} currency="DKK" scale="billion" decimals={0} /> of grants conditional on the regions staying within their budgets.[^umowa-2027]

## A regional budget and a rate per patient

Hospitals receive their money from the region’s budget. The reform abolishes the additional mechanisms that tied part of the money to results and to the municipalities’ share of the costs.[^reforma]

A family doctor is paid by the region under a collective agreement. According to the table of the family doctors’ organisation in force from April to September 2026, the doctor received <Money value={570.96} currency="DKK" /> a year for every registered patient and <Money value={172.88} currency="DKK" /> for a consultation in the surgery.[^plo] The right to bill the region is limited: the region issues new provider numbers only in the 17 municipalities with the greatest shortage of doctors, and existing ones can be bought from a predecessor.[^profil]

## What the patient pays

A visit to a family doctor, a visit to a specialist with a referral and a stay in hospital are free of charge. The patient contributes to the cost of medicines and dental treatment; to cover these expenses, and to get faster access to private clinics, Danes buy private insurance — themselves or through their employer.[^profil] Out-of-pocket payments by patients account for 14.6% of health spending, slightly less than the European Union average.

## Doctors’ work and pay

Hospital doctors are employees of the regions and are paid under a collective agreement. Since April 2026 the basic rate for a specialist doctor has been <Money value={65228} currency="DKK" /> a month; on top of that the employer pays a pension contribution.[^lon] A senior doctor (*overlæge*) in the Region of Southern Denmark has received from <Money value={73316} currency="DKK" /> to <Money value={76311} currency="DKK" /> a month since April 2026, depending on seniority.[^overlaege] Neither amount includes on-call duty or local supplements, so they are not full earnings. We found no average doctor’s pay in official statistics.

## Public and private work at the same time

A hospital doctor may have secondary occupations as long as they can be reconciled with work in the hospital. The collective agreement places the duty to inform the employer about such occupations on chief physicians, senior doctors and ward doctors — not on all doctors — and the regions are responsible for applying it.[^rigs]

How this worked was examined in 2016 by the Danish national audit office. Two of the three regions examined had not ensured that doctors’ secondary occupations could be reconciled with work in the hospital, and none of the selected hospitals had set assessment criteria. According to the parliamentary auditors, nearly half of the senior doctors heading departments had secondary occupations, although in two regions a ban was the rule.[^rigs] These are findings from 2016. In 2018 the office concluded that the regions had put remedies in place — including electronic registration of secondary occupations in two regions — and closed the case.[^rigs-2018] We found no more recent audit.

The matter carries extra weight in Denmark because the public system itself sends patients to private hospitals when it cannot meet the deadline.[^sundhedsloven]

## What is public

**Doctors’ ties to industry.** A doctor who works with a pharmaceutical company or a manufacturer of medical devices must notify the medicines agency or obtain its permission. The agency publishes on its website the doctor’s first name and surname, the number of their licence to practise, the company, the type and period of the tie, and the total amount the doctor received from the company in the calendar year. The entry disappears two years after the cooperation ends. Shares in one company worth up to <Money value={200000} currency="DKK" /> on the day of acquisition only need to be notified; larger holdings require the agency’s permission.[^lms]

**The pay of a public employee — on request.** The act on access to public administration files excludes personnel matters from access, but with an exception: for every person employed in the public sector, the name, position, education, duties, pay terms and business trips must be disclosed. Since 2024 the name is not disclosed where significant considerations of the employee’s safety speak against it.[^jawnosc] Hospital doctors are employees of the regions, so the provision covers them. This is access on request, not publication — and we found no example of how the regions apply it to doctors.

**Disciplinary decisions — with the name in four cases.** The patient complaints agency publishes a decision with the first name, surname and licence number when a doctor has received criticism with a reprimand, a third criticism within five years, when there is a suspicion of an offence under the act on health professions, or when the criticism concerns a cosmetic procedure. The decision is available for two years.[^stpk]

**Waiting times.** The health data authority measures the time to diagnosis, treatment and surgery, and the ministry announces the results every year for the country and for each region.[^maal]

**What is not visible.** We found no publication of how much a region pays to individual practices, nor a public list of hospital doctors’ secondary occupations.

## How many doctors there are and how long people wait

<CountryBars title="Doctors and unmet healthcare needs (Eurostat)" codes={['DK', 'PL', 'EU']} keys={['doctors', 'unmet', 'unmetWait']} />

Danish law gives the patient two deadlines. The region must provide diagnosis within one month if that is medically possible, and if not — present a plan for further examinations within that time. It must offer treatment within one month of the end of the diagnostic process. If it misses either deadline, the patient can choose a private hospital or clinic with which the regions have an agreement, and the region covers the cost.[^sundhedsloven] [^profil]

In 2024 the right to diagnosis on time was met in 79% of cases of somatic illness — from 73% in the Central Denmark and Zealand regions to 93% in North Denmark. The average wait was 40 days for treatment and 57 days for surgery.[^maal]

Primary care is short of doctors: in 2024 there were about 3,500 family doctors.[^profil]

## Professional and criminal liability

**Professional.** Complaints against doctors are heard by the healthcare disciplinary board. In 2025 it closed 2,365 cases and issued 2,042 decisions; 79% contained no criticism, 17% ended in criticism and 3% in criticism with a reprimand. The largest share of complaints — 40% — concerned family doctors and out-of-hours doctors.[^stpk] The patient safety authority can temporarily withdraw or restrict a licence to practise; a court does so permanently, at the authority’s request.[^autoryzacja] In 2025 the authority issued 87 decisions that were open to appeal.[^stps]

**Criminal.** The act on health professions has its own criminal provision: a doctor guilty of more serious or repeated neglect or carelessness is liable to a fine or up to four months’ imprisonment.[^autoryzacja] We did not establish how many such cases reach the courts.

**Compensation.** The patient does not have to sue the doctor. They report the injury to a public body, which assesses whether it could have been avoided. In 2025 there were 16,621 reports of treatment injuries, compensation was awarded in 25% of the cases decided, and the total amount was <Money value={898950000} currency="DKK" scale="million" decimals={2} />. As the tables of the report show, since 2024 the statistics have also covered dentists, so they are not directly comparable with earlier years.[^erstatning] We write more about this model in the text on [compensation without establishing fault](/en/mechanisms/no-fault-compensation-and-doctors-liability/).

The Danish deadline is built differently from the Polish waiting list. The patient knows the date after which they are allowed to go elsewhere, and the cost of the delay is borne by the region, not the patient. The second thing is the list of ties to industry: it is collected and published by a public agency, on the basis of an act, with the amount next to the name.

## Method

We base the description on the texts of Danish acts and executive orders from the official legal database, the government’s agreement with the regions for 2027, the reports of the patient complaints and patient compensation bodies, a report of the national audit office, and the profile of Denmark prepared by the OECD and the European Observatory for the European Commission. The comparative indicators for Denmark, Poland and the European Union come from the same Eurostat datasets and the same year.

We give pay as basic rates from collective agreements — without on-call duty, supplements or the employer’s pension contribution. The family doctors’ rates come from the table of their organisation and are the price of a service, not the income of a practice.
