There is a new text on the site: [The minimum pay act: what it costs and why the cost rises by itself](/mechanizmy/ustawa-podwyzkowa/) (in Polish). It explains how the act of 8 June 2017 on the lowest pay in healthcare providers works, what it costs the NFZ year by year and who decided on it.

## What it shows

- **In 2025 the pay rises required by the act cost the NFZ PLN 58 billion**, a quarter of the payer’s budget. Each July indexation stays in the costs for good, and the next one is added on top.
- **That is about two thirds of the growth in NFZ spending on care since 2021.** This is the editors’ estimate based on NFZ financial plans.
- **Annual indexation was added in 2020 from a bill tabled by members of the Sejm,** whose explanatory memorandum stated that it would have no additional financial effects. The act names no separate source of money: the NFZ pays from the same pool it uses to fund treatment.
- **At the current pace of wage growth the cost will exceed PLN 100 billion a year in 2030.** This is the editors’ projection. The chart lets readers recalculate it for a different pace.

## What else is in the text

Government forecasts set against the cost that materialised, how the money flows through the prices of services, the Sejm votes by parliamentary club and the history of the act together with the citizens’ bills. The people and institutions involved in the work on the act are in the [Healthcare constellation](/konstelacja/) (in Polish). Every number is sourced in the notes.
