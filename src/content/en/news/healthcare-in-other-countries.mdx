There is a new section on the site: [Healthcare in other countries](/en/countries/). It brings together descriptions of the systems of fourteen countries and lets you compare them with Poland using the same measures.

## What the section contains

- **Fourteen countries:** Austria, Belgium, Czechia, Denmark, France, Spain, the Netherlands, Canada, Germany, Sweden, the United States, Hungary, the United Kingdom and Italy.
- **One template.** Each text answers the same questions: who the payer is, what the system costs, how facilities are paid, how much the patient pays out of pocket, how much doctors earn, how much of that money is public, how many doctors there are, what access to treatment is like, how people rate the system and how professional liability works.
- **The same measures for all.** Figures compared between countries come from one dataset and one year — from Eurostat, the OECD and satisfaction surveys. Where there is no data, the field stays empty.
- **A chart and a table on the section page.** Each country is a dot on the chart and a row in the table; Poland is the point of reference.

## Two cross-country texts

Alongside the country descriptions there are two comparisons: [how other countries regulate a doctor’s work in a public hospital and in a private practice](/en/analysis/public-and-private-practice-in-other-countries/) and [who measures treatment queues and how](/en/analysis/waiting-times-in-other-countries/).

Every number is sourced in the notes.
