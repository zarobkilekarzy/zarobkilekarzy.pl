When a doctor treats patients badly, neglects medical records or behaves unethically, their professional liability is decided not by a common court but by the medical self-government. A disciplinary prosecutor (*rzecznik odpowiedzialności zawodowej*) brings the charges, and the regional medical courts and the Supreme Medical Court decide — **doctors are judged by doctors**.[^uil-droga] The harshest penalties are rare in this system: in {allYears} **{plTotal} doctors** lost the right to practise with final effect, and in {PL_ZERO_YEARS} of the {YEARS_COUNT} years — none.[^nil-sady]

Below is the path of a case step by step, with examples from the [catalogue of rulings](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/) (in Polish) — summaries of final rulings that the Supreme Medical Chamber (NIL) publishes without names.[^portal] All the case summaries linked in this text are in Polish. The figures on penalties are described in our analysis [Poland’s harshest penalty for doctors is almost never imposed](/en/analysis/doctor-discipline-licence-revocation/).

## What a doctor is liable for

The Act on medical chambers provides: “Members of medical chambers are subject to professional liability for breaching the principles of medical ethics and the provisions related to practising the profession of doctor” (our translation). Such a breach is **professional misconduct**.[^uil-zakres] The principles of ethics are set out in the Code of Medical Ethics, adopted by the National Medical Congress.[^kel]

Misconduct does not have to concern treatment. The catalogue includes cases about [the title of psychiatrist on a website](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/psychiatra-portal/) used by a doctor without that specialisation (a fine), [unpaid membership fees to the chamber](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/skladki/) (an admonition) and [advertising on a television programme](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/telewizja-metoda/) (an admonition).

The proceedings run independently of criminal proceedings concerning the same act. They may be stayed until the end of the criminal case if its outcome may affect the decision. The bodies of the self-government are bound by a final court decision “shaping a right or a legal relationship” (our translation).[^uil-zakres]

**A criminal judgment does not close the case in the self-government.** [A doctor from an ambulance crew](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/karetka-aorta/) who diagnosed torticollis in a patient with a dilated aorta was fined by a criminal court and given a reprimand by the medical court. The Supreme Medical Court added that the criminal judgment did not make the self-government’s penalty pointless. In other cases the courts saw this differently — more on that below.

## The path of a case

<CaseRoute />

The act divides the proceedings into four stages: verification steps, the preliminary investigation, proceedings before the medical court and enforcement proceedings.[^uil-droga]

<Steps>

1. **Complaint.** On receiving “information indicating that professional misconduct may have been committed” (our translation), the disciplinary prosecutor must without delay either open a preliminary investigation or refuse to open one.[^uil-droga] The Patient Ombudsman refers patients to the prosecutor.[^rpp] In the catalogue, cases began with, among other things, [a complaint from a patient’s mother](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/okulistka-badanie/), [a complaint from another doctor](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/wypowiedz-zgony/) and [a police note](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/nietrzezwa-gabinet/). How to make a complaint is explained in our guide [Patients’ rights](/en/patient-rights/).
2. **Verification steps.** The prosecutor makes a preliminary check of whether there are grounds for proceedings; no experts are appointed at this stage. When checking facts, the prosecutor must decide whether to open proceedings or refuse no later than 3 months after receiving the information. The injured party may challenge a refusal before the regional medical court.[^uil-droga] In the case of [examinations of employees without the required qualifications](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/medycyna-pracy-uprawnienia/) the prosecutor refused to open proceedings, the medical court set that decision aside after a complaint, and the doctor eventually received an admonition.
3. **Preliminary investigation.** The prosecutor questions witnesses, appoints experts and brings charges against the doctor — from that moment the doctor is the accused. The investigation should be completed within 6 months; the Chief Disciplinary Prosecutor may extend this by 6 months, and beyond that the Supreme Medical Court decides. A complaint about excessive length is available. The investigation ends with a discontinuation or a request for a penalty.[^uil-droga]
4. **First instance.** The case is heard by the regional medical court of the chamber to which the accused belongs, sitting as a panel of three. The prosecutor appears before it as a party, alongside the injured party and the accused.[^uil-droga]
5. **Appeal.** Within 14 days of service of the decision the parties may appeal to the Supreme Medical Court. It sits as a panel of five and may uphold, amend or set aside the decision. It may not, however, itself find guilty a doctor whom the first instance acquitted or whose case it discontinued. Its decision is final once announced.[^uil-instancje] In the case of [three unnecessary extractions](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/ortodontka-ekstrakcje/) the Supreme Medical Court replaced a one-year suspension of the licence to practise with a reprimand. In the case of [a consultation in the emergency department](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/rtg-pulmonolog/) it upheld an acquittal against which the patient’s family had appealed.
6. **Cassation and reopening.** Against a final decision of the Supreme Medical Court, the parties, the minister of health and the President of the Supreme Medical Council may lodge a cassation appeal with the Supreme Court within 2 months — on the grounds of a flagrant breach of the law or a disproportionate penalty.[^uil-instancje] After a Supreme Court judgment, for example, the case of [ozone therapy](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/ozonoterapia/) went back to the Supreme Medical Court: the Supreme Court ordered expert opinions to be obtained. A closed case can also be reopened when new facts or evidence come to light.[^uil-instancje] This is how the court [acquitted a doctor](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/skladki-wznowienie/) who had been suspended for unpaid membership fees: the new fact was the doctor’s belief that the employer was paying the fees.

</Steps>

**The injured party is a party to the proceedings**, not just a witness. They may appoint up to two representatives, challenge a refusal to open proceedings and a discontinuation, appeal against the decision and lodge a cassation appeal. After their death these rights may be exercised by their next of kin. The accused may have up to two defence counsel, assigned ex officio if necessary.[^uil-strony] In the case of [a psychiatric examination without consent](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/badanie-bez-zgody/) the injured party’s representative appealed, demanding that the doctor be deprived of the right to practise. In the case of [the reading of an X-ray](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/rtg-radiolog/) the appeal was lodged by the family of the patient who had died.

## When a case ends without a penalty

A doctor cannot be penalised until the misconduct has been established by a final decision. “Any doubts that cannot be removed in proceedings concerning doctors’ professional liability shall be resolved in favour of the accused” (our translation).[^uil-bez-kary] A case can end without a penalty in three ways.

**Acquittal.** In the case of [a nose operation](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/rynoplastyka/) the court of first instance banned the doctor from operating on the head for 12 months, and the Supreme Medical Court acquitted: it found no objective evidence of poor technique and resolved the doubts in favour of the accused. In the case of [a death after an appendix operation](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/dyzur-chirurgia-zgon/) the Supreme Medical Court noted that an untrue entry in the records must be “beyond doubt, not merely probable” (our translation).

**Limitation.** Proceedings are not opened if 3 years have passed since the act; each step taken by the prosecutor interrupts this period. Liability lapses 5 years after the act, and if the act is also a crime — not earlier than liability for the crime.[^uil-bez-kary] In the catalogue the Supreme Medical Court discontinued for this reason four cases in which the first instance had imposed penalties, and in each it noted that the evidence did not justify an acquittal or that the act remained misconduct. In the case of [orthodontic treatment that lasted more than ten years](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/ortodoncja-11-lat/) the Supreme Medical Court “regrets the length” of such cases (our translation). In the case of [the examination without consent](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/badanie-bez-zgody/) there were no criminal proceedings that would have extended the period.

**Minor gravity.** [A doctor with a specialisation obtained abroad](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/endokrynolog-bez-uprawnien/) who, holding a conditional licence to practise, saw patients as an endocrinologist received no penalty. According to the court, the doctor’s conduct was “rather an expression of a certain recklessness” (our translation), and the finding of misconduct itself is meant to serve as a warning.

**A penalty from other proceedings.** In five cases in the catalogue the medical court discontinued the proceedings because a criminal court had already ruled on the same act. This was so with [a heart attack missed in the emergency department](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/sor-zawal/), for which the doctor was fined, and with [fictitious pregnancies and sick notes](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/ciaza-fikcyjna/), for which a criminal court sentenced the doctor to, among other things, a temporary ban on practising the profession. In the latter case the Supreme Medical Court stressed that discontinuation is not acquittal: the act was attributed to the doctor, but the criminal penalty had already been severe.

## Seven penalties and their consequences

The act gives the court seven penalties, from an admonition to revocation of the licence to practise. The provisions for each of them are listed next to the scale in the [game](/sad-lekarski/) (in Polish). Some rules become visible only in specific cases:

- **A fine** ranges from one third to four times the average monthly wage in the enterprise sector and goes to a social purpose related to healthcare.[^uil-kary] The maximum was imposed on [an expert witness who for two years returned neither the opinion nor the case files](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/biegly-akta/).
- **Penalties can be combined.** To a restriction of the scope of practice or to a suspension the court may add a ban on holding managerial positions, and to penalties four to six — a fine.[^uil-kary] [A head of ward who discharged a patient before the diagnosis was complete](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/oddzial-ordynator/) received a 2-year suspension and a 5-year ban; [a doctor from a live stream about DMSO and hydrogen peroxide](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/dmso-transmisja/) — a one-year suspension and a fine.
- **A restriction of the scope of practice** requires the court to specify the activities that the doctor may not perform.[^uil-kary] In the catalogue these include a 6-month ban on performing orthodontic procedures independently ([orthodontics with a diet](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/ortodoncja-dieta/)) and a ban on treating people under 18 ([a teenager treated without indications](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/nastolatek-leki/)).
- **Publication in the chamber’s bulletin.** With penalties four to seven the court may order it.[^uil-kary] It did so with [the five-year suspension of an emergency department doctor](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/sor-symulant/) who had dismissed a patient as a malingerer.
- **A suspended doctor may not practise the profession “in any form”** (our translation). Suspension and revocation are grounds for terminating an employment contract or a civil-law contract without notice.[^uil-kary]
- **Expungement.** An admonition is expunged after a year, a reprimand 3 years after it becomes final, penalties three to five 3 years after they have been served, and a suspension 5 years after it has been served. **Revocation of the licence to practise is never expunged.**[^uil-kary] In that doctor’s case the court found no mitigating circumstances, partly because an earlier penalty had not been expunged. [A doctor who for the second time worked an emergency department shift after drinking alcohol](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/sor-alkohol-powtorny/) lost the right to practise. The Supreme Medical Court pointed out, among other things, that the doctor had not taken the “chance to improve” (our translation) given by an earlier decision.

## Temporary suspension

Before a decision is issued, the medical court may, at the prosecutor’s request, temporarily suspend a doctor’s licence to practise or restrict the scope of practice — for up to a year. There are two conditions: the evidence points “with high probability” (our translation) to serious misconduct, and its nature indicates that the doctor’s continued work endangers patient safety or risks further misconduct. The order is immediately enforceable, even after a complaint to the Supreme Medical Court. If no final decision is issued within that time, the court examines of its own motion whether the suspension should continue. The period of suspension is counted towards the penalty.[^uil-zawieszenie]

In 2024 the regional medical courts issued **36 orders temporarily suspending** the licence to practise and 3 temporarily restricting the scope of practice.[^osl-2024]

## What is public

“Proceedings before a medical court take place at a public hearing” (our translation). The court excludes the public, however, if openness could, among other things, breach medical confidentiality or an important private interest. The announcement of the decision is public, and the presiding judge gives its main reasons orally.[^uil-jawnosc]

An acquitted doctor may demand that the chamber publish the acquittal in its bulletin at its own expense. The Register of Penalised Doctors, kept by the Supreme Medical Council, is open only to persons and institutions that show a legal interest.[^uil-jawnosc]

Since 2024 the Supreme Medical Chamber has also published final rulings with their written reasons on a portal run by the President of the Supreme Medical Council. It includes rulings of the Supreme Medical Court served on the President of the Council after 1 January 2024, and of the regional courts — after 1 July 2024. A version with narrower anonymisation is available only to members of the medical courts and to disciplinary prosecutors.[^regulamin]

In the public version, among other things, the case number, the dates of the decision and of the misconduct, the name of the court, first names and surnames, licence numbers and amounts are removed, and place names are replaced with an ellipsis. If the presiding judge of the court neither approves the anonymisation nor sends it back for correction within 7 days, the ruling is published automatically. The rules allow the rulings to be used “only for scientific, educational and information purposes” (our translation).[^regulamin]

The cases in the catalogue come from this portal. That is why it gives no amounts for fines: in the case of [an OCT scan without indications](/sad-lekarski/orzeczenia/okulistka-oct/) all that is known is that the fine was twice the average wage.

## Arguments and limits

The Constitution allows professional self-governments to be created by statute to represent persons practising professions of public trust and to “exercise care over the proper practice of these professions within the limits of the public interest and for its protection” (our translation).[^konst] The Act on medical chambers entrusts this care to the doctors’ self-government, and the regional medical court and the disciplinary prosecutor are bodies of every chamber.[^uil-zakres]

The self-government does not decide without outside review. A cassation appeal to the Supreme Court may also be lodged by the minister of health, and compensation for a doctor acquitted after reopening or cassation is decided by a common court.[^uil-instancje] The injured party has the rights of a party, including the right to appeal and to lodge a cassation appeal.[^uil-strony] The first ground for excluding the public from a hearing is medical confidentiality, which protects information about patients.[^uil-jawnosc]

<Aside variant="warn" title="The catalogue is a selection of cases, not a representative sample">

In the catalogue, **19 of the 59 outcomes (32%) ended without a penalty**. Among the final rulings of the medical courts from the first half of 2024 to the first half of 2025, discontinuations and acquittals made up 24% (200 of 827).[^nil-prawomocne] There is one revocation of the licence to practise in the catalogue. The game draws outcomes so that their distribution is close to the NIL data; the catalogue shows all the cases described.

A revised Code of Medical Ethics has been in force since 1 January 2025.[^kel] The rulings in the catalogue cite articles of the code in the wording from before that change, and the case descriptions give them that way.
