In Polish debates about healthcare, Taiwan keeps coming up as an example of a single-payer system in which you can see a specialist without a referral, know the copayment at the counter in advance, and every visit leaves an electronic trace. We describe how this system works according to Taiwanese legislation and government data, and check what is public in it. The patient receives a receipt showing the amount claimed from the insurer, hospitals’ financial reports are published, and the state knows where every doctor works. What a specific doctor earns, Taiwan does not disclose.

## A single payer, contributions based on income

Universal health insurance (National Health Insurance, NHI) has operated in Taiwan since 1995. It is run by a single public institution — the National Health Insurance Administration (NHIA). According to the ministry of health, more than 99.9% of the people entitled to it are insured.[^mohw30]

An employee pays 30% of the contribution, the employer 60% and the state 10%. The same contribution covers dependent family members, but for each of them — up to three people — the employee pays their share again. With a full family, they therefore pay four times as much as a single person.[^ustawa-skladka] Since 2013 there has also been a supplementary premium, now 2.11%, levied on bonuses, fees, dividends, interest and rental income.[^ar-skladka]

In 2023, TWD 836.4 billion was allocated to healthcare services, for a population of 23.4 million.[^budzet2023]

## A global budget and points instead of prices

The system is based on a global budget, introduced sector by sector: from dentistry in 1998 to hospitals in 2002.[^ar-budzet] Facilities bill services in points from a single national fee schedule. What a point is worth becomes clear only after settlement: the sector’s budget is divided by the sum of points claimed by all facilities.[^ustawa-62]

<PointValue />

The point value is calculated separately for each sector — hospitals, clinics, dentistry and Chinese medicine — and for each of the six regions. If more services were provided in a region than the budget assumed, all facilities in the sector lose, because each of their “floating” points is worth less. In hospitals the average annual point value in 2023–2025 ranged from TWD 0.95 to 0.98,[^punkty-srednie] but in some sectors and regions it is clearly lower: in Q4 2023 a clinic point in the Taipei region was worth TWD 0.813, and a Chinese medicine point in the Northern region TWD 0.694.[^punkty] In hospitals almost half of the points (47% in Q4 2024) have a fixed value — among them medicines, surgery and some emergency services — so the fluctuations affect the rest.[^punkty-szpitale]

Since 2025 there has been one more level. In a pilot run in all regions, each hospital gets its own budget, agreed in advance. The basis is the hospital’s revenue from the same quarter a year earlier, converted at a point value set in advance for that quarter. Points above the budget are paid at progressively lower rates — in the Central region at TWD 0.75, 0.5 and 0.25 in turn, and beyond the third threshold not at all; new hospitals received nothing for the excess.[^prospektywne] A hospital thus knows its limit, and the reduction hits mainly whoever exceeds it.

The global budget keeps payments to facilities in check, but it does not guarantee the balance of the fund itself. The NHI closed the years 2017–2021 with a loss (TWD −67.6 billion in 2020). The years 2022–2024 brought surpluses — after a contribution increase and thanks to subsidies from the state budget. For 2027 the ministry estimates a gap of TWD 55.9–84.3 billion, and the reserve is approaching the statutory minimum, equivalent to one month of benefits.[^deficyt]

Decisions on how the money is divided are made in the open. The budget for the following year is negotiated by the 39-member NHI Committee: the payers — the insured, employers and the government — hold at least half of the seats, the providers ten. The agenda must be published 7 days before a meeting and the minutes within 10 days after it.[^komitet] The government sets the range of growth (for 2027 from 2.619 to 5.5%),[^widelki] and if the parties fail to agree, the minister of health decides.[^ustawa-61]

The fee schedule also discourages seeing very many patients a day, because the fee for a consultation decreases with their number. For each of the first 30 consultations per doctor per day a clinic bills 364 points, for consultations 31 to 40 — 250, 41 to 60 — 220, 61 to 80 — 160, 81 to 150 — 70, and above 150 — 50 points.[^degresja] The rule applies to the consultation itself, not to tests or medicines.

## What the patient pays

No referral is needed to see a specialist. The copayment depends on the level of the facility and on whether the patient has a referral — the most is paid by those who go to a large hospital without one.[^doplaty]

<Copays />

On top of the copayment for the visit there is a copayment for medicines. In medical centres and regional hospitals it is about 20% of their value, up to TWD 300. In clinics and local hospitals medicines costing up to TWD 100 carry no copayment, and for more expensive ones the copayment does not exceed TWD 200; medicines for chronic conditions prescribed at a clinic carry no copayment.[^doplaty]

According to the NHIA, copayments are meant to discourage treating a paid contribution as a subscription to unlimited treatment, but they must not cut people off from treatment they really need. The higher rates in large hospitals are meant to steer minor complaints to clinics.[^cel-doplat] A facility may not waive or reduce the copayment. If it does not collect it as the act requires, the NHIA gives it a penalty point, and after three points a further violation means suspension of its NHI contract for a month.[^doplata-obowiazkowa]

A separate matter is the registration fee, which is not part of the insurance at all. From 2010 the ministry recommended that it be TWD 0–150 for a clinic visit and TWD 0–300 at the emergency department. In 2024 it dropped this recommendation: it considered the fee administrative rather than medical, so the limit had no legal basis. Local health authorities are instead to make sure that price lists are public and that facilities do not set prices jointly, do not exploit a local monopoly in remote areas and do not charge medical fees under another name. In the 26 hospitals run by the ministry the fee was then usually up to TWD 100, and some of them charged none at all.[^rejestracyjna] Public health stations (衛生所) charge little or nothing: TWD 50 in New Taipei, while in Nantou County registration is free.[^osrodki]

At a clinic counter, then, one usually pays the copayment and the registration fee. With registration at TWD 150 — the upper limit of the former range — a visit costs TWD 200, of which only TWD 50 is the copayment under the insurance. At a local hospital in Taichung, a visit without a referral with medicines costing just over TWD 300 means TWD 80 of copayment, TWD 60 of medicine copayment and TWD 100 for registration — TWD 240 in total. We break down this receipt in the next section.[^cennik]

For a hospital stay the patient covers part of the costs: on an acute ward 10% for the first 30 days, 20% from day 31 to day 60 and 30% thereafter. In 2025 the cap was TWD 51,000 for a single stay and TWD 86,000 for the whole year.[^hospitalizacja] Copayments are not paid by, among others, people with serious illnesses and injuries, such as cancer, or by women giving birth.[^zwolnienia]

An extra charge for the room itself is allowed only in single and double rooms, and beds without extra charges must make up at least 75% of beds in public hospitals and 60% in the others. In a large hospital, a day in a single room costs, for example, an extra TWD 4,000.[^sale] Such expenses can be covered by a private policy — according to the Commonwealth Fund it pays out money in case of illness, but does not speed up access to specialists.[^cw] How many residents have a health insurance policy is not known; in 2023, 70.88% of the population had a life insurance policy.[^polisy]

## A receipt for every visit

Since 2010 the receipt for every visit must separate items billed to the insurance from those paid privately, and in the insurance part show separately the patient’s copayment and the amount the facility claims from the NHI.[^rachunek] This is what a receipt from a visit to a neurologist at a local hospital looks like:[^rachunek-przyklad]

<Receipt />

The same information — since 2022 in five categories for an outpatient visit and seventeen for a hospital stay — is shown by the government “health passbook” app (健康存摺). By the end of August 2026 a total of 12.76 million people had used it.[^ksiazeczka] The app shows points; how much the facility ultimately received depends on the point value after the quarter is settled.

A Polish patient has similar data in the Internet Patient Account (IKP): visits reimbursed by the National Health Fund (NFZ) since 2008, together with the cost of reimbursement ([how to check](/en/mechanisms/visits-that-never-happened/)). The difference is that in Taiwan the patient gets this breakdown at every visit, because the law requires it. In neither country, however, will the patient learn what part of the amount went to the doctor who treated them.

## The insurance card and a shared treatment history

The chip-based insurance card has been in use since 1 January 2004.[^karta-ic] At every visit the facility records the visit on it with a sequential number and within 24 hours sends the data to the NHIA — together with the identifier of the health professional who provided the service.[^karta-przepisy] The NHIA matches these records against the claims. A facility that does not record visits receives a demand to correct this; there are separate codes for a reader failure or a patient without a card.[^karta-sankcja]

<NhiCard />

The cards also underpin MediCloud, the central database of treatment history. A doctor opens it after authenticating with three cards: the patient’s card, their own health professional card and the facility card, and the patient can block access with a password. Since 2020 an emergency department doctor can look up a patient by ID number in urgent cases (triage categories 1–3); the NHIA logs every such query.[^medicloud]

The shared history also serves to limit duplicated medicines and tests. When two facilities prescribe the same medicine, the NHIA deducts its cost once a quarter if the duplicates at a given facility reach TWD 1,000 (a supply of up to 10 days does not count).[^duble-leki] Since 2019 the NHIA has not paid for CT or MRI scans ordered at an outpatient clinic if the doctor did not first review the patient’s previous images in the database; a justified repeat scan is allowed.[^duble-ct]

Like any system that holds medical data, Taiwan’s is also a target of attacks. In February 2025 ransomware disabled more than 500 computers in the outpatient clinics and the emergency department of Mackay hospital.[^mackay] The attacker later offered data said to come from that hospital; according to reports, their origin was not confirmed.[^mackay-dane] We found no reports of a breach of the MediCloud database itself.

## The state knows where a doctor works

A doctor in Taiwan has one registered place of practice.[^jedna-placowka] Outside it, they may work in emergencies, for consultations and support of other facilities, when called in, and in other situations — after notification and with the authorities’ consent. Regular scheduled work in a second hospital requires such consent; without it the doctor faces a fine of TWD 20,000–100,000.[^lekarz-miejsce] The notified hours count towards the staffing standards of the hospital where the doctor also works: 44 hours a week count as one doctor.[^normy-44]

Working in several places is therefore legal, but the state knows about it in advance. In Poland nobody compiles how much one doctor received in total from public funds and how many hours they reported across all their workplaces ([more on this mechanism](/en/mechanisms/multiple-jobs-parallel-contracts/)). This is one of the reasons why we call for [public total payments to each person, under a permanent identifier and without names](/en/demand/).

Hospitals also have minimum staffing standards: at least one doctor per 10 beds and a specialist in every ward.[^normy] Since 2019 there have been standards for the number of patients per nurse, and every hospital publishes its ratio monthly. Since 2026 the standards for each shift have been written into the act, with fines of up to TWD 2 million; a hospital that meets them receives a 10% higher NHI fee for nursing care.[^pielegniarki]

## Pay: averages for the profession and hospital reports

Doctors’ earnings are described mainly by data from the ministry of labour. In the survey of wages by occupation for 2025, employed doctors (excluding practice owners) had a regular monthly wage of about TWD 180,000, and for the whole of 2024 they earned TWD 2.497 million.[^mol] That is about 3.8 times the average regular wage, which in 2025 was TWD 47,884 a month.[^dgbas]

Taiwan does not publish the pay of individual doctors. When in 2025 the ministry of health announced the average pay of nurses reported by 445 hospitals, the director of the responsible department explained that pay is personal data and that the pay of specific people is unlikely to be published.[^pielegniarki-place]

Hospital finances, on the other hand, are public. Since 2013 a facility that receives more than a set threshold a year from the insurance has had to file a financial report on its NHI activity, audited by a certified public accountant or a government audit body, and the NHIA must publish it.[^art73] The threshold was lowered from TWD 600 million to 400 million, and since 2018 it has been TWD 200 million. For 2024 the reports of 240 facilities were published.[^art73-2024]

In these reports staff costs are a single figure, with no breakdown into doctors, nurses and other staff. In 2017 the NHIA proposed a table with such a breakdown, but the rules did not change.[^art73-2017] A Taiwanese foundation for healthcare reform points out that the totals alone do not show whether hospitals hired more people or raised pay.[^cna] The median share of staff costs in the costs of medical activity was 46.6% in 2023; the indicator does not include management costs, and in university hospitals part of the salaries comes from the university’s budget.[^art73-466]

Hospital doctors are usually employees, but a large part of their pay depends on the number of services. In private hospitals a fixed salary plus a fee for services (the so-called physician fee) is typical; in a nationwide survey in 1998, the variable part made up more than half of pay in 80% of hospitals.[^pf-1998] The rules for dividing it are set by the hospital, which usually does not disclose them. In one large hospital group whose formula came to light in 2020, a doctor received about 70% of the consultation fee and 60% of the surgery fee. The consultation fee, however, is only part of what the NHI pays for a visit — according to the same report, 5–13% of the total amount paid by the NHI for an outpatient went to the doctor.[^pf-2020]

## Medical education, number of doctors and working hours

Since 1998 total admissions to medicine have been 1,300 students a year, from 2025 including the military university.[^1300] That is 5.6 places per 100,000 population. In Poland the limit for the 2025/26 academic year is 10,504 places, or about 28 per 100,000 (including 1,888 in programmes taught in a foreign language).[^limit-pl] The number of specialist training places in each specialty and each hospital has been approved every year by the ministry of health since 2001; the ministry itself describes this model as controlling limits at the stages of education, examination, training and employment.[^rezydentury]

Taiwan has 24.2 practising doctors of Western medicine per 10,000 population (2025),[^rocznik] Poland 38.5 doctors working with patients.[^gus] Both figures refer to people, not full-time posts.

Doctors of traditional Chinese medicine form a separate group: 8,234 people, or 3.5 per 10,000 population.[^rocznik] Chinese medicine is covered by the insurance — including acupuncture and treatment of injuries[^tcm] — with a copayment of TWD 50 regardless of the level of the facility,[^doplaty] and since 2000 it has had its own global budget.[^ar-budzet]

Doctors work long hours. In 2018 an attending physician in a Taiwanese hospital worked on average 69.1 hours a week, and in surgical specialties 79–85 hours.[^godziny] Attending physicians are not covered by the labour law; residents have been covered by it since 2019, and ministry guidelines limit their working time to 320 hours per four weeks.[^godziny-prawo] As early as 2015 the ministry of health pointed to staff shortages in five major specialties: internal medicine, surgery, obstetrics and gynaecology, paediatrics and emergency medicine.[^piec-specjalnosci] A report prepared for the World Economic Forum notes that nobody regularly collects data on doctors’ actual working hours and pay.[^wef]

We found no official statistics on waiting times for an appointment, a test or a procedure; only the occupancy of emergency departments is published in real time.[^sor-live] An appointment with a specialist in a hospital can be booked online, or the patient can come without a booking and join the end of that day’s queue. According to Rafał, who lives in Taiwan, the wait for very popular doctors is several days or even weeks, and for other specialists much shorter.[^relacja] According to press reports from 2026, the wait for a CT scan at large medical centres ranged from 1–1.5 months to as long as a year, partly because of the popularity of free screening.[^ct]

## Professional liability

A practising doctor must belong to a local medical association, but other bodies rule on their professional liability.[^stowarzyszenie] Cases are heard by disciplinary committees appointed by city or county authorities, and appeals by a committee at the ministry of health. At least one third of the members must be lawyers and people from outside the profession, and elected politicians may not sit on them. Revoking the licence to practise requires a two-thirds majority. Hearings are closed, but decisions are published in the official gazette, and the ministry runs a search engine for decisions from the last five years.[^dyscyplina] We found no annual statistics on sanctions. For comparison: [how it works in Poland](/en/analysis/doctor-discipline-licence-revocation/).

## Taiwan in international comparisons

Taiwan is not a member of the OECD or the WHO, so it is usually missing from comparisons based on their data. It is ranked first by Numbeo — whose rankings are based on surveys of the website’s visitors — and by CEOWORLD.[^rankingi] According to Taiwanese data compiled by the Commonwealth Fund, the country spent 7.3% of GDP on health in 2023, and life expectancy was 80.2 years.[^cw]
