A doctor who works in a public hospital in the morning and sees patients privately in the afternoon breaks no rule in Poland. The trouble starts where both workplaces meet the same patient: the queue in one is shortened in the other, and where the patient ends up is decided by a person who earns on both sides. We checked how fifteen countries regulate this situation, whether the state knows how many doctors it concerns, and what part of it is public.

## Four approaches

The countries we describe use one of four models — from separating the sectors to having no rules.

## Choosing one side: Canada

Canada separates the sectors most sharply. A doctor participating in a province’s public plan bills only the plan and may not charge the patient extra. The doctor can opt out of the plan — but then the plan does not pay for the doctor’s services, or reimburses the patient at most the rate from the fee schedule. Outside Quebec only a handful do so: in 2024–25 Ontario had 37,442 doctors in the plan and 10 outside it, and British Columbia 13,385 and 24. The figures are given every year in a federal report; Quebec did not submit its own.[^ca-cha]

Quebec, where there are more doctors outside the plan, restricted departures by an act in April 2025: according to the provincial government’s announcement, new doctors are to work in the public network for five years, and moving to the private sector requires the consent of a public agency.[^ca-loi83]

## Permission and exclusivity: Spain, Hungary, Italy, France

**Spain** requires permission. An act of 1984 requires permissions to be entered in the personnel register and bans private activity connected with the doctor’s own unit, and working without permission is a very serious disciplinary offence.[^es-ley53] [^es-estatuto] The transparency act requires such permissions to be published, and Madrid does so by name — with the doctor’s name, position and hospital.[^es-ley19] [^es-madryt]

**Hungary** introduced permission in 2021. A doctor employed in a state or local-government hospital may take up additional gainful work only with the prior permission of a designated body, which has eight days to decide; working without applying may lead to dismissal with immediate effect. Within the public post, a doctor also may not treat a patient whom the doctor previously treated for the same illness outside that post.[^hu-eszjtv] [^hu-528] We found no figure for the number of permissions granted.

**Italy** gives a doctor of the public health service a choice: exclusivity with a pay supplement, or practice outside the hospital. A doctor with exclusivity may see private patients in their own hospital, after hours, and the hospital takes part of the fee. The health minister submits a report to parliament every year: in 2023, 37.9% of employed doctors used this option, and revenue amounted to €1,286.1 million, of which €1,000.0 million went to staff.[^it-alpi] The report gives the total amount, not the earnings of individual doctors.

**France** allows doctors employed in a public hospital to practise privately in the same hospital on three conditions: the doctor performs the same kind of work as part of the public service, the private practice takes up no more than 20% of working time, and the number of private consultations and procedures is lower than the number of public ones. France’s audit office (Cour des comptes) found in 2023 that these restrictions are easy to circumvent — because of insufficient oversight and tolerance of frequent abuses.[^fr-cdc] The same three conditions still apply.[^fr-hcaam] In 2022, 4,671 doctors had a private practice in a public hospital, and their fees amounted to €644 million.[^fr-hcaam]

## Notification: the United Kingdom, Germany, Denmark, Sweden, Czechia

In this group the doctor has a duty to inform the employer, and the employer may object.

In the **United Kingdom** a consultant must disclose commitments to private patients to the employer, but does not need permission.[^uk-kontrakt] [^uk-bma] Separately, the competition authority ordered in 2014 that consultants in private practice submit their prices for consultations and procedures to an independent organisation, which publishes them.[^uk-cma]

In **Germany** the collective agreement for municipal hospitals requires written notification of a paid occupation in advance; the employer may prohibit it or impose conditions.[^de-tv] A doctor contracted by the sickness funds, in turn, may not take on additional work to an extent that prevents them from seeing the funds’ patients.[^de-zv]

In **Denmark** the duty to inform arises from a collective agreement and applies to chief physicians, senior doctors and ward doctors. The national audit office established in 2016 that two of the three regions examined did not enforce it, and that nearly half of the senior doctors heading departments had secondary occupations despite a ban in force in two regions.[^dk-rigs]

In **Sweden** the act bans a public employee from occupations that may undermine trust in their impartiality, and the employer has a duty to prohibit such an occupation.[^se-loa] In 2023 the auditors of the Västerbotten region checked the company register: 378 of the region’s 1,547 doctors held a position or shares in a company. In a sample of 60 such cases, 13% had been notified and documented.[^se-rev] The register shows shareholdings and board positions, not private practice itself, and concerns one region.

In **Czechia** the labour code requires the employer’s written consent only for gainful activity that coincides with the employer’s line of business.[^cz-kp]

## No statutory rules: Poland

In **Poland** the act on medical activity and the act on the professions of doctor and dentist do not require a doctor to obtain the employer’s permission for, or to notify the employer of, work in another place.[^pl-udl] [^pl-uzl] The exception is the head of a public facility, who needs the written consent of the founding body for any other employment.[^pl-udl] The labour code goes in the opposite direction: an employer may not prohibit an employee from working in parallel, unless they have concluded a separate non-compete agreement.[^pl-kp] Doctors working on civil-law (B2B) contracts are not covered by the labour code.

Nobody measures the scale. The only data come from a 2026 survey by the medical chamber, carried out on a sample that is not random: 30.8% of respondents worked only in the public sector, 11.9% only in the private sector, and 54.9% combined the two. Among the problems of such combining, doctors pointed to adding patients out of turn (41.4%) and the use of public resources (35.4%).[^pl-nil] Since August 2026 the health minister has been able to demand from facilities data on the pay and working time of specific persons, but only on request and without publication.[^pl-972]

## A different divide: the USA, Austria, the Netherlands, Belgium

In the **USA** the same doctor sees patients of public programmes and of private insurers, so the question of combining sectors makes no sense there. What is regulated instead is the conflict of interest: an act bans a doctor from referring patients of the Medicare programme to an entity with which the doctor has a financial relationship. The federal audit office established on 2010 data that doctors referring to their own diagnostic facilities ordered about twice as many MRI and CT scans as the others.[^us-stark] [^us-gao]

In **Austria**, alongside doctors with a sickness fund contract there are practices without one: the patient pays in full and gets part of the cost reimbursed by the fund. In 2023 patients submitted bills for €551.45 million to the largest fund, of which the fund reimbursed €208.85 million.[^at-rh] We did not establish how many hospital doctors run such practices.

For the **Netherlands** and **Belgium** we found neither rules nor data on how many hospital doctors work in parallel in competing clinics.

The countries described do not ban doctors from private work as such — Canada goes furthest, making them choose one side. They differ in whether anyone knows about that work. Where permission or a choice of one side is required, a register comes into being, and statistics can be made from a register. Where notifying the employer is enough, audits in Denmark and Sweden showed that the notifications are often simply not there.

Poland is among the countries with neither permission, nor notification, nor statistics. This site’s demand — [total pay from public money under a permanent identifier instead of a name](/en/demand/) — does not concern private earnings. It would, however, show how much work a doctor does at the public payer’s expense, and that is the first figure needed for a discussion about combining the two sectors. We describe the mechanism in the texts on [working in many places](/en/mechanisms/multiple-jobs-parallel-contracts/) and on [the queue and the price of a private visit](/en/analysis/private-visits-queues-and-prices/).

## Method

For every country we checked four things: what rule follows from an act or a collective agreement, whether the state or the payer knows the scale of the phenomenon, what is published and what audit bodies have established. We rely on the texts of acts, the reports of audit offices and public authorities, and reports to parliaments. Where we write “we found no data”, we are not claiming that none exist — we did not manage to reach an official source. We describe the countries at greater length in the texts collected on the page [Healthcare in other countries](/en/countries/).

The assessment of oversight in France comes from a report of 2023; the conditions themselves are confirmed by a report of 2025. The data on Denmark come from an audit of 2016.
