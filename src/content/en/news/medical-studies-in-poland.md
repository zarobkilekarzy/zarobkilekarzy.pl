There is a new analysis on the site: [Medical studies in Poland: from the matura to the LEK](/en/analysis/medical-studies-in-poland/). It describes the whole path — admission, six years of study, the postgraduate internship and specialisation — and compares medicine with law, psychology, architecture, mathematics, physics and computer science on the same measures.

## What it shows

- **Medicine is hard to get into because there are far more applicants than places.** At the University of Warsaw in 2026 there were 32.8 applicants per place, and the cut-off was 79.83 points — lower than in computer science, mathematics, physics, psychology and law.
- **Fewer students drop out than in many other programmes.** 81.3% of medical students move on to the second year on time, compared with 73.3% in law and 42.3% in mathematics.
- **What sets medicine apart is its length:** six years and, in the Medical University of Warsaw programme, 5,760 hours and 30 exams.
- **39 universities now teach medicine**, compared with 12 up to 2014/15. Neither cut-off scores nor LEK results show a split between old and new ones.
- **Paid studies cost from PLN 40,000 to 69,500 a year** at a public university. The state lends up to PLN 22,000 per semester for tuition and writes the loan off for doctors who work 10 years in the public system and become specialists in a priority field.

## What you can check yourself

The analysis includes an anatomy model with pins, as in the practical exam, the study plan year by year with every subject, and a video from an anatomy lab with VR headsets. Every number is sourced in the notes.
