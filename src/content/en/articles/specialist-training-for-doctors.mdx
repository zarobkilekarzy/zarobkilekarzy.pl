After the internship and the Medical Final Examination (LEK) a doctor may practise independently. To become a surgeon, a cardiologist or a family doctor, they still have to complete specialist training. That means four to six years of work in a hospital or clinic under a programme that lists courses, placements on other wards, on-call duty and the procedures the doctor must perform independently. Training ends with the State Specialisation Examination.

## Recruitment: a ranking based on the LEK result

There is no entrance exam for specialist training. Applications are submitted in the Training Monitoring System (SMK) twice a year, in February and in September, and list up to 15 options, i.e. combinations of field, voivodeship and mode.[^uzl] When there are more applicants than places, the order is set by a ranking. For a residency, what counts is the LEK result, up to 200 points, and a doctorate and publications can add at most 10 points more. In a tie, the average grade from the studies decides, and then the order in which applications were submitted.[^uzl][^rozp]

For the autumn 2026 recruitment, the list of the Centre of Postgraduate Medical Education (CMKP) provides **6,530** residency places and **7,752** non-residency places.[^cmkp-j26] Overall there are more places than applicants: in spring 2026, 52% of residency places and 89% of non-residency places remained empty.[^cmkp-w26] In the most sought-after fields, however, the ranking decides who gets a place. We describe the cut-off scores in the analysis [Restricted access to the medical profession](/en/mechanisms/restricted-access-to-the-profession/#the-ranking-what-counts-is-the-percentage-of-the-maximum), and the number of places and applicants in individual fields in our overview [by specialty](/en/data/residents-pay-by-specialty/).

## 77 fields, from four to six years

The Minister of Health’s regulation lists **{FIELDS.all}** medical specialties. Training is modular. In {FIELDS.single} fields, such as family medicine, psychiatry and anaesthesiology, it is a single module. In {FIELDS.modular} it begins with a basic module shared by several fields, followed by a specialist module. There are five basic modules: general surgery, internal medicine, otorhinolaryngology, pathomorphology and paediatrics. The remaining {FIELDS.afterOther} fields, such as clinical transplantology and paediatric urology, can be started only after obtaining another specialty.[^rozp] A doctor who completes a basic module can move to a related field without starting from scratch, if they qualify in a later recruitment round.[^uzl]

Dentists have a separate list of nine specialties, such as orthodontics, periodontology and paediatric dentistry. Three of them — maxillofacial surgery, epidemiology and public health — are shared with the medical list.[^rozp] This text is about medical specialties, but the CMKP and the Medical Examinations Centre (CEM) report the number of places and the exam results together with dentists.

### Is 77 a lot

The number of specialties depends on what counts as one. In Germany the model regulation of the German Medical Association lists 51 medical specialties and, within them, further subspecialties, such as paediatric cardiology within paediatrics.[^de] The Czech act lists 43 specialties, and a regulation another 46 fields that can be obtained only after a specialty.[^cz] In Spain the rules list 43 specialties for doctors.[^es] The Polish list combines both levels: paediatric cardiology is a separate specialty here, which begins with the basic module in paediatrics.[^rozp]

In Sweden, alongside 44 basic specialties, the rules list 9 subspecialties and 10 additional specialties, which require a basic specialty.[^se] So Poland has the longest list of specialties that can be started without another specialty, while Czechia has more once subspecialties are included.

The start of the path differs too. In Czechia, training takes place in the chosen field from the first day: it begins with a common trunk shared by several fields, which counts towards the length of the specialty.[^cz] In Poland, specialist training is preceded by a 13-month [postgraduate internship](/en/analysis/medical-studies-in-poland/#postgraduate-internship) on several wards.

## The programme: courses, placements and procedures

Specialist training is work under a programme. The programme for each field is drawn up at least once every five years by a team of experts that includes the national consultant, a representative of the scientific society and representatives of the Supreme Medical Council, one of them a doctor in training. The CMKP edits the programme and the minister of health approves it.[^uzl] The act lists what the programme consists of: an introductory course in the first year, specialty courses, a basic placement with a set number of procedures, specialty placements on other wards, on-call duty, self-study and an attestation course at the end.[^uzl]

In surgery, one of them lasts eight weeks on a general surgery ward other than the doctor’s home ward and is recommended in the fourth year.[^chir] In specialties with a basic module in general surgery, internal medicine or paediatrics, at least six months of training must take place in a hospital of the first or second tier of the basic level of healthcare provision.[^uzl]

### Courses and assessment

The courses in the programme are run by institutions on the CMKP list, and doctors can sign up only through the SMK. The facility where the doctor trains must release them for a course they have been accepted for.[^uzl] In the four programmes in the table, courses last from one to ten days. Every course ends with a test and every placement with an oral or written assessment. In general surgery, before the attestation course the organiser also checks knowledge from the whole training.[^chir][^int][^kard][^rodz] Assessments are confirmed by the specialisation supervisor or the placement supervisor, and the doctor is obliged to keep the electronic training record in the SMK up to date.[^uzl][^chir]

The four programmes described do not say who pays for the courses. In 12 fields, including internal medicine, family medicine, paediatrics and psychiatry, the CMKP is running the FERS1 project in 2024–2029, worth more than <Money value={135000000} scale="million" decimals={0} />. It covers about 2,500 compulsory specialty courses and co-financing of travel, accommodation and meals for courses and placements away from the doctor’s place of residence.[^fers]

Apart from courses and placements, each of the four programmes provides for 6 days a year of self-study, for example conferences, and requires a research or review paper published in a peer-reviewed journal. In family medicine the doctor must also prepare and run classes, for example for residents or students.[^chir][^int][^kard][^rodz]

## On-call duty

A doctor in specialist training works full-time and is on call for the number of hours set in the programme.[^uzl] The programmes for general surgery, internal medicine and cardiology provide for on-call duty “for an average of 10 hours 5 minutes a week” or shift work, up to an average of 48 hours a week in total.[^chir][^int][^kard] The family medicine programme does not specify on-call duty.[^rodz]

During the basic placement a doctor is on “accompanied” duty, under the direct supervision of a specialist or another doctor authorised to be on call independently, or on independent duty if the specialisation supervisor agrees in the SMK. During a specialty placement at another facility, duty is accompanied only. For the first year of training a doctor may refuse independent duty, but they then cannot do it under contracts at other facilities either, except for night and holiday medical care.[^uzl] Duty under the programme is paid like overtime or, at the doctor’s request, compensated with time off. When on-call duty does not allow for 11 hours of uninterrupted rest, the rest is due immediately afterwards and does not extend the training.[^uzl]

A doctor may do more on-call duty than the programme requires at their own facility under the opt-out clause, that is, written consent to work more than an average of 48 hours a week. This requires the consent of the specialisation supervisor, and rest after on-call duty may not exceed an average of five working days a month. A resident receives no salary for those days.[^uzl][^udl96] The opt-out clause is due to expire on 1 January 2028.[^udl96] How on-site duty differs from standby “on the phone”, and what it costs a hospital, we describe in the analysis [On-call duty in a county hospital](/en/analysis/county-hospital-on-call-duty/).

## The specialisation supervisor

Every doctor trains under a specialisation supervisor appointed by the head of the facility. The supervisor must hold a second-degree specialisation or the title of specialist in the field, or, in justified cases, in a related field. One supervisor oversees at most three doctors at a time, and four with the consent of the national consultant when staffing needs require it. They may also oversee the specialty placement of two more. For this they receive an allowance: **<Money value={500} />** gross a month for one doctor or **<Money value={1000} />** for more than one.[^uzl]

The supervisor sets the doctor’s detailed training plan, including where courses and placements take place. Until the supervisor gives a written opinion that the doctor may treat patients independently, the doctor provides care under supervision — the supervisor’s or that of another specialist.[^uzl] Only facilities accredited by the CMKP may provide training. How accreditation and the limit on supervisors restrict the number of places we describe in the analysis [Restricted access to the medical profession](/en/mechanisms/restricted-access-to-the-profession/#specialisation-three-limits-on-one-place).

### Supervision in practice

Nobody measures how much time a supervisor devotes to the doctors they supervise. It is not even known how many supervisors there are. In 2016 the Ministry of Health estimated about 6,000–8,000, but it worked this out from the statutory limit on the number of doctors per supervisor, not from data.[^mz1995]

The rules allow accompanied on-call duty to be supervised not only by a specialist but also by “another doctor authorised to be on call independently”.[^uzl] This can be, for example, a more senior colleague in training who already has consent to be on call independently. How often this happens is not known: no study we have found asked about it.

There are individual findings. An audit by the Supreme Audit Office (NIK) of anaesthesiology and intensive care wards in the Podkarpackie region found that in two hospitals doctors in training treated patients without the supervision of a specialist in the field. In one of them, supervision was provided by a specialist on standby “on the phone”, which in NIK’s view did not meet the supervision requirement.[^nik22] In a survey by the Residents’ Agreement of the OZZL trade union among doctors training in psychiatry, 44% said that a specialist is present at the facility during 24-hour on-call duty, and almost 20% that one never is. The survey was completed by 825 people who chose to take part, so the result is not representative.[^pr-psych]

### A supervisor with no minimum working time

The act sets out how many doctors a supervisor may oversee and how large an allowance they receive for it. **It does not set out how much time they must have for those doctors, and it does not require them to work at the training facility for any minimum number of hours.** They may have an employment contract or a civil-law contract there for any number of hours.[^uzl]

The problem has been known since at least 2014. NIK described an audit by a voivode (the government’s regional representative) in which three periodontologists employed half-time supervised the training of four doctors. The facility refused to make changes, and a department of the Ministry of Health replied that the role of supervisor “should not be held by a doctor employed for fewer hours than the doctor training under them”. The rules, however, do not require this.[^nik16-42]

This is to change under a draft amendment to the act prepared by the Ministry of Health. A supervisor would have to work at the training facility at least half-time, and the monthly allowance would be replaced by an award paid once a year, on condition that the supervisor has filled in the electronic training record.[^ud384] The government plans to adopt the draft in the fourth quarter of 2026; for now it is a draft, not law in force.[^ud384-wykaz] The Presidium of the Supreme Medical Council is calling for the whole draft to be rejected. In its view, the half-time requirement would eliminate a large group of current supervisors, especially dentists. The Presidium acknowledges that the role “requires a considerable commitment of time” and proposes an allowance of <Money value={1000} /> a month for each doctor.[^nrl6]

## Residency and non-residency modes

Specialist training can be completed in two modes. They differ in who pays the doctor and under what contract. The programme and the examination at the end are the same in both modes.[^uzl] As of {IN_TRAINING.date}, **{formatEN(IN_TRAINING.all)}** doctors and dentists were in specialist training, {formatEN(IN_TRAINING.all - IN_TRAINING.in2015)} more than at the end of 2015.[^mz13211] Most train on a residency: in mid-2022 — the latest split between the modes we have found — {formatEN(IN_TRAINING_2022.resident)} of {formatEN(IN_TRAINING_2022.all)} people were on a residency and {formatEN(IN_TRAINING_2022.nonResident)} in the non-residency mode.[^nik23]

### Residency

A resident doctor has an employment contract with the facility for the time needed to complete the programme, and the salary is financed by the minister of health from the state budget.[^uzl] From 1 July 2026 a resident’s basic salary is <Money value={RESIDENT_PAY.other.first} decimals={2} /> gross a month, and in one of the {PRIORITY_FIELDS.length} priority fields <Money value={RESIDENT_PAY.priority.first} decimals={2} />. After two years it rises to <Money value={RESIDENT_PAY.other.later} decimals={2} /> and <Money value={RESIDENT_PAY.priority.later} decimals={2} />.[^rez-placa] The minister reimburses a resident’s on-call duty up to the overtime rate calculated from the basic salary, and the hospital where the doctor was on call pays the rest of the cost.[^uzl]

In 2025 the minister of health spent **<Money value={BUDGET.spent * 1000} scale="billion" decimals={2} />** under the budget chapter “Internships and medical specialisations”. According to the ministry, this money went on residency training for doctors and dentists as well as nurses and midwives; it financed the training of 28,300 people.[^nik26][^zdr149] The budget act provided <Money value={BUDGET.planned * 1000} scale="billion" decimals={2} /> for this purpose, so {formatEN((BUDGET.spent / BUDGET.planned) * 100, 1)}% of the plan was spent.[^nik26] The ministry explains that it plans spending as if all advertised places were filled and nobody were absent, and that it moved the unspent money to the National Health Fund (NFZ).[^zdr149] For 2026 the budget provides <Money value={BUDGET.plan2026 * 1000} scale="billion" decimals={2} />.[^ub2026] How much of this goes on salaries, and how much on on-call duty and social contributions, the budget documents do not show.

A resident may also voluntarily commit to working in Poland for {COMMITMENT.years} years out of {COMMITMENT.window} after the specialty, at a facility that treats patients with public money. In return they receive **<Money value={COMMITMENT.other} />** more a month, and in a priority field **<Money value={COMMITMENT.priority} />**.[^uzl] A doctor who does not keep the commitment or stops following the training programme pays a penalty: 75% of the supplements received, reduced in proportion to the time worked in line with the commitment. A change of field under the rules in the act, or a change because of health contraindications, does not count as stopping the programme.[^uzl] For example, a doctor who received <Money value={COMMITMENT.priority} /> more for four years, <Money value={48 * COMMITMENT.priority} /> in total, and did not work a single day of the commitment repays <Money value={48 * COMMITMENT.priority * COMMITMENT.penaltyShare} />. Salaries in individual fields are shown in our overview [by specialty](/en/data/residents-pay-by-specialty/).

### Non-residency places

Non-residency places are the remaining free places at facilities authorised to provide training. They come with no salary from the minister of health’s pool. The act allows several forms: an employment contract or a full-time civil-law contract with the training facility, paid training leave from the doctor’s own employer, a scholarship from the facility or a civil-law training contract. The amount of pay is set by the contract. For a civil-law training contract, the act only requires that it be no lower than the minimum hourly rate — **<Money value={31.4} decimals={2} />** in 2026.[^uzl][^stawka] It is mainly these places that stay empty: in spring 2026, 89% of them were not filled.[^cmkp-w26]

### Interrupting training and returning to a residency

The act lists seven situations in which a doctor cannot continue training. They include: not starting training within three months of the date on the referral; “ceasing” training, meaning actually stopping work under the annual plan or making no entries in the electronic record for 12 months; and interruption of training at the request of the specialisation supervisor, after an opinion from the consultant and the regional medical chamber. The decision to remove a doctor from the register of doctors in training is taken by the voivode, and it is final.[^uzl]

A return to a residency is possible only to a limited extent. A doctor can change field once without giving a reason, and when changing to another basic or single module only in the first 12 months. After training is interrupted at the supervisor’s request for reasons beyond the doctor’s control, a doctor can apply for a residency once, but in a different field; the same applies to health contraindications confirmed by an occupational medicine doctor. A doctor who has three times been accepted for a residency and started training cannot apply again. Every new application goes through the regular recruitment and ranking.[^uzl]

Moving between the modes is less clear. Moving from a non-residency place to a residency goes through recruitment: after the lists of accepted doctors are published, the voivode enters the change of mode and a new end date for the training.[^uzl][^rozp] The act and the regulation describe a move in the other direction in only one case: a resident who does not manage to complete the programme during the contract may finish training in the non-residency mode with the voivode’s consent.[^uzl] What happens to a resident who wants to give up the residency but stay in training on a non-residency place, the rules do not say explicitly. The draft amendment also changes the procedure for interrupting training; the Presidium of the Supreme Medical Council considers the two-month deadline it proposes for the regional chamber’s opinion to be unworkable.[^nrl6]

### Work outside the residency

The Act on the professions of physician and dentist does not prohibit a doctor in specialist training from working at other facilities, for example on a contract, outside training hours. It limits this in two places. A doctor who refuses independent on-call duty in the first year cannot do it under civil-law contracts at other facilities either, except for night and holiday medical care. Additional on-call duty at the training facility, beyond the programme, requires the consent of the specialisation supervisor.[^uzl]

How far this can go was shown by a case from 2026. A Warsaw doctor — a resident doctor without a completed specialisation who coordinated a hospital emergency department (SOR) — worked simultaneously in four public facilities. In 2025 the doctor earned more than <Money value={1600000} scale="million" /> from self-employment, according to the doctor’s asset declaration.[^tvn] According to data from the hospital where the doctor coordinated the SOR, 3,976 hours of work were reported there over the year, an average of 331 hours a month.[^tvn][^rz-331] The amount is visible only because the doctor, as a local councillor, filed an asset declaration.[^tvn] We describe the case and its consequences in the text [Multiple jobs and parallel contracts](/en/mechanisms/multiple-jobs-parallel-contracts/).

This may change. The act on pay limits for medical staff, passed by the Sejm on 22 October 2026, limits total working time across all workplaces to two full-time posts, and makes further employment in a hospital conditional on the consent of the heads of the hospitals where the person already works. The act is now before the Senate.[^sprawozdanie][^dodatkowe] Details are in the analysis [Pay limits for Polish health professionals](/en/analysis/medical-pay-limits-act/).

## The specialty examination

A doctor can apply for the State Specialisation Examination (PES) after completing the penultimate year of training, with the consent of the specialisation supervisor, and can take it within five years of confirmation that training has been completed. It is organised twice a year by the CEM. It consists of a test with 120 questions with five answers each, passed with 60% of the points, and an oral exam. Those who score at least 75% in the test are exempt from the oral part. A doctor is entitled to 6 days of paid training leave to prepare for the exam, and from the third application the exam carries a fee.[^uzl]

## After specialist training

Six years of study, 13 months of postgraduate internship and four to six years of specialist training add up to 11 to 13 years from admission to medical school to the title of specialist, not counting breaks and waiting for a place.[^staz] What this means for the number of specialists in the coming years we describe in the analysis [Why Poland is short of doctors](/en/analysis/why-poland-is-short-of-doctors/). In 2025, 63.9% of doctors working directly with patients had a specialty.[^gus]

A specialist can work under an employment contract, on a contract or combine several contracts at different facilities. How these forms differ we describe in the texts [Contract or employment](/en/mechanisms/contract-vs-employment/) and [Multiple jobs and parallel contracts](/en/mechanisms/multiple-jobs-parallel-contracts/).

## What is not known

- **How many doctors complete specialist training each year.** The Ministry of Health does not say, because many doctors extend their training. As an approximation it gives the number of people who passed the PES: from 3,376 to 4,388 a year in 2016–2021, together with dentists.[^mz-pes]
- **How long training really takes.** Extensions are recorded in the SMK, but we have not found aggregate data on them.
- **How many doctors give up.** The last measurement comes from a NIK audit in 2016: 5.2% of those referred to a residency in the 2011–2014 recruitment rounds gave up.[^nik16]
- **How the oral exam goes.** The CEM publishes only the test results; data on the oral part appear in the reply to one parliamentary question, for two sessions.[^mz18220]

Where else the data on a doctor’s path stop we describe in the analysis [Who drops out of medical school in Poland, and who leaves the country](/en/analysis/medical-school-dropouts-and-emigration/#specialist-training-here-the-data-stop).
