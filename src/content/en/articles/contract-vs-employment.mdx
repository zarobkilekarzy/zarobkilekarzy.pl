Most specialist doctors do not work in hospital as employees but under a contract (*kontrakt*).[^aotmit][^hiredoc] This single difference decides whether it is possible at all to establish how much public money goes to one person — which is why it ties together the other mechanisms described on this site.

## Two forms of employment, two levels of transparency

Employment (*etat*) means an employment contract: the base salary cannot fall below a threshold set by an act, working time has limits, and the employee is entitled to paid leave and sick pay. A contract is a civil-law contract: the doctor issues an invoice as a business or is paid under a contract of mandate. The rate is agreed by the parties and nobody publishes it.

## Legal and often advantageous

A contract in itself is not an abuse. The doctor gains flexibility and a higher rate, the hospital — freedom in drawing up the rota. We do not judge the form of employment. We describe its side effect: public money disappears from view.

Salaried pay follows a public tariff and appears on the payroll. A contract is business revenue settled by invoices. Nobody collects these amounts in one place, and the same doctor may hold contracts with several facilities at once. As long as payments from public funds are not added up for each person, the total remains unknown.

<Aside title="“Contract” means two different agreements">

In everyday Polish, *kontrakt* refers both to an agreement with a doctor who runs a business (B2B, registered in the business register CEIDG) and to a contract of mandate (*umowa zlecenie*) or a contract for a specific work (*umowa o dzieło*) concluded with a private individual. Both have one thing in common: they do not appear in the register of salaried pay.

</Aside>

## Three reasons why the contract wins

73% is neither a fashion nor a coincidence. Three mechanisms push towards the contract, and none of them stems from bad faith on the part of the doctor.

## Six types of contract

The contract is not a single model. Prof. Urszula Demkow, a doctor and former deputy minister of health, distinguishes at least six, each linking payment to the work done in a different way. Her argument: the pathology is not the form of the agreement itself but the lack of transparency — pay detached from the time, cost and outcome of treatment.

Most often the models are mixed: an hourly rate plus on-call shifts, procedures, a lump sum for availability and a management allowance. When several mechanisms overlap, without close scrutiny it is hard to say what exactly the hospital is paying for — and this is where a [pay chimney](/en/analysis/hospital-directors-pay/) most easily arises. That is why the solution is not a ban on contracts but transparency: the total payments to each person, records of working time and public reference points for rates.

The typology was published by Klub Jagielloński, a Polish think tank, under the CC BY 4.0 licence.[^demkow]

At the median the difference is moderate — a contract pays about 8% more. It grows, however, towards the top of the scale, and at the very top it is several-fold. And it is precisely contract amounts that nobody publishes: salaried pay is visible to the zloty, while a contract is business revenue outside the payroll register.

A rare insight into full annual income comes from the asset declarations of doctors who are local councillors. Of the 182 declarations checked for 2024–2025, 165 show the doctor’s own practice and 11 only employment and contracts. For 2025, the median income from the practice alone was about <Money value={500000} /> (104 declarations), and the median total income from work — about <Money value={633000} /> (113). We have compiled the distribution and links to the declarations in our analysis [Doctors who are councillors](/en/analysis/doctor-councillors-public-income/).

<Aside title="The paradox of the pay act">

Every year the state raises salaried pay by act: from July 2026 the minimum for a specialist is <Money value={12910} />, and the whole pay ladder costs the NFZ about <Money value={72000000000} scale="billion" decimals={0} /> this year — according to an estimate by the deputy president of the NFZ. Meanwhile, about 73% of specialists work outside it. The act sets a floor from which contract rates bounce, but it does not cover the contracts themselves and does not disclose their amounts. What is regulated most precisely is what can be seen anyway. We show the cost of the act in [the NFZ budget](/en/data/nfz-budget/).

</Aside>
