During the pandemic the state spent a large part of the public money for tackling its effects outside the regular budget. This text is not about vaccines or restrictions. We describe only public money: how much was spent, which way it flowed and whether anyone accounted for it.

The figures come mainly from audits by the Supreme Audit Office (NIK), the National Health Account of Statistics Poland (GUS) and announcements by the National Health Fund (NFZ). Where we rely on press reports, we say so in the sentence.

## A fund outside the budget: almost PLN 190 billion

The main channel for the money was the COVID-19 Response Fund (Fundusz Przeciwdziałania COVID-19) at Bank Gospodarstwa Krajowego (BGK), the state development bank. It was set up on 1 April 2020 as a state special-purpose fund, but 18 days later its form was changed and it was excluded from the public finance sector. From then on, the Fund’s spending was not covered by the provisions of the Public Finance Act or by the Sejm’s scrutiny provided for the budget act. The money came mainly from BGK bonds and payments from the state budget. The bonds do not increase the state’s public debt, but their redemption falls in 2024–2040.[^nik-fundusz]

NIK gives two totals, and both are correct. Total spending in 2020–2022 amounted to <Money value={FUND.total * 1e6} scale="billion" />. Tasks alone took <Money value={FUND.tasks * 1e6} scale="billion" />: <Money value={FUND.tasksByYear[0].value * 1e6} scale="billion" /> in 2020, <Money value={FUND.tasksByYear[1].value * 1e6} scale="billion" /> in 2021 and <Money value={FUND.tasksByYear[2].value * 1e6} scale="billion" /> in 2022. The difference, <Money value={(FUND.total - FUND.tasks) * 1e6} scale="billion" decimals={2} />, is bond servicing and the BGK commission.[^nik-fundusz] We calculate the shares of the spending authorities from the amount for tasks.

The remaining {fundFmt.nonHealthShare}% was spent by other spending authorities, including the minister responsible for labour, the Social Insurance Institution (ZUS) and the Chancellery of the Prime Minister. The Fund also financed local government roads, office buildings and the purchase of refuse trucks, and from the second half of 2022 the coal and electricity allowances and compensation for energy and gas sellers. NIK found that with such a general purpose the Fund became a tool for financing practically any task, at the prime minister’s disposal. It summed up the whole as chaos, a lack of transparent and clear procedures and inefficient management of public funds.[^nik-fundusz]

## What part of it was healthcare

Of the <Money value={FUND.health * 1e6} scale="billion" /> allocated to the Minister of Health, most went to the NFZ. By mid-2022 the minister had spent <Money value={52.2e9} scale="billion" /> from the Fund, of which <Money value={40.8e9} scale="billion" /> (78.1%) on NFZ tasks and on refunding health contributions for the payers who had been exempted from them.[^nik-fundusz-pdf] In 2021 alone the NFZ spent <Money value={23.7e9} scale="billion" /> from the Fund, including <Money value={13e9} scale="billion" decimals={0} /> on COVID-19-related services together with medical transport and <Money value={2.5e9} scale="billion" /> on RT-PCR tests.[^prawo-nfz]

This money shows up in the statistics. Under the method laid down in the Act on publicly funded healthcare services (Article 131c), public spending on health in 2021 was planned at <Money value={120.5e9} scale="billion" /> and amounted to <Money value={141.3e9} scale="billion" />, including <Money value={23.7e9} scale="billion" /> transferred to the NFZ from the Fund. That is about PLN 25 billion (21.5%) more than in 2020.[^gus-2019-2021] So only the non-health part of the Fund remains outside the health account.

The National Health Account of Statistics Poland counts all current spending on health, public and private. The pandemic did not produce a one-off jump in it. Growth accelerated year after year: from 2.7% in 2020 to 24.3% in 2023.[^gus-2019-2021][^gus-2021][^gus-2021-2023][^gus-2023]

### Readiness and temporary hospitals

From March 2020 to April 2022, keeping all the adapted hospitals ready to treat COVID-19 patients cost about <Money value={7e9} scale="billion" decimals={0} />, and treating infected patients just under <Money value={5e9} scale="billion" decimals={0} />. Neither amount includes COVID-19 bonuses. NIK found that the reserve of beds and staff was larger than needed, because action was not coordinated nationally.[^nik-szpitale]

Setting up, running and closing 33 temporary hospitals cost <Money value={941.6e6} scale="million" />. More than <Money value={612.6e6} scale="million" />, i.e. two thirds, was spent on 14 facilities in halls, stadiums and exhibition centres. According to NIK, the reserve of COVID-19 beds would have been safe even without them. The hospital in the Netto Arena hall in Szczecin cost more than <Money value={29e6} scale="million" decimals={0} /> and did not admit a single patient.[^nik-szpitale]

The best known was the National Hospital at the stadium in Warsaw. An answer to a parliamentary inspection shows that running the hospital from November 2020 to April 2021 cost <Money value={NATIONAL_HOSPITAL.operation} scale="million" decimals={2} />, and according to the Chancellery of the Prime Minister adapting the stadium cost another <Money value={NATIONAL_HOSPITAL.adaptation} scale="million" decimals={2} />. Together that is <Money value={NATIONAL_HOSPITAL.operation + NATIONAL_HOSPITAL.adaptation} scale="million" />, i.e. on average <Money value={nhPerMonth} scale="million" /> a month without the adaptation or <Money value={nhPerMonthAll} scale="million" /> with it. In that time the hospital admitted {nhPatients} patients.[^termedia-sn] For the period from June to November 2021, when the hospital was not admitting patients, rent and other charges cost more than a further <Money value={11e6} scale="million" decimals={0} />.[^nik-szpitale]

## Purchases without tenders

The special COVID-19 act made urgent purchases of equipment easier by bypassing public procurement. According to NIK, it also removed the mechanisms that ensure public money is spent properly.[^nik-covid] The most widely reported were the purchases of ventilators and masks.

### Ventilators

On 14 April 2020 the Ministry of Health concluded a framework agreement with E&K, a company from Lublin, for the supply of ventilators worth up to <Money value={250e6} scale="million" decimals={0} /> gross. Under the order the company was to deliver 1,241 devices for €44.4 million. On the same day the ministry transferred an advance payment of <Money value={161.3e6} scale="million" /> to it. 200 ventilators arrived, and 1,041 were not delivered. Those delivered had gaps in their documentation and equipment that had to be made good before they went to hospitals.[^nik-p20062]

At the end of 2022 the ministry’s receivables from the company amounted, according to NIK, to <Money value={85.3e6} scale="million" />: <Money value={63.6e6} scale="million" /> of principal and <Money value={21.7e6} scale="million" /> of contractual penalties, interest and enforcement costs.[^nik-p23001] According to Ministry of Health information reported by Rzeczpospolita (article updated on 16.10.2026), the ministry recovered <Money value={38.19e6} scale="million" decimals={2} /> of the principal, and €4.6 million with interest remains to be repaid. Enforcement against the company proved ineffective, so the General Counsel to the Republic of Poland (Prokuratoria Generalna) is seeking the money from the estate of the owner, who died in 2022.[^rp-resp] In July 2026 the Regional Court in Warsaw awarded more than €3.6 million in contractual penalties against the company. A representative of the General Counsel’s office then assessed the chances of recovering this money as close to zero.[^polsat-2026]

Status of proceedings: the investigation into failure to perform duties and abuse of power by public officials in connection with this agreement was discontinued, and the Regional Prosecutor’s Office in Warsaw reopened it on 16 May 2024.[^tvn-wznowienie] The sources do not mention charges; we do not know the status of the investigation after May 2024.

This is not the only ventilator case. NIK calculated that the Chancellery of the Prime Minister and the Ministry of Health spent <Money value={82.3e6} scale="million" /> uneconomically on devices that were incomplete, faulty or did not meet Polish standards, and filed three notifications with the prosecutor’s office.[^nik-covid] One concerns the then deputy minister of health, who in April 2020 decided to buy 30 ventilators from a State Treasury company for more than <Money value={3.6e6} scale="million" /> without market research and without a written contract, and the devices were not fit for treating patients.[^nik-covid] The notification was reported in April 2022.[^polsat-2022] The sources mention no charges, and we have not found any further decisions by the prosecutor’s office.

Masks are usually talked about as a single scandal, but these were two separate purchases.

The ministry’s purchase: on 30 March 2020 the ministry bought, through an intermediary, 100,000 FFP2 masks at <Money value={39} /> net each, 20,000 surgical masks at <Money value={8} /> each, 3,000 face shields and 10,000 FFP2 masks from another manufacturer. According to the then deputy minister of health, the whole cost <Money value={4859640} /> gross.[^money-maski] In February 2024 the prosecutor’s office discontinued the investigation, finding that the act did not constitute an offence.[^wp-maski] At the end of June 2025 the Regional Court in Warsaw dismissed the ministry’s appeal. According to an account by Polityka Zdrowotna, a health policy news site, the court indicated that the State Treasury may pursue civil claims, and that later laboratory tests had confirmed that the masks complied with standards.[^pz-maski]

The Chancellery’s purchase: according to NIK, the Chancellery of the Prime Minister spent almost <Money value={200e6} scale="million" decimals={0} /> on protective equipment in 2020–2021 in a way that was uneconomical, unreliable and inexpedient. The Central Institute for Labour Protection rejected 9.4 million of the 22.6 million medical masks tested (41.7%), 132,700 of the 154,300 face shields (86%) and 299,000 of the 641,800 protective suits (46.6%). Because of doubts about quality, the Chancellery did not pay 48% of the invoices, worth <Money value={27.8e6} scale="million" />, which in practice was a loss for Lotos, to which the Chancellery was to reimburse its expenses.[^nik-kprm] Following NIK’s notification, the Regional Prosecutor’s Office in Warsaw opened an investigation on 12 June 2024 into abuse of power by public officials, including the then prime minister. The prosecutor’s office indicated damage of great magnitude to Grupa Lotos.[^mp-sledztwo] The sources mention no charges, and we do not know the status of the investigation after June 2024.

### Disinfectant: a stock that expired

A similar question of sound management concerns reserves. In 2021 the Government Strategic Reserves Agency (RARS) ordered, under two contracts of about <Money value={1.2e6} scale="million" /> each, almost 2.5 million litres of disinfectant in total. Of the first batch, 86.5% reached recipients. Of the second, 53% was not made available to anyone: the agency disposed of almost 668,000 litres of expired fluid worth <Money value={646400} /> at a cost of <Money value={2.5e6} scale="million" />, almost four times more than the fluid was worth.[^nik-rars] Across both batches this is about 27% of the fluid (our calculation from NIK data). In total, disposing of denatured alcohol cost RARS <Money value={8.2e6} scale="million" /> in 2021–2024.[^nik-rars]

## Pay in the pandemic: a lot of money, little insight

Closest to the subject of this site is the COVID-19 bonus for staff who treated COVID-19 patients. From 1 November 2020 it amounted to 100% of pay, up to <Money value={15000} /> a month.[^nfz-dodatek] From May 2020 to March 2022, <Money value={8.98e9} scale="billion" decimals={2} /> was paid out on this basis.[^nik-dodatki]

NIK found that neither the Minister of Health nor the President of the NFZ checked whether the bonuses were granted and paid correctly. According to NIK, there is a risk that it will be impossible to estimate how much was spent without justification, and that some of the Fund’s assets will have to be written off as lost. The audit showed, among other things:[^nik-dodatki]

- **A limit counted in each hospital separately.** The minister’s rules allowed the same person to be paid the bonus in several facilities, so the PLN 15,000 limit did not work. The record was <Money value={41500} /> in a single month.
- **The full bonus for incidental contact.** In one of the hospitals in Szczecin, 18 people received the bonus for 30 minutes of contact with patients in a month: three received PLN 15,000 each, and 15 between PLN 3,300 and 8,300.
- **More people entitled than beds.** After corrections to the reports in the audited hospitals, there were as many as 31 people receiving the bonus per COVID-19 bed, and 23 per patient.
- **Excessive transfers.** NFZ branches transferred to 15 hospitals a total of <Money value={13.8e6} scale="million" /> more than they paid out to staff.

Wirtualna Polska, a news portal, describing the preliminary findings of the audit, gave the example of a doctor who worked in three facilities and received the bonus in each — almost <Money value={38000} /> a month in total.[^wp-dodatki] Rates for the work itself were also higher. The director of the National Institute of Public Health NIH – National Research Institute (NIZP PZH), a former NFZ vice-president, said in an interview that at the National Hospital one could get PLN 8,000–10,000 for a single on-call shift. This is the director’s assessment, not an amount from documents.[^rp-dyzury]

### A vaccination point

Fee-for-service payment shows a different problem. According to press reports from June 2026, a doctor who ran a mass vaccination point in a one-person practice had more than <Money value={4.3e6} scale="million" /> of income from medical activity in 2021, and <Money value={1.6e6} scale="million" /> in 2022. According to NFZ data, the practice reported more than 56,000 vaccinations in 2021, on average more than 150 a day. According to the doctor, the vaccinations were combined with on-call duty in the ambulance service, the taxes were paid and the practice passed NFZ and tax office inspections.[^wp-punkt]

The NFZ paid the vaccination point <Money value={61.24} /> per vaccination without transporting the patient.[^nfz-stawka] If all 56,000 vaccinations had been billed at this rate, that would come to about <Money value={3.43e6} scale="million" decimals={2} />. This is our estimate: income from the practice is not the same as the amount paid by the NFZ, and we know only the figures from the press. We do not claim that the doctor broke the law, and we do not give the doctor’s name.

## What this means for transparency

The same pattern recurs in all these cases. The money came from many places at once: from the Fund outside the budget, through the NFZ, temporary hospitals and State Treasury companies. Each of these places knew only its own piece. Payments above the bonus limit for one person came to light only in an NIK audit, not in current accounting. We know the amount from the vaccination point from the press, not from a public account.

This is the same gap that we describe outside the pandemic: nobody publishes the total amount of public money paid to one person — see [Lack of transparency](/en/mechanisms/lack-of-transparency/). That is why we call on the state to publish the total paid to each person from public money under a permanent identifier, without names. Such a dataset would show totals from many facilities straight away, not years later in an audit report. Details: [Our proposal](/en/demand/).
