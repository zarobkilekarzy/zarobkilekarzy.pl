There is a new tool on the site: [What doctors earn around the world](/en/data/doctors-pay-worldwide/). On a globe and in the table next to it we compare doctors’ pay in 43 countries, using OECD data and national statistical offices. Pay can be shown as a multiple of the average wage in the country, as a multiple of a nurse’s pay, in US dollars, euro or zloty, and in purchasing power. A separate layer shows whether the pay of an individual doctor paid from public money can be known.

## What the globe shows

- **A specialist on an employment contract in Poland earns 2.86 times as much as the average employee** (OECD, 2024). That is 16th of the 42 countries with this measure; Hungary is highest (4.41 times).
- **These data do not cover civil-law (B2B) contracts.** According to a working document of the Agency for Health Technology Assessment and Tariff System (AOTMiT), 73% of specialists work under civil-law contracts, and the statistics do not add up one person’s pay from several jobs.
- **Compared with a hospital nurse, a salaried specialist in Poland earns 1.72 times as much** (OECD, same year).
- **None of the 29 countries we studied publishes the total payments from public money to each doctor.** The closest are Italy (senior staff only, including heads of hospital departments) and British Columbia in Canada (payments from one plan).

## How to read these numbers

The comparison is very approximate: countries measure pay differently, and the data come from 2015–2026. For every country we give the source, the year and whom the number covers. The tool is also [in Polish](/dane/zarobki-lekarzy-na-swiecie/), with amounts in zloty.
