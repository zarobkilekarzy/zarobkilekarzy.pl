The act amending the Act on healthcare services financed from public funds, passed by the Sejm on 22 October 2026 on the basis of a government bill (print No. 3157), sets an upper limit on the pay of doctors, nurses and other health professionals in facilities that have a contract with the National Health Fund (NFZ).[^druk][^glosowanie] The limit has two parts: a maximum hourly rate and a maximum monthly amount. Both are calculated as a multiple of the minimum wage, so they rise along with it.

The act also changes the rules of employment: it limits total working time, requires at least a half-time post in a hospital, prohibits contracts concluded through intermediary companies and bans setting pay as a percentage of the contract. At the third reading the Sejm added a ban on providing NFZ-funded services for a doctor who has knowingly certified something untrue. Below we describe each change separately: in the wording adopted by the Health Committee, with the amendments adopted by the Sejm.[^sprawozdanie][^dodatkowe] The Sejm has not yet published the text of the act as passed.

## What stage the act has reached

The bill reached the Sejm on 28 September 2026. The Health Committee held the first reading on 7 October and recommended that it be passed. On 20 October, after the second reading, the bill went back to the committee with 29 amendments and 9 minority motions. The committee gave its opinion on them on 21 October.[^proces][^dodatkowe]

On 22 October the Sejm passed the act: **229 MPs voted for, 144 against and 67 abstained**.[^glosowanie] The act now goes to the Senate, which has 30 days to deal with it: it can adopt it without changes, amend it or reject it. The President then signs the act within 21 days, or refers it to the Constitutional Tribunal or returns it to the Sejm.[^konstytucja]

The current rules are set out in Articles 26 and 96 of the Act on medical activity, and the practice of “employee leasing” (<span lang="pl">leasing pracowniczy</span>) and percentage-based pay is described in the explanatory memorandum to the bill.[^udl][^druk]

## Whom the limits cover

The act refers to a “person practising a medical profession” (<span lang="pl">osoba wykonująca zawód medyczny</span>), so not only to doctors. This term from the Act on medical activity covers people entitled to provide healthcare services, including nurses, midwives, paramedics and physiotherapists.[^sprawozdanie]

The scope of the two main limits differs:

- **the working-time limit** counts employment with all providers, including work in one’s own practice outside a medical establishment (Article 132aa(1));
- **the pay limits** cover pay for services under an NFZ contract, paid by facilities that have such a contract (Article 132ab(1) and (7)).

A facility may therefore pay for privately funded work without these limits. The time spent on that work, however, counts towards the two full-time posts.

## Hourly rate: 1/20 of the minimum wage

The gross hourly rate of base pay for services under an NFZ contract may not exceed **1/20 of the minimum wage**. In 2026 the minimum wage is <Money value={4806} />, so the limit is **<Money value={240.3} /> an hour**.[^min2026] The provision applies to employment contracts and, through the cross-reference in paragraph 6, also to contractor agreements and other civil-law contracts.[^sprawozdanie] In the description of the version sent for consultation in July, the hourly rate applied to people working under contracts.[^rz-projekt]

The act links the limits to the minimum wage rather than to the average wage. The Sejm rejected amendments that would have calculated them from the average wage.[^dodatkowe] For comparison: the average wage in the economy in 2025 was <Money value={8903.56} />,[^przecietne] so a rate of PLN 240.30 is about 4.3 times the average hourly wage at 160 hours a month (PLN 55.65).

The limit applies to base pay. On top of it a facility may grant:

- a performance bonus;
- allowances due under statute, up to the amount set by that statute;
- a seniority allowance, in public facilities;
- additional pay for a managerial role, e.g. head of a hospital department, and for teaching and research duties;
- additional pay for organ retrieval and transplantation in clinical transplantology, for highly specialised services and for work in trauma centres.

The narrowing of transplantology to organ retrieval and transplantation was added by an amendment adopted at the third reading.[^dodatkowe][^alert] All these allowances, however, must fit within the monthly cap.

## Monthly cap: three thresholds

The second limit is the maximum average monthly pay. The average is calculated over a settlement period of no more than four months, so a month with more hours can be balanced by a lighter one.[^sprawozdanie]

The two limits are consistent with each other. A full-time post is about 160 hours a month, and 160 × PLN 240.30 gives exactly PLN 38,448, i.e. 8 times the minimum wage. Two full-time posts are 320 hours and PLN 76,896, i.e. 16 times. This is how the explanatory memorandum to the bill calculates the amount.[^druk]

The memorandum also placed transplantology, highly specialised services and trauma centres in the 12-times group,[^druk] but paragraph 8 in the committee’s version expressly listed only statutory allowances, seniority and managerial, teaching and research roles.[^sprawozdanie] An amendment adopted at the third reading added the pay under paragraph 5 to paragraph 8, so these three types of work also fall within the 12× threshold.[^dodatkowe]

Before taking up a job, the health professional submits a declaration of their total maximum pay across all facilities. Any change affecting this total must be reported within 7 days. The head of the facility then has 3 working days to propose a change to the contract that keeps the total within the limit. If the health professional does not agree within the following 3 working days, the head terminates the contract.[^sprawozdanie] The committee wanted this to happen without the notice period being observed. The Sejm deleted those words with the Lewica amendment, so the ordinary notice period applies.[^dodatkowe]

A shorthand circulating in the media reads “8 times the minimum wage, i.e. PLN 240 an hour” (our translation). These are two different limits: PLN 240.30 is the hourly rate (1/20 of the minimum wage), and 8 times is the monthly cap with one provider for a full-time post. The amount of PLN 76,800, sometimes given as the total cap, comes from rounding the rate to PLN 240. The act calculates from the minimum wage, which in 2026 gives PLN 76,896 (16×).[^druk]

## Working time: at most two full-time posts in total

The total working time of a health professional across all providers may not exceed twice the full-time working hours in a month. Full-time hours are 8 hours multiplied by the number of working days in the month, minus public holidays falling on weekdays. Under a civil-law contract, the full-time equivalent counts, i.e. the number of hours in the contract.[^sprawozdanie]

The limit does not include time spent on standby outside a medical establishment to provide services under an NFZ contract: standby “on the phone”, with travel in on call-out (Article 98 of the Act on medical activity), and standby at the patient’s place of residence or stay. The second case and the narrowing to services under an NFZ contract were added by an amendment adopted at the third reading.[^dodatkowe]

Before taking up a job, the health professional submits to the head of the facility a declaration of their total working time in all workplaces. Any change in working time with another employer must be reported within 7 days.[^sprawozdanie]

## Hospitals and clinics: at least a half-time post and the first employer’s consent

In hospital treatment and in hospital-based specialist outpatient clinics (AOS), under an NFZ contract, a doctor, nurse, midwife, physiotherapist and paramedic must have at least a half-time post or its equivalent under a civil-law contract.[^sprawozdanie][^dodatkowe] The explanatory memorandum gives the aim: frequent rotation of people on part-time posts makes it harder to manage the treatment of a single patient and increases the risk of errors.[^druk]

Each further such job requires the consent of the heads of all the hospitals in which the health professional already works. The procedure has four steps:[^sprawozdanie][^dodatkowe]

The half-time requirement does not cover:

- on-call duty and shift work outside normal working hours, except for planned procedures;
- services provided by geriatricians, pathologists and psychiatrists — this exception was added by an amendment adopted at the third reading;[^dodatkowe]
- clinics in which shorter working time results from the schedule of the NFZ contract;
- a person who works in only one such hospital and submits a declaration to that effect.

The Sejm rejected the PSL amendment, which would have deleted the entire requirement of a half-time post and the employer’s consent, by 179 votes to 203.[^glosowanie]

Where a field is short of staff or on-call duty needs to be covered, the president of the NFZ may, at the facility’s request, agree to a derogation from the limit of two full-time posts and from the half-time requirement, for up to 6 months. No reply within 14 days means consent. In the event of a sudden death, a long absence of an employee or force majeure, the head of the facility may waive these requirements on their own, for 21 days.[^sprawozdanie]

## Contracts with individuals, not companies

The act amends Article 26 of the Act on medical activity, which allows a hospital to commission healthcare services from outside. Today any entity performing medical activity, including a company, can take on such a commission.[^udl] After the change, a commission for work in a hospital may be given only to an individual with a sole professional practice. A hospital will commission services from another medical entity only if that entity provides them in its own establishment or at the place of call-out.[^sprawozdanie]

The explanatory memorandum calls the current practice “employee leasing”. A hospital commissions services from a company that is formally a medical entity but in practice only supplies staff. When a company is the party to the contract, working-time restrictions do not apply to it, and it is difficult to establish the total working time of a specific person.[^druk]

A contract for services must last at least 3 months, unless the type and number of services justify a shorter one. It must specify the total number of hours, the hourly rate and the maximum monthly working time and pay. A facility financed from public funds awards the contract through a competitive tendering procedure.[^sprawozdanie]

## Pay not as a percentage of the contract

The pay of a health professional for services under an NFZ contract may not be set as a percentage of the amount the facility receives under that contract (Article 132ab(12)).[^sprawozdanie] According to the explanatory memorandum, with such an arrangement every increase in the NFZ’s pricing of services automatically raises pay. The government sees this as one of the sources of a “wage spiral” and of hospitals’ debt.[^druk] We describe how this mechanism shows up in disclosed payments in our text on [record payments](/en/data/record-doctor-payments/).

## Inspections and penalties

The health professional submits the declarations on working time and pay under criminal liability for making a false statement (Article 233 § 6 of the Criminal Code). The facility keeps them for 10 years.[^sprawozdanie]

A head of a facility who breaches the requirements on the limits commits a petty offence punishable by a fine of up to <Money value={30000} /> (new Article 193). The act also extends the NFZ’s inspection powers:[^sprawozdanie]

- facilities with an NFZ contract must provide information, including on services outside the contract, and staff records: contracts, qualifications and working-time records;
- the NFZ may also request such records from facilities without an NFZ contract;
- the NFZ may inspect again a matter it has already examined if new facts come to light or the previous inspection breached the law;
- explanations given to NFZ inspectors are made under criminal liability for making a false statement.

According to the regulatory impact assessment, the NFZ will hire about 400 additional staff for inspections, including 352 in the inspection department at its head office. In 2027–2036 this will cost a total of <Money value={545240000} scale="million" decimals={2} />.[^druk]

## Deadlines and adjustment of contracts

The act is to enter into force one month after its promulgation. Existing contracts must be brought into line with the new requirements within 6 months of that date.[^sprawozdanie] The consultation version allowed until 31 December 2026 for this.[^rz-projekt] The government replaced that deadline with a six-month transitional period.[^rz-rzad] The provisions on the ban on providing NFZ-funded services will enter into force later, 6 months after promulgation.[^sprawozdanie]

If a contract cannot be adjusted, the head of the facility terminates it: an employment contract with a notice period no longer than under the Labour Code, a civil-law contract with at most one month’s notice.[^sprawozdanie] The committee proposed that the provisions protecting employees — including consultation with a trade union and pre-retirement protection (Articles 38, 39 and 41 of the Labour Code) — should not apply to these terminations. The Sejm deleted this provision with the KO and Lewica amendment, so the protection applies.[^dodatkowe]

Collective agreements and pay regulations must be adjusted within 6 months. In the committee’s version, those not adjusted would lose force in full. After the amendment adopted by the Sejm, they are invalid only to the extent that they conflict with the limits, and labour law applies to that extent.[^dodatkowe]

A person who works in several hospitals designates one of them, when contracts are adjusted, as their “primary provider” (<span lang="pl">świadczeniodawca podstawowy</span>). Its head decides on consent to the other jobs.[^sprawozdanie]

## Ban on providing NFZ-funded services and the register of bans

The committee rejected two groups of amendments tabled by an MP of Rozwój Plus, so the Sejm voted on them as minority motions.[^sprawozdanie][^rz-zakaz] It adopted the first by 416 votes to 20 and rejected the second.[^glosowanie]

The adopted group introduces a **ban on providing services financed from public funds**. The president of the NFZ imposes it by decision on a doctor or dentist if an NFZ inspection or information from the Social Insurance Institution (ZUS) shows that they knowingly:[^sprawozdanie]

- certified something untrue about providing a service, e.g. about performing a procedure, in medical records or in a document on the basis of which the facility settles accounts with the NFZ;
- certified something untrue about the time or place of work, e.g. in working-time records, a duty roster or an invoice;
- issued a sick note inconsistent with the facts;
- issued another certificate or medical opinion inconsistent with the facts so that the patient or someone else would gain a benefit.

The ban lasts from one month to 8 years. The president of the NFZ takes into account the seriousness and number of the breaches, how long they lasted, the amount of money unduly spent and the consequences for patients. A person who breaches the rules again, during the ban or within 10 years of its end, receives a lifetime ban. A ban is not imposed for an obvious error or for a mere difference in medical judgement, and no proceedings are opened 5 years after the breach. The ban also applies by operation of law to a doctor whose authorisation to issue sick notes ZUS has withdrawn for a knowingly false certificate.[^sprawozdanie]

The decision takes effect on delivery. The doctor may appeal against it to the labour and social insurance court, which has 30 days to hear the case. An appeal does not suspend the ban, but the court may suspend it on request. The ban does not apply to help given in a sudden threat to life or health when no other doctor is available.[^sprawozdanie]

The president of the NFZ keeps a **public register of bans** in the Public Information Bulletin (BIP), with an interface for automated checks. The register gives the first name and surname, the licence to practise (PWZ) number, the legal basis and duration of the ban, and information on any appeal. The data also go to the Central Register of Doctors kept by the Supreme Medical Council. After the ban ends, the entry disappears from the public part.[^sprawozdanie]

The ban applies only to work financed from public funds, so a doctor subject to a ban can continue to treat patients privately. The provisions apply to breaches committed after they enter into force, 6 months after the act is promulgated.[^sprawozdanie]

The second, rejected group would have moved the working-time declarations to the P1 IT system. In it the health professional would have listed all the facilities where they work, and the heads of those facilities would have seen their declarations.[^sprawozdanie] PiS, Rozwój Plus and Razem voted for; KO, PSL, Lewica, Centrum and Polska 2050 voted against.[^glosowanie]

<Aside title="What the act does not provide for">

The act contains no provision on publishing pay or the total amounts paid to one person.[^sprawozdanie] Nor does it create a shared register of declarations — the Sejm rejected the motion to move them to the P1 system. Each facility keeps the declarations of its own staff, and only the person who declares it knows the total from several workplaces.

The only public register, with first names and surnames, will be the register of doctors banned from NFZ-funded work. The act does not change the rules on the transparency of the amounts that facilities pay health professionals from NFZ money.

Since August 2026 the minister of health may collect data on each person’s pay, labelled with the PESEL number and the licence to practise number, under a different act. That act does not provide for their publication either. We describe it in our text on [the pay data act](/en/analysis/pay-data-act-vs-public-register/).
